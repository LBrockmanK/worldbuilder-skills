## Character Arc Summary

Adeline's arc is about learning to separate self-worth from
productivity. At the start she is a competent, cheerful
administrator whose entire identity is service to Mistria. The
player's relationship gradually reveals that this competence masks
an inability to rest, take time for herself, or feel deserving of
care. The arc resolves when she recognizes that connection — not
usefulness — is what matters.

The arc operates on two timelines simultaneously:
1. **Town recovery** (story events): Mistria rebuilds through her
   leadership. This succeeds — her competence is real and effective.
2. **Personal vulnerability** (heart events): she learns to let
   someone in despite defining herself through what she does, not
   who she is.

The town arc validates her work; the personal arc challenges whether
work is enough.

| Stage | External behavior | Internal state | Key tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early (0-3h) | Cheerful, organized, encouraging | Stable identity through work | None visible |

## Heart Event Progression — Behavioral Analysis

### 2 Hearts — Establishing identity through work

The paperwork party introduces Adeline's core identity: she genuinely
enjoys administrative work. This is not a flaw yet — it's charming
and effective. The family dynamic positions her as the competent
organizer (Eiland is reluctant, Elsie is savvy, Adeline is enthusiastic).

**Arc function:** Baseline. This is who she is before pressure.

### 4 Hearts — Competence producing results

The earthquake relief grants show her administrative work paying off
for the community. She presents options diplomatically, manages
stakeholder input (Eiland's cookie idea), and defers to the player's
judgment. The portrait shifts (concerned → sigh before the cookie
option) show she has opinions but presents herself as neutral.

**Arc function:** Peak competence. Her work identity is validated
externally. The flaw is invisible.

### 6 Hearts — First cracks

Landen explicitly tells her to take care of herself. She deflects with
"I'm okay!" but the game marks this with a `concerned` portrait and
`drop` effect — the visual language of something hitting harder than
she admits. She admits enjoying the player's company but frames it
through obligation and offers an exit. The walk ends with her instinct
to return to desk work.

**Arc function:** Foreshadowing. Others see the problem before she
does. The player's presence creates a new pull competing with work.

### 8 Hearts — Crisis and confession

She works to the point of physical collapse. The tired portrait
variants throughout the scene create sustained tension before the
faint. After recovery, two things happen:

1. She shares her first childhood memory (mother's picnics, lemons,
   baking with Eiland) — the first time she talks about who she was
   before she was Town Leader.
2. She confesses she broke the player's welcome vase on arrival night
   because she was flustered by the player's appearance — "I wasn't
   expecting you to be so cute."

The event branches into best friend or romantic partner. Both paths
involve her asking if the player will stay, and giving the Hero's Ring
(a town heirloom she received as leader, now passed to the player as
protector).

**Arc function:** The body enforces what the mind resists. The
childhood memory connects her current overwork to a pre-existing
identity. The confession reveals feelings she has suppressed since
the player arrived. The Hero's Ring is the first time she gives away
a piece of her leader identity.

### 10 Hearts — Resolution

At the Summit (her mother's picnic spot), Adeline explicitly names her
core pattern: "I used to think I couldn't live with myself unless I
could meet others' expectations of me." She states the resolution:
"I don't feel that way anymore." She gives the player a handcrafted
Lifetime Planner — her administrative nature expressed as emotional
commitment ("I cut out and colored all the little tabs!").

She tries to propose but can't find the words — the player proposes
instead. She was going to ask the same thing.

**Arc function:** Explicit resolution. She identifies the flaw, names
its source (expectations, not her own joy), and chooses differently.
The Lifetime Planner is administrative love language — she hasn't
stopped being organized, she's redirected it from obligation to
connection.

## Story Event Arc — Parallel Track

While the heart events track personal growth, the story events track
Mistria's recovery. Adeline coordinates every infrastructure project:

**Early game:** Bridge repair, Inn renovation, General Store upgrade
**Mid game:** Mill restoration, Summit stairs, Beach bridge, Barn expansion
**Late game:** Carpenter shop, Saturday Market (negotiations with Aldarian Merchant's Guild), Market Plaza, Stone Refinery, Food Reserves

Her role is always the same: identify need → present plan → coordinate
townspeople → assign tasks to player → celebrate completion. The
escalating scale of projects (from basic bridge repair to
international guild negotiations) mirrors her growing confidence.

**The parallel tracks reinforce each other:** as the town recovers,
her work identity is increasingly validated — which makes the personal
arc's challenge harder. She has to learn vulnerability despite being
proven right that hard work pays off.

## NPC Follow-up Patterns

Heart events generate follow-up reactions from other NPCs, revealing
how the community perceives Adeline:

**After 4-heart (relief choice):**
- Landen thanks the player for helping Adeline with supplies
- Hayden and Henrietta appreciate the materials
- Eiland is embarrassed but pleased about cookies (if chosen)
- Dell is delighted about "the BIGGEST cookie"

**After 8-heart (collapse):**
- Eiland: "I heard that Adeline collapsed... Thank goodness you were there"
- Elsie (romantic path): calls it romantic that the player carried her to her room
- Elsie (friend path): "What a good friend"

These follow-ups show that Adeline's inner life is visible to her
community — they notice when she's overworking, they're relieved when
someone takes care of her.

## Post-Resolution Behavior

After the 10-heart resolution, Adeline's scheduled behaviors and
ambient dialogue suggest she hasn't fully abandoned her patterns —
she still schedules relaxation, still works late, still gets excited
about paperwork. The resolution is not a personality transplant but a
new capacity: she can now choose rest without guilt, enjoy connection
without framing it through work, and ask for what she wants (most of
the time — she still stutters).

**Wedding vows** confirm this: she frames love through Mistria ("this
land that we would protect and lead together") — she hasn't stopped
being a leader, she's stopped being only a leader.

## Unanswerable from Game Data Alone

These questions would need human interpretation for a character card:

1. **Physical appearance detail:** Portraits provide visual reference
   but no text descriptions of posture, movement quality, physical
   mannerisms beyond expressions. How does she carry herself? How
   does she move?

2. **Core fear specifics:** The game implies fear of uselessness/
   letting people down, but never states it explicitly. A Q&A
   workflow should propose "fear of being unnecessary" from
   behavioral evidence and ask for confirmation.

3. **False belief articulation:** Candidate: "If I stop working,
   everything falls apart." Well-supported but interpretive. The
   10-heart resolution suggests she recognizes this as false.

4. **Knowledge boundaries for worldbuilding:** She's educated
   (noble, grant writer, tax law) but the game doesn't map her
   knowledge against the world's systems (magic, dragons,
   dungeon ecology). The main knowledge dividers in the game appear
   to be magic knowledge and dragon knowledge — Adeline has no
   demonstrated expertise in either.

5. **Voice register specifics:** Dialogue suggests formal-but-warm
   with stuttering when emotional, all-caps shock, and deflection
   to work topics. But explicit style rules (sentence length,
   vocabulary tier, metaphor habits) need human judgment to codify.
