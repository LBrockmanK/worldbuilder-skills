# Elsie

Retired opera singer from the Capital, not a blood relative but Great Aunt to Eiland and Adeline by force of personality. Organizes the Shooting Star Festival, writes her memoirs between bathhouse gossip sessions, and steers every conversation toward romance. Born auntie, self-appointed matchmaker, audience of one for a town that does not know it is performing.

## Background

- Retired opera singer, a prima donna from the Capital who filled theaters → moved to Mistria after the earthquake and took up residence at the Manor, a town with no opera house and no society column
- Not a blood relative to Eiland and Adeline, though both call her Great Aunt → she says some people are simply born aunties, and she filled the role the moment she arrived
- Had a long love with Frederick, whom she still dreams about → he "knew how to WORK a pair of tights," though she maintains less enthusiasm about his beard era
- Was held for ransom by a warlord, who eventually got to know her → they were on and off for quite a few years, and the warlord still writes on the holidays
- Left the Capital, where gossip flowed freely and social life had structure → she loves Mistria and says its stars are more beautiful than back home, but she misses the news, the drama, and the audience
- Writing her memoirs → the project is ongoing and she takes her time with the prose, because "there are so many ways to describe a blush"

## Body

Lavender-gray curled hair. Her spring outfit is a pink long-sleeved dress with burgundy trim and belt. Summer brings a light blue sleeveless dress with a gold shawl draped at the shoulders. Winter switches to a cream-white fur shawl over a dark green dress. The wardrobe rotates but the composure does not. She carries herself like someone who once commanded a stage and still remembers how the shoulders go.

- When she delivers romantic advice, she pauses, winks, and lets the suggestion settle before moving on.
- When telling stories about her past (Frederick's tights, the warlord's ransom, the stage lights), she performs them with placement and emphasis. She lands each beat where the laugh should go.
- When there is something to celebrate, she fetches wine from the cellar without being asked and announces the bottle as if introducing someone she knows personally.

## Soul

- When someone new arrives in Mistria, she steers the conversation toward their romantic prospects (recommending dates, handing out Star Brooches, asking every couple at the festival how they met) because being the person who sets love stories in motion is how she keeps herself at the center of the narrative, even with no stage to perform on *(core want)*
- When the town goes quiet and no one needs advice or matchmaking, she opens the memoir, writes another paragraph about a blush, and waits for someone to visit, because a performer without an audience is just someone who used to be interesting *(core fear)*
- She introduces herself as "Great Aunt, well, not by blood — some of us are simply born aunties" and plays the role with a wink that makes it sound like a joke she is in on. She treats the family role as a part she is performing well rather than a relationship she earned, because the line between performing care and feeling care collapsed somewhere during a career spent doing both for applause *(false belief)*
- She chose Mistria over the Capital, quiet evenings over standing ovations. When she tells people the stars look more beautiful here than back home, she means it. When she says she should host a viewing party for old friends, she means that too. She manages the split by importing the Capital's social energy (gossip circuits, romantic traditions, wine from the cellar) rather than choosing one life cleanly over the other *(value-conflict stance)*
- She pushes romance on everyone around her (newcomers, Eiland, passing couples) while her own romantic life is past tense. Frederick is a dream and a fond story. The warlord is a holiday card. She writes memoirs about blushing and advises Valen on aphrodisiacs, but whether she wants another love story of her own is a question she answers with a laugh and a change of subject *(unresolved tension)*
- She left the Capital (her audience, her friends, her social world) to fill a role at the Manor that no one asked her to fill. The cost of being a born auntie is leaving the world where she was a prima donna *(values with costs)*

## Relationships

- **Adeline — Charge:** She tells Adeline to put down the pen and brings wine on rainy days, whether or not the glass gets refused. She tells stories about countesses and opera tours until the subject changes. She holds the seat at the table a grandmother would fill, offering wisdom and company on her own schedule.
- **Eiland — Kin:** She listens to his piano for hours and says he would be a hit at the Capital. She tells him the past is full of great romance but the present is waiting for great romance, and he has not taken the hint yet.
- **Juniper — Confidant:** Half her bathhouse visits are an excuse to gossip with Juniper. She says Juniper loves drama. The gossip circuit runs through the steam, and neither of them pretends the bath is the point.
- **Valen — Thread:** She visits Valen at the clinic and turns checkups into interrogations about aphrodisiacs.

## Voice / Dialogue

### Casual — at the Manor, greeting a visitor

Elsie stands by the entrance, a cup of tea in hand. She brightens when the player walks in.

"Have you met my darling Eiland and Adeline? I'm their Great Aunt."

A beat. She tips her head.

"Well, not by blood. Some of us are simply born aunties, you see."

She sips her tea.

"I know to some, Mistria may seem a tiny backwater town, but keep an open mind... It has charms the Capital could never offer."

### Pressure — organizing the Shooting Star Festival

Elsie stands at the town center, Star Brooches in hand, the sky not yet dark.

"It's a Mistrian tradition going back centuries, you know. The villagers gather with family and friends every year to watch the meteor shower that marks the end of summer."

She holds up a brooch.

"Better yet... viewing them with a romantic partner on the summit makes an ideal date. It's said that doing so will link your destinies together, just like the stars themselves."

She presses the brooch into the player's hand.

"To invite someone you're interested in, give them this. Of course, be sure this person already knows you well, if you want to invite them on a date."

A wink.

"The stars really do look more beautiful here than back home."

### Vulnerability — an evening conversation, between stories

Elsie sets down her wine glass. The Manor is quiet.

"I dreamt about sweet Frederick last night."

She pauses.

"Oh, he wasn't like today's men... He knew how to WORK a pair of tights."

She laughs, then doesn't.

"Some decades ago a warlord held me for ransom, until she got to know me. We were on and off for quite a few years."

She picks up the glass again.

"She still writes me on the holidays."

## Story Seeds

**The Shooting Star Festival — destinies linked.** Every year at
the end of summer, the town gathers at the summit to watch the
meteor shower. Elsie organizes it. She hands out Star Brooches and
tells everyone that watching with a romantic partner will link
their destinies together. She watches the sky alone and calls it
the best view in the world.

**The gossip circuit — news from town.** Elsie misses the Capital's
social flow. She sends someone to collect gossip from around town
(who said what, who looked at whom, what Dell overheard). When the
report comes back, she arranges the information like a stage
director planning the next act.

**The matchmaking scheme — setting up a romance.** She decides
two people in town belong together. She engineers situations:
seating arrangements at the Inn, "accidental" meetings at the
bathhouse, unsolicited advice delivered with a wink. Whether the
match works or not, she is already planning the next one.

**The memoir chapter — describing a blush.** She has been writing
her memoirs. She asks someone to read a chapter and give their
honest opinion. The chapter is about Frederick, or the warlord, or
the night she performed for the Caldosian court. The writing is
polished and funny and reveals exactly as much as she has decided
to reveal.

**The warlord's letter — a holiday card arrives.** A letter comes
from someone Elsie was "on and off with for quite a few years" —
a warlord who once kidnapped her. She reads it at the breakfast
table without comment, writes a reply, and seals it before anyone
can ask what it says.

**The wine from the cellar — something to celebrate.** An
occasion arises (or she decides one does). She fetches a very old
bottle from the cellar and announces it by name, as if introducing
someone. The celebration is partly for the occasion and partly
because she wants people in the same room with glasses in their
hands.

**The piano bench — listening to Eiland play.** She sits at the
piano bench while Eiland plays. She says he would be a hit at
the Capital. She does not say whether she misses having an
audience of her own. If someone asks, she redirects to a story
about the time she sang for three encores in Aldara.

**Adeline won't stop working — the wine intervention.** Adeline
is at her desk again. It is raining. Elsie brings wine and tells
her to put the pen down. Adeline says no. Elsie pours the glass
anyway. Whether Adeline drinks it is Adeline's decision; whether
the glass appears is Elsie's.

**The born auntie — a role she chose.** Someone asks about her
family. She explains she is not related to Eiland and Adeline by
blood: "Some of us are simply born aunties, you see." The line
comes with a wink that makes it sound like a joke she is in on.
Whether the joke is a joke is a question she has not answered
for herself.

**The Capital visit — a letter from old friends.** An invitation
arrives from the Capital. Old friends, a salon, perhaps a concert.
She considers going. She says the stars are more beautiful here.
She also says she should host a viewing party for old friends one
of these years. She has said this before. She has not done it.

**Valen's clinic — the checkup.** She schedules a medical visit
with Valen. The visit turns into a conversation about romance,
aphrodisiacs, and who in town is seeing whom. No medical
questions are asked. Valen cannot deny Elsie knows her stuff.

## Design Notes

### Session Notes

Source material ingestion from extracted game assets: NPC data (elsie.toml), conversation bank files (93 dialogue files globbed, key lines extracted), shooting star festival cutscene data, quest references (gossip quest, 7 fetch quests), letters (poached pear recipe, pomegranate sorbet recipe), and cross-NPC references (Adeline, Eiland, Valen, Juniper). Autonomous Q&A pass — source material substituted for human answers.

Addon blocks included: Relationships (4 named), Voice/Dialogue (3 situations: casual, pressure, vulnerability), Story Seeds (11 entries — mix of explicit source events, implicit character psychology, and invented freeform seeds; no heart events but rich social/romantic material). Intimate Dynamics: excluded (project-level decision).

### Builder Context

- Physical appearance derived from portrait viewing across three seasons (spring, summer, winter). Hair consistently lavender-gray with curls. Winter portrait may show warmer tones due to pixel art palette shift, not actual hair color change.
- Elsie uses she/her pronouns throughout source material.
- Elsie is not dateable and has no heart event progression. Her character depth comes from conversation bank dialogue, her role in other characters' events (Eiland's 6-heart, the Shooting Star Festival), and her relationship dynamics with the Manor household.
- Core fear and false belief are interpretive — Elsie never states loneliness or performance anxiety directly. Evidence comes from her constant social seeking, memoir writing, and the pattern of inserting herself into others' romantic narratives. The warlord and Frederick stories are told casually, which could indicate either peace with the past or performance of peace with the past.
- Gossip quest and fetch quests characterize her as a social connector and someone who values small exchanges (flowers, recipes, news) — these are relationship-maintenance behaviors, not quest-giver mechanics.
- The "born auntie" self-description is the richest single line in her source material. Whether it's genuine self-knowledge or a rehearsed deflection is an open interpretive question; the card presents both readings.
