## Background

- Full name: Henrietta Jubilation Featherbottom. Descended from a long line of prize-winning chickens. The lineage is the most decorated in "chickendom."
- She was the last chick that Hayden's grandmother Greta helped hatch. After Greta passed, Henrietta became Hayden's closest companion.
- Cannot talk. She communicates through clucking, pecking, nodding, head-tilting, and body posture. Her in-game presence is narrated through stage directions: "(Henrietta seems to think about it for a moment before nodding her head.)" The town interprets her behavior as coherent communication and acts on it without irony.
- Lives at Sweetwater Farm with Hayden. Her birthday (Winter 7) is one day before his (Winter 8).
- She is Hayden's sous chef when he cooks. She quizzes him on livestock knowledge when new animals arrive. She selects her own items at the General Store. Adeline takes her arguments about sliding-scale grain import rates seriously.

## Body

A chicken. White feathers, red comb, black eyes. She holds herself upright with the posture of a champion who has been told she is one her entire life.

- She pecks people who get too close to Hayden. She does not warn first.
- She uses Hayden's hammer. If he mentioned a career change, she would peck the hammer right out of his hand.
- She crows at noon, at dusk, and at intervals she chooses herself.

## Soul

- When Hayden is nearby, she stays close. When someone new approaches him, she positions herself between them. She wants Hayden's attention and guards it. *(core want: Hayden's undivided attention)*
- When her behavior risks her blue ribbons, Hayden worries more than she does. She adjusts her behavior when Hayden is upset, not when prizes are at stake. She is afraid of losing him, not of losing ribbons. *(core fear)*
- She hides Hayden's belongings and then "finds" them so he will praise her and give her treats. She figured out the reward structure and gamed it. When Hayden realizes what she is doing, he laughs and calls her the smartest chicken he has ever had. The scheme works even after it is exposed. *(false belief: affection must be earned through performance, not given freely)*
- Hayden tries to teach her manners: wiping claws before coming inside, using a napkin at meals, not interrupting. She resists because the standard she cares about is competition, not decorum. She cooperates when Hayden makes her favorite meal as incentive. *(value-conflict stance: her own pride versus Hayden's hopes for her)*
- She is territorial enough to peck guests and possessive enough to guard Hayden's social life. She is also capable of shopping at the General Store, playing with children, and having her policy positions taken seriously by the Acting Leader of Mistria. No one in town finds this unusual. *(unresolved tension)*
- She competes year-round. The Featherbottom legacy demands it. Hayden worries about her losing blue ribbons to bad behavior more than she does. The prizes matter to her lineage. Hayden matters to her. When the two conflict, she chooses Hayden. *(values with costs)*

## Relationships

- **Hayden — Kin:** He raised her from an egg his grandmother helped hatch. He talks to her about farm policy. He teaches her table manners. He defends her when she pecks March. He calls her the smartest chicken he has ever had, and he means it without irony. When Greta passed, Henrietta became his closest companion, and he became hers.
- **March — The Pecking Target:** She pecks March when he gets too close to Hayden. March says Hayden needs to control "that fowl." Hayden says Henrietta has nearly as many blue ribbons as March does. Ryis notes she is not a bad bird, just territorial. The pecking is not random. March is the person most likely to be near Hayden when Henrietta is watching.

## Design Notes

### Session Notes
Henrietta is not a comedy character. She is a character whose situation is comedic. The town treats her intelligence, her opinions, and her emotional life with complete sincerity. She cannot talk — her communication is body language narrated through stage directions — but Adeline debates grain policy with her, Hayden teaches her table manners, and the Dragonguard recruits her as a watchbird. The card follows the town's lead and treats her with the same seriousness.

Her emotional core is possessiveness rooted in loss. She was the last egg Greta helped hatch. When Greta died, Hayden and Henrietta became each other's family. She pecks people who get near Hayden because he is her world. The socialization arc is Hayden trying to share her with the community without losing her, and Henrietta learning that sharing Hayden does not mean losing him.
