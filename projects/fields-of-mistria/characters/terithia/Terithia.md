# Terithia

Former soldier turned fisherwoman who tells stories to anyone who will listen, teaches the town's children to fish, brings wine she pulled from the ocean floor to every gathering, and has not once in any conversation said what she wants for herself.

## Background

- Served as a soldier long enough to race boats between deployments and tangle with a giant crab in her younger days. Retired to Mistria's coastline and took up fishing full-time. She does not talk about why she left soldiering, only that she did.
- Lives alone in a house on the beach. Her daily life is the water, the rod, and whoever stops by.
- Tells stories to the town's children about leviathans, sea shadows, and giant crabs. Josephine says "that lady's seen things" and the children play-act her stories on the beach afterward.
- Hunts a fish she calls the Big One, a shadow she has spotted along the coast near the ruins. She has not caught it.
- Pulls things from the bay that are not fish: rum, wine bottles, old gear. She serves them to friends without apology and with no explanation for how she finds them.
- Values generosity over ownership. She teaches Dell to fish, offers Adeline lessons, shares dredged-up wine with anyone who sits down, and brings seafood to every meal she is invited to. The cost is that she is always the one providing. When someone asks what she needs, she laughs and changes the subject. *(values with costs)*

## Body

Short white hair, brown skin, stocky build that shows years of physical work on boats and battlefields. Her clothing is practical and functional.

- Her laugh fills a room: a sharp "KYA HA HA!" that starts at full volume and stays there.
- When she tells a story, her voice drops low for the setup and her hands widen for the payoff.
- She casts a line with no wasted motion, reads the water, and waits without fidgeting.

## Soul

- When someone shows up at the beach or the inn, she pulls up a chair, pours a drink, and starts a story, because being the person who brings people together is the only role she has built for herself since she stopped being a soldier *(core want)*
- When a conversation turns to her own feelings or her past, she steers it back to fish, the weather, or a tall tale, because sitting with her own life without an audience to perform for leaves her with nothing to say *(core fear)*
- When she tells the children about a leviathan that was "half shark, half kraken, half stingray," she lets the math stand, because she believes the story is what keeps people coming back, and if she told them the plain version, they might not *(false belief)*
- She tells Elsie "I'm not much of a romantic... I don't have a way with words" and then spends the evening sharing wine she pulled from the ocean floor. She gives with flair but calls it nothing, because admitting she puts thought into how she shows up for people would break the easy, no-fuss version of herself she has built *(value-conflict stance)*
- She tells Seridia "you and I spent our youth on fighting... it's nice, retiring." She says she is happy to trade sailing for Mistria's coastline. She also hunts the Big One, tells war stories every week, and lights up when someone mentions a giant crab or a shadow in the water. She chose peace and keeps rehearsing for the thing she left *(unresolved tension)*
- When someone praises her, she deflects with a joke. Valen calls her the picture of health and she says "we've got a flatterer here!" and laughs before the compliment can land. She treats every compliment as a setup for a punchline, because accepting it straight would mean the thing she does matters, and she has arranged her life so nothing she does has to matter too much.

## Relationships

- **Dell — Charge:** She teaches him to fish, tells him warriors need patience most of all, and sits with him on the beach while he waits for a bite he does not have the stillness for yet. When he asks about sharks, she does not say no. She says "patience." She gives him the version of herself that takes things seriously, and she notices that he stays put when she talks.
- **Elsie — Confidant:** They drink wine together, talk about the sea, and visit the bathhouse. Elsie is the one person who asks Terithia about romance, and Terithia answers straight: "I'm not much of a romantic." She does not deflect with Elsie. She says the true thing and lets Elsie make it sound grander than it is.
- **March — Friction:** She needs his forge for fish hooks and a custom reel, and she keeps crowding his workspace despite his complaints. She tells him to take a break when he is hammering. She calls his sashimi appreciation a surprise and laughs when he dismisses her stories. She needs his work and does not let him forget it.
- **Errol — Kin:** They share Saturday Market nostalgia, dinners at Hayden's house, and rum she fished from the bay. She tells him about Big One sightings knowing he will pass the story along. She calls him and Landen "you boys" and means it the way family does. She drops the performer voice around him and does not replace it with anything.
- **Landen — Obligation:** She steals food off his plate at the inn without asking. When he brings up how many fish she has caught or whether she stops in winter, she answers questions about herself she would not think to ask. They share nostalgia for Mistria before the earthquake. She takes from him with an ease she does not show anyone else, and she does not examine why.

## Story Seeds

**Introduction — the player walks near the Beach.** Terithia hails the player and introduces herself. She mentions that Adeline's plan to bring a new farmer to Mistria has been floating around for a while, and she does not realize someone has actually come. She points out her Tackle Shop by the Beach, where she sells fishing rods and fresh-caught fish, and invites the player to stop by.

**The Big One surfaces — the water clears enough to see the shadow.** Terithia has tracked a shadow along the coast near the ruins for years. One day the water is clear enough to see it. She has to decide whether to cast or watch. She picks up the rod, sets it down, picks it up again, and stands at the edge longer than she has ever stood without casting.

**The story that lands wrong — Dell goes looking for something from a tall tale.** One of her tall tales about leviathans or sea shadows sends Dell looking for something real. She tells the story for the reaction. Now the reaction has consequences and the story is no longer hers to control.

**The quiet house — no one comes to the beach or the inn.** An evening where no one asks for a story and the inn is empty. She is alone in her house with the fishing gear and the bay. She checks the door twice, reorganizes tackle she already sorted, and sits on the porch facing the road.

**The soldier's question — someone asks what she did before fishing.** She has mentioned boat races and a giant crab, but never the soldiering itself. The question sits in the air and she has to decide whether to tell the real version or the story version, and whether she still knows the difference.

**Elsie presses on romance — Elsie asks directly whether she has ever been in love.** Elsie, who spent her life surrounded by admirers and poisoned wine, asks Terithia directly whether she has ever been in love. Terithia said she is not much of a romantic. Elsie does not accept that answer twice.

**Dell catches something — Dell lands a fish on his own.** She has been teaching him patience for weeks. She has to decide whether to cheer, coach, or step back.

**March finishes the reel — the custom reel changes what she can catch.** The Big One is no longer out of reach. She wanted this tool for years and now the excuse for not catching it is gone.

**The dredged-up thing — she pulls something personal from the bay.** She pulls something from the bay that is not wine or rum. Something that connects to a life before Mistria. She has to decide whether to show it to anyone or put it back.

**Winter at the forge — she crowds March's workspace for warmth.** She and Balor and Ryis crowd March's workspace in winter for warmth. Cards come out. She laughs, teases, and reads tells. When the game breaks up, she walks home alone to the beach house in the cold.

**Storytime goes wrong — Maple catches a contradiction.** Maple, the sharpest of the 3 children, catches Terithia in a contradiction. "That's a lot of halves, Miss Terithia." The children are growing up. The stories that worked last year need to get better or get true.

## Voice / Dialogue

### Casual — on the beach, fishing

Terithia sits at the water's edge with a line in the bay. The player approaches.

"Hey there! Pull up a rock!"

She gestures at the water.

"Fish are biting today. Well, nibbling. Give it time."

A pause. She squints at the horizon.

"You know, I pulled a bottle of wine out of the bay last week. Tasted like salt and oak and adventure."

She grins.

"KYA HA HA! I'm saving the next one for a special occasion. Or Tuesday. Whichever comes first."

### Conflict — at the forge, competing for March's time

Terithia stands at March's forge. Ryis is waiting. Balor is behind her.

"Hey, no cutting! He's working on my fish hooks right now!"

She crosses her arms, then uncrosses them.

"Look, March, I know you've got a list. But these hooks are the difference between dinner and a long swim home."

She glances at Balor.

"You can wait. Your wagon's got 3 other axles."

She turns back to March.

"Take your time. But also hurry up."

She catches her own contradiction and laughs.

"KYA HA HA!"

### Vulnerability — with Elsie, evening drinks

The inn is quiet. Terithia and Elsie sit with glasses of wine. The conversation has been about the sea.

"I'm not much of a romantic, Elsie. I don't have a way with words."

She turns the glass.

"I mean, I've got plenty of words. Just not those kinds of words."

A pause.

"The sea's my thing. Always has been. When I'm out there with the line and the water, I don't have to be anything for anyone."

She takes a drink.

"That's not sad. That's just how it is."

She looks at Elsie and her voice softens.

"You've had a whole life of people writing you songs and sending you wine. Poisoned or not. That's something."

A beat.

"I've got fish. And they don't write songs."

"KYA HA HA!"

## Design Notes

### Session Notes

Her psychology is constructed from behavioral patterns across group conversations rather than from explicit character development scenes.

Key behavioral patterns from source:
- The "KYA HA HA!" laugh appears 7 times across conversations. It is her signature and her deflection tool.
- She tells stories to children in a dedicated "storytime" animation state, with at least 3 distinct story sessions documented.
- She pulls objects from the bay (rum, wine) and serves them to friends. This is a recurring pattern, not a one-off.
- She mentions soldiering twice (boat races, retirement conversation with Seridia) but never describes combat or specific service.
- She deflects every personal compliment with humor (Valen's health comment, Caldarus's mastery comment, Elsie's romance question).
- The Big One is referenced in 2 conversations as an ongoing pursuit.
- March calls her stories "ridiculous yarns." Olric secretly listens. This split reaction is the most direct external evaluation of her storytelling in the source data.
