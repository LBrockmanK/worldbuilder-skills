# Fields of Mistria — Adventure Structure

Notes on the core pillars of the adventure. These will become the
adventure-level instructions for ainime export — the narrative
threads that give the freeform experience shape without constraining
it.

## The four pillars

### 1. Town Restoration

The communal arc. The town is earthquake-damaged; the player helps
rebuild it. Progressive projects unlock buildings, expand services,
and change daily life. This is the reason the player is here — the
homestead-for-help exchange.

Documented: `concepts/town-restoration.md`

### 2. Magic and the Dungeon

The mystery arc. The earthquake disturbed something ancient beneath
the Narrows. Strange magic flows through the land. The seal chambers,
the dragon tablets, Caldarus and Seridia's involvement — this is the
story's depth, the question of what Mistria is built on top of.

Documented: `concepts/the-seals.md`, `concepts/magic.md`

### 3. Self-Development and the Homestead

The personal arc. The player's own progression — developing their
farm, expanding their home, building skills, becoming self-sufficient.
This is freeform and player-directed: farming, ranching, fishing,
mining, cooking, crafting — whatever the player gravitates toward.
Not everyone will focus on farming; the homestead supports any
lifestyle.

Documented: `concepts/the-homestead.md`, `concepts/farming.md`,
`concepts/ranching.md`, `concepts/fishing.md`, `concepts/mining.md`

### 4. Interpersonal Relationships

The social arc. Building friendships and romances with the cast.
This is largely built into the system already — character cards carry
personality, preferences, and relationship dynamics. The ainime
platform handles relationship tracking natively (Influence scores,
relationship stages).

What still needs definition for export:
- How relationship progression surfaces in the adventure (milestones,
  behavioral shifts, unlocked conversations)
- Festival date mechanics as relationship-driven story moments
- Marriage and family as late-game relationship outcomes
- How NPCs reference the player's relationships with other NPCs
  (gossip, awareness, reactions)

## How the pillars interact

The pillars aren't sequential — they run in parallel and feed each
other. Restoring the town creates spaces for socializing. Mining
for materials funds restoration projects. Relationship depth unlocks
story conversations that touch on the seals and the town's history.
The homestead reflects all of it — a farm that grows alongside the
community.

The adventure instructions should present all four as available
threads and let the player pull on whichever interests them. An
AI narrator's job is to keep all four threads warm — advancing the
ones the player engages with, gently reminding them of the others
through NPC dialogue and world events.

## Export notes

These pillars map to ainime adventure configuration:
- Town Restoration → core quest/story progression system
- Magic/Dungeon → story arc with progression gates
- Homestead → player-state tracking (what's built, what's grown)
- Relationships → native Influence system + character card behaviors

Detailed adventure instructions will be written as part of the ainime
export work. These notes capture the structure so it's not lost.
