# Fields of Mistria — Seed

## Setting Summary

Mistria is a small coastal village in a pre-industrial fantasy world, set between dense forests and the sea. An earthquake recently devastated the town's infrastructure: the bridge to the outside world collapsed, mine shafts caved in, and several buildings need repair. The village's ruling nobles, Baron Wiscar and Baroness Linnet, were called to the Capital in the aftermath, leaving their daughter Adeline as Acting Leader. She posted a call for a capable outsider, offering an overgrown homestead in exchange for help rebuilding.

The player is that outsider. They arrive to find a community of thirty-four named characters pulling itself together through shared work and stubborn routine. Twenty-four are in town at the start; eight traveling vendors are stranded on the other side of the broken bridge; two draconic beings arrive later as the story unfolds.

The world runs on a four-season calendar of 28 days per season. Magic exists but is poorly understood. The earthquake exposed ancient ruins and strange energy beneath the land. Sealed chambers lie under the Narrows, dragon tablets are carved into a stele on the Manor grounds, and the history beneath Mistria is older than anyone alive remembers. The tone is warm and hopeful despite the damage. This is a community repairing itself, not a place in despair.

## Genre and Tone

Farming life-sim RPG with fantasy elements. The primary tone is warm, pastoral, and community-focused: the everyday rhythm of a small town where people know each other and pitch in. The world allows moments of genuine melancholy — characters carry real losses, loneliness, unfulfilled ambitions — but never despair. The darkest register is bittersweet: a character's regret about a path not taken, the weight of responsibility on someone too young for it, the quiet ache of missing someone who left.

Romance is earnest and slow-building, never cynical. Fantasy elements (magic, ancient ruins, draconic beings) add wonder and mystery without overwhelming the domestic core. Violence is limited to mine combat against dungeon creatures; interpersonal conflict is emotional, not physical. The overall feel: a world that is difficult in honest ways and kind in honest ways.

## Inspirations

- Stardew Valley — farming life rhythm, community restoration arc, relationships built through daily interaction and seasonal cycles
- Harvest Moon — pastoral tone, the feel of a small town that runs on routines and traditions
- Rune Factory — integration of dungeon exploration and combat alongside farming and social life, fantasy elements grounded in a domestic setting
- Animal Crossing — the unhurried pace, satisfaction in small daily accomplishments, a world that continues when you are not looking

## Tonal Inspirations

- Kiki's Delivery Service — a young person earning belonging in a new community through work and persistence
- My Neighbor Totoro — wonder existing alongside the mundane, nature as presence rather than obstacle
- Whisper of the Heart — quiet ambition and self-discovery within a community setting
- Mushishi — ancient forces beneath the surface of a calm world, understood through patience rather than force

## Key Tropes and Themes

- Found community — belonging earned through presence and effort, not birthright
- Rebuilding together — restoration as communal act, not individual heroism
- Slow seasons — time measured in harvests and festivals, not deadlines
- Hidden depths — ancient ruins beneath the village, private struggles beneath pleasant faces
- Earned trust — relationships built through consistent small attention, not grand gestures
- Nature as partner — farming, foraging, and fishing as collaboration with the land, not extraction from it
- Gentle mystery — sealed chambers and dragon lore that invite curiosity, not urgency
- Second chances — a damaged town and an outsider starting over, people remaking their lives
- Domestic magic — extraordinary elements woven into everyday routines without displacing them
- Festival rhythms — communal celebrations that mark time and deepen bonds
- The weight of leadership — young people carrying responsibilities they did not choose
- Roots and wandering — the tension between staying and leaving, home and horizon

## Community

Mistria is a village where everyone has a role and most people have been here long enough to take each other for granted, in the comfortable way. The community runs on informal obligation: Hemlock and Josephine feed the town at the Inn, March and Olric keep the forge going, Nora and Holt stock the General Store. People gather at Friday Night at the Inn not because it is organized but because it is what they do.

The earthquake disrupted services but not social bonds. If anything, shared hardship tightened them. There is a class divide between the Manor household on the hill and the town below, but it is gentle: Adeline is respected for stepping up, not resented for privilege. Elsie, a retired opera singer from the Capital, moved into the Manor after the earthquake and serves as the household's warm, meddling elder — not a blood relative of the family but treated as one.

Outsiders are uncommon. The broken bridge means no one new has arrived in months. The player is the first outsider in a while, which makes them interesting but subject to quiet scrutiny. The community is warm but not naive — people notice things, talk, and form opinions. Acceptance is genuine once earned.

## World Introduction

Somewhere along the coast, past the forest and over the ridge, there is a village called Mistria. A few dozen buildings between the trees and the sea, fields that catch the morning light, a river winding through the center of town.

An earthquake changed that. The bridge connecting Mistria to the outside world collapsed. Buildings cracked. The mines caved in. The Baron and Baroness were called to the Capital, leaving their daughter to hold things together.

She posted a call for help: a homestead, overgrown and in need of work, but yours, in exchange for a capable pair of hands. You answered.

The ferry drops you at the dock. The town is quieter than the posting suggested. Smaller. The people who meet you are friendly but tired, hopeful but cautious. They have been managing on their own for a while now. They could use someone who sticks around.

## Opening Situation

The player arrives in Mistria with nothing but what they carry. Adeline, the Acting Leader, greets them and walks them to Sweetwater Farm: a neglected homestead on the edge of town. The farm is overgrown but structurally sound. A house, some land, and the implicit expectation that the player will make themselves useful.

The town's bridge is out, isolating Mistria from outside trade and travel. Twenty-four named characters are in town; eight traveling vendors cannot arrive until the bridge is repaired. The mines are closed after the earthquake. The Request Board at the town center lists community needs: rebuilding projects, material gathering, small favors.

Four parallel threads are available from the start: restoring the town's infrastructure through community projects, developing the player's own farm and home, building relationships with the residents, and exploring the mysteries emerging from the earthquake — strange magic, exposed ruins, and sealed chambers beneath the Narrows.

Nothing is urgent. The town has survived this long; it will survive a few more days. The player chooses what matters to them, and the story follows.

## Era

Pre-industrial fantasy village. No electricity, no engines. Magic is rare and poorly understood. Hand tools, horse-drawn transport, oil lamps, wood and stone construction. The cultural reference is roughly late-medieval European rural, but without specific real-world mapping — this is its own place.

## Art Style

The visual identity draws from Studio Ghibli's painted backgrounds and the soft character design of modern farming-sim games. Environments are warm, detailed, and richly colored: shingled and tiled roofs, stone and timber buildings, cobblestone plazas with a central fountain, cherry blossom trees along the paths, a river running through the east side of town. Light carries mood — golden morning light through farm fences, blue twilight over the ocean, warm lantern glow from inn windows at night.

Characters are anime-influenced but grounded: expressive faces, natural proportions, clothing that looks functional and lived-in. Ages range from children to elderly; body types vary naturally. Fantasy elements (draconic characters, magical effects) are visually distinct but belong in the same world as the thatched cottages — nothing garish, nothing that breaks the pastoral register.

Color palette shifts with the seasons: fresh greens and cherry-blossom pinks in spring, deep greens and golden yellows in summer, warm oranges and russet browns in fall, cool blues and whites with warm interior contrast in winter.

Musical reference: acoustic instrumentation — piano, strings, woodwinds, gentle percussion. Pastoral and warm, with hints of melancholy in minor-key passages. Seasonal variations in tempo and mood.
