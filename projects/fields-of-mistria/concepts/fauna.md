# Fauna

*Each level of the mines has its own ecosystem of creatures adapted to that world.*

## Premise

The mines beneath the Narrows are not empty tunnels — they are habitats. Each level supports its own population of creatures, distinct in form and behavior. The surface has its own wildlife too: seasonal bugs, birds, and the small animals that share the landscape. But the underground fauna is what gives the mines their character.

## By habitat

- **Upper Mines** — Rockclods hop and spit stone. Saplings lunge from corners. Red mushrooms retract into their caps when threatened. Essence bats flit through the dark. These are the familiar dangers of shallow tunnels.
- **Tide Caverns** — Enchanterns flicker like underwater lanterns. Green mushrooms explode when defeated. Blue essence bats are stronger and faster than their upper-level cousins. The salt air and wet stone produce creatures adapted to damp and darkness.
- **Deep Earth** — Stalagmite creatures erupt from the floor. Blue mushrooms phase in and out of visibility. The creatures here are tougher, shaped by pressure and mineral-rich stone. Ironclods and silverclods carry ore in their bodies.
- **Lava Caves** — Lava cats prowl the ledges, petrifying when exposed to water. Flame spirits drift and teleport, firing homing projectiles. Purple mushrooms spew molten material. Everything here is adapted to volcanic heat.
- **The Ruins** — Griffin statues animate from their pedestals. Flying tomes circle and dive. Void cats lurk in the darkness, vulnerable to light. Rock stacks launch themselves into the air. The creatures of the ruins feel less natural and more constructed — guardians of something ancient.
- **Surface** — Seasonal bugs follow their own calendar: butterflies and caterpillars in spring, fireflies and cicadas in summer, walking leaves in fall, frost flutter butterflies in winter. The beach, deep woods, and grasslands each host their own insect populations.

## Narrative significance

The fauna reinforces that each mine level is a distinct world, not just a deeper tunnel. Creatures are not random — they belong to their environment the way the flora and minerals do. Fighting through them is part of understanding the place, and the progression from rockclods to griffin statues mirrors the journey from practical mining to uncovering ancient secrets.
