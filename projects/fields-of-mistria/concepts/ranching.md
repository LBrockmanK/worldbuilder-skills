# Ranching

*Eight species, daily care, and the quiet satisfaction of a barn full of animals.*

## Keywords

ranching, animals, livestock, barn, chickens, cows, sheep, breeding

## Premise

The fields around Mistria support a range of domesticated animals. Chickens, cows, ducks, horses, sheep, rabbits, alpacas, and capybaras — each with their own products, temperaments, and needs.

## Life with animals

Raising animals starts with building the right housing — a coop for small animals (chickens, ducks, rabbits, capybaras) or a barn for large ones (cows, sheep, horses, alpacas). Once housing is ready, Hayden at Sweetwater Farm sells the animals themselves, along with feed, accessories, and care supplies. It is a real investment — not something that happens overnight.

Animals need consistent care — feeding, attention, shelter from bad weather, time outdoors on good days. In return they produce eggs, milk, wool, feathers, bristles, and hair. Animals that are well cared for over time produce finer goods. Breeding introduces variety: rare color variants appear, and seasonal births bring animals adapted to the time of year they were born.

Small animals can be picked up and carried. Large animals are tended where they stand, and some can be ridden.

## Narrative significance

Ranching adds a living dimension to the homestead. Crops are patient and predictable; animals are not. They create daily obligations and small rewards — the first egg from a new hen, a rare-colored foal, a capybara splashing in the pond. The animals make the farm feel inhabited, not just productive, and Hayden's involvement ties ranching into the broader community.
