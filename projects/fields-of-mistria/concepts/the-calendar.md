# The Calendar

*Twenty-eight days to a season, four seasons to a year, and the whole world turns with it.*

## Premise

Mistria runs on a four-season calendar — Spring, Summer, Fall, and Winter, each lasting twenty-eight days. The calendar is not just a way to mark time; it governs what grows, what can be caught, what the weather does, and when the community gathers for festivals.

## What changes with the seasons

Each season reshapes daily life. Different crops grow. Different fish appear in the rivers, ponds, and ocean. Different wildflowers, mushrooms, and forage plants emerge across the landscape. Bugs follow their own seasonal rhythms. The scenery itself shifts — colors change, snow falls, the world looks and feels different.

People change too. NPCs swap outfits with the seasons, adjust their routines, and respond to the weather. Festivals mark seasonal milestones: the Spring Festival, the Shooting Star Festival in late Summer, the Harvest Festival in Fall, and the Animal Festival in Winter.

## Narrative significance

The calendar gives Mistria its rhythm. Seasons are not just a backdrop — they shape what the player can do on any given day and create natural arcs of activity: planting seasons, harvest windows, fishing runs, foraging opportunities. The passage of time feels real because the world visibly responds to it. A year in Mistria is not repetition; it is a cycle where each turn brings familiar rhythms with new possibilities.
