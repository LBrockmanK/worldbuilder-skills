## Variant Spreads

### Entry 1: The Performance

**Source fact:** Presents as airhead; reads situations and people with precision; the vapidity is performance; the laziness is real.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
When a conversation turns serious around her, she yawns, stretches, asks something tangential — and later acts on exactly what was said, word for word, proving she caught every detail while performing disinterest.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The airhead act works so well that humans who survive encounters with her genuinely believe she stumbled into predation by accident. The cost is that the people she actually likes also underestimate her, and she has to watch them make decisions she could have warned them about.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
To anyone watching, she looks like the village's least attentive resident — she sunbathes through council meetings, forgets names she has been told twice, loses track of conversations mid-sentence. She has not misread a room in years.

---

### Entry 2: The Framework

**Source fact:** Views humans through an animal-husbandry framework; community matters, individuals do not; only giantess who has actively named it.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
She refers to Viralys's human population the way a farmer discusses herd health — tracking birth rates, watching for sickness in the group, noting when someone new arrives or someone old stops showing up. Individual names attach to patterns, not to people.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The framework lets her eat a man she spoke to yesterday and check on the district he lived in tomorrow. Other giantesses who do similar things flinch from naming what they are doing. She named it first and has not flinched yet, and the naming is what makes the others uncomfortable around her.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
A visiting merchant would see a giantess who knows the town's demographics better than its steward — which neighborhoods are growing, which trades are understaffed, whose children survived the winter. What they would not see is that this knowledge comes from the same attention a rancher pays a herd, and she would tell them so if they asked.

---

### Entry 3: The Predation Method

**Source fact:** Social predator through persuasion; constructs pretexts for humans to enter her mouth; manipulates through airhead persona; unpredictability is her tool.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
She does not chase or grab. She suggests, invites, asks for help with something near her mouth, drops something a human has to retrieve. Each scenario has a plausible reason for proximity that the human agrees to before anything happens.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The method requires patience and a reputation for harmlessness. A human who has watched her bumble through three conversations will not think twice about climbing onto her palm to inspect an itch she cannot reach. The setup sometimes takes days; she does not mind.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
An outside observer would see a clumsy giantess who somehow ends up with humans near her face through a series of innocent accidents. Someone tracking patterns would notice that the accidents only happen when no one else is watching, and that the humans who help her with the most harmless-sounding requests are the ones who stop appearing in town.

---

### Entry 4: Core Want

**Source fact:** Cares about the people around her and will not admit this; found people she does not want to leave; the community matters.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
She wants to belong somewhere without having to change what she is. She found a village that tolerates a predator, a tree big enough to sleep in, and a friend who does not ask her to stop. She arranges her life to keep all of it, and the arranging takes more effort than she would ever admit to.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
What drives most of her decisions — where she sleeps, which humans she talks to, how aggressively she responds to threats — is preserving the specific arrangement she has in Viralys. Not any village; this one, these people, this particular configuration of tolerance and proximity. If she had to name what she wants, she would say comfort. The word she avoids is home.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
To an outside observer she wants nothing — she sunbathes, she eats, she sleeps. To someone who has watched her for years, she wants everything exactly as it is and will fight to keep it that way, which is why she guards the village with a ferocity that contradicts every other thing about her.

---

### Entry 5: Core Fear

**Source fact:** "She did not want to know that" (that she cared about the community); will not admit caring.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
What frightens her is not exposure or vulnerability in any conventional sense. It is the possibility that someone will state plainly what she already suspects — that she cares about these people as people, not as a resource — and she will not be able to deny it, because by then her behavior will have already proved it.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
She arranges her life to avoid a single conversation: the one where someone says "you love them" and she cannot deflect with a joke. The fear is not the accusation. The fear is that she will agree, and once she agrees, the framework she uses to eat the ones she loves stops working.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
She looks like a creature with nothing to lose — no attachments, no stakes, no investment. She is terrified that this has stopped being true, and the evidence is in every decision she makes to stay.

---

### Entry 6: False Belief

**Source fact:** Tells herself she stays for the tree and the convenience; stays because she found people she does not want to leave.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
She has an explanation for every year she has spent in Viralys: the tree is comfortable, the climate suits her metabolism, moving would require effort. Each explanation is factually true and none of them are the reason. She has never tested whether she could leave.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The false belief costs her a kind of self-knowledge that would change how she treats people. If she admitted the tree is not the reason, she would have to reconsider what the predation means when it is directed at a community she chose to join. She does not reconsider.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
Ask her why she stays and she will list practical reasons with the fluency of someone who has rehearsed. Ask her what would make her leave and the answer comes slower, full of qualifications, and ends with a subject change. She has not noticed the asymmetry.

---

### Entry 7: Value-Conflict Stance

**Source fact:** Spares Rado Stary — claims no sport, reason closer to pity; when humans die permanently she says nothing; operating code says individuals expendable.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
Her operating code says humans are individually expendable. When that code meets a specific human whose suffering she recognizes — not a category of suffering but this person, this moment — she makes an exception and explains it away as pragmatics. The exceptions have a pattern she has not examined.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The collision shows in her excuses. She spares a man and calls it unsporting. She sits silent after a death and calls it boredom. The excuses get thinner each time, but as long as she has one, the framework holds. She needs the framework to hold.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
Someone watching her predation over months would see a system with invisible rules she has never articulated. Certain people in certain states are exempt, and the exemptions follow emotional lines she would deny having. The code says expendable; her behavior says otherwise, case by case, never as a policy.

---

### Entry 8: Grief Response

**Source fact:** When a human she knows dies permanently she notices and says nothing; moves to a different spot and lies there.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
She does not attend funerals or ask what happened. She moves to a part of the village she does not usually occupy, lies down, and stays there. After a day or two she moves back. She has never explained this and would deny it has a pattern if confronted.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The grief costs her the one thing she values most about herself: the belief that individual humans do not register. Each time she moves, she is proving herself wrong, and the proof dissipates only because she never examines it afterward.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
To the village it looks like a lamia's territorial quirk — she shifts position sometimes, that is what snakes do. To Vesper, who has watched it happen four times, it looks like mourning with the name filed off.

---

### Entry 9: Protection Behavior

**Source fact:** When the village faces external threats she responds with brutal effectiveness, returns to sunbathing, refuses credit.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
When something threatens Viralys from outside — raiders, territorial animals, weather damage that could cascade — she stops performing and moves with a speed and precision that makes the airhead act retroactively obvious. As soon as the threat is handled, she is back on her rock, complaining about the sun angle.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The contradiction costs her nothing as long as no one names it. She eats the people she guards. She guards the village she treats as a resource. Every external threat she handles reinforces a pattern she has no interest in acknowledging: she is defending something she values, not maintaining a food supply.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
A visiting official would see a giantess who is useless six days out of seven and irreplaceable on the seventh. The villagers see a creature who will demolish anything that threatens them and then pretend she was just stretching. Both observations are correct; neither is complete.

---

### Entry 10: Social Texture

**Source fact:** Presents as careless about her size while actually being very careful; cajoles people closer rather than grabbing; slow, deliberate positioning.

**Variant A (trigger + action):**
In ordinary conversation she slumps, lets her tail drape, gestures loosely — all of it calculated to take up more space than necessary without appearing to try. Humans step around her, adjust their paths, and end up closer to her face than they planned. She never reaches for anyone.

**Variant B (cost / consequence):**
The carefulness is the tell. She has never knocked over a market stall, never displaced a rope ladder, never crushed a cart by accident. In a body that could demolish the village center by rolling over, she has a perfect safety record — except for the people who disappear. The control it takes to be that precise is invisible to everyone who benefits from it.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
Watch her with humans and you see warmth: she laughs easily, touches people gently with the tip of her tail, remembers jokes. Watch her positioning and you see a predator: the laugh puts people at ease, the tail-touch tests whether they flinch, the jokes extend the interaction past the point where a nervous human would leave.

## Secondary Trial: Variant Count Comparison

### Goal

Determine optimal variant count for synthesis. Compare 2-variant, 3-variant, and 5-variant spreads feeding the synthesis mechanism.

### Additional Variants (D and E)

Two additional divergence angles beyond A (trigger+action), B (cost/consequence), C (observer vs. reality):
- Variant D: the internal framing — what the character tells themselves, how they categorize their own behavior
- Variant E: the exception — the moment the pattern breaks or an edge case reveals the rule

#### Entry 1: The Performance

**Variant D (internal framing):**
She knows they think she is stupid. She considered correcting this exactly once, decided the performance was worth more than the acknowledgment, and never considered it again.

**Variant E (exception):**
The act drops entirely around Vesper. Not because she trusts Vesper more — because Vesper saw through it years ago, and maintaining a performance for an audience that is not buying tickets is the one kind of effort she will not waste.

#### Entry 2: The Framework

**Variant D:**
She does not think of what she does as a philosophy. It is closer to bookkeeping — a system for knowing what she has, what she uses, and what replenishes. The word "husbandry" would not occur to her; it occurred to others watching.

**Variant E:**
The framework has no entry for Vesper. She has never tried to categorize her, which is its own kind of data — the system that accounts for everything cannot account for the one thing she chose before the system existed.

#### Entry 3: The Predation Method

**Variant D:**
She does not think of it as hunting. She thinks of it as hospitality that goes one way — she invited them, they came, what happens next is between the two of them. The word "prey" belongs to creatures who chase.

**Variant E:**
She has never used the method on a child. She has not articulated this rule. She would not recognize it as mercy if confronted, and the children who grow up around her do not know they were ever exempt.

#### Entry 4: Core Want

**Variant D:**
She does not frame it as wanting. She frames it as not leaving — a subtraction rather than a choice. Every morning she does not leave is not a decision to stay; it is the absence of a reason to go. She has been absent of reasons for years.

**Variant E:**
When the village was threatened badly enough that evacuation was discussed, she moved to block the only route large enough for her body. She said she was too lazy to travel. No one pointed out that she had just moved faster than anyone had seen in years.

#### Entry 5: Core Fear

**Variant D:**
She has rehearsed the deflection. If someone says it — the actual words — she will yawn, scratch her neck, ask about dinner. She has practiced this in the specific sense that she has imagined the conversation and chosen the exit. People who are not afraid do not plan escapes from hypothetical sentences.

**Variant E:**
Vesper came close once. Said something about how Kallya "chose this place." Kallya changed the subject so fast that Vesper stopped talking for the rest of the afternoon. They have not mentioned it since.

#### Entry 6: False Belief

**Variant D:**
She has told herself the story so many times it has the smoothness of something true. The tree, the climate, the effort of moving. She could recite it in her sleep. The fluency is the tell — real reasons do not need rehearsal.

**Variant E:**
When the tree was damaged in a storm, she repaired it with an urgency that surprised her. Not because she needed shelter — she had slept in open fields for decades before Viralys. She repaired it because it was hers, and the word "hers" had started meaning more than property.

#### Entry 7: Value-Conflict Stance

**Variant D:**
She knows the excuses are getting worse. Unsporting. Inconvenient. Not hungry. She does not examine why they are getting worse because the examination would end the system, and the system is how she eats and cares in the same body without having to choose.

**Variant E:**
She ate a man she liked. Not by accident — she liked him specifically, his particular way of laughing at nothing, and she ate him anyway. He resurrected. She has not spoken to him since, and he does not remember why she avoids him.

#### Entry 8: Grief Response

**Variant D:**
She does not call it grief. She does not call it anything. The body moves to a place it does not usually occupy, stays there, then moves back. If asked, she would say she was bored of the usual spot. She is not aware that the timing correlates.

**Variant E:**
She did it once for a human she had eaten. The human resurrected, walked past her new resting spot, and waved. She moved back to her usual place without acknowledging the wave. This is the closest she has come to a visible reaction.

#### Entry 9: Protection Behavior

**Variant D:**
She does not think of it as protection. Protection implies something worth guarding, which implies attachment, which implies the conversation she has been avoiding. She thinks of it as pest control — things that threaten her territory get removed, same as clearing a fallen log.

**Variant E:**
After the last external threat, a child brought her food as thanks. She ate the food, not the child, and said nothing. The child came back the next day with more food. This has been happening for two months and she has not mentioned it to anyone.

#### Entry 10: Social Texture

**Variant D:**
She does not think about the positioning. It happens the way breathing happens — automatic, trained into the body over decades. The calculation is real, but she would be surprised to hear it described as calculation. She thinks of it as being comfortable.

**Variant E:**
There is one human she has never positioned near her face. A woman who brings her cloth samples to feel, and who talks about weaving the way Kallya talks about the village — as a system to be tended. Kallya has not decided what this exception means.

**3S-5:** She arranges her life to avoid a single conversation: the one where someone says plainly that she cares about these people as people, and she cannot deflect with a joke. The fear is not the accusation. The fear is that she will agree, and once she agrees, the framework she uses to eat the ones she loves stops working.

**3S-6:** She has an explanation for every year she has spent in Viralys: the tree is comfortable, the climate suits her, moving would require effort. Each explanation is factually true and none of them are the reason. Ask her what would make her leave and the answer comes slower, full of qualifications, and ends with a subject change.

**3S-7:** Her operating code says humans are individually expendable. When a specific human's suffering registers — not a category but this person, this moment — she makes an exception and explains it away: unsporting, inconvenient, not worth the effort. The excuses get thinner each time, but as long as she has one, the code holds.

**3S-8:** When a human she knows dies permanently, she does not attend the funeral or ask what happened. She moves to a part of the village she does not usually occupy and lies there for a day or two. To most of the village it looks like a territorial quirk. Vesper, who has watched it happen four times, recognizes it as mourning with the name filed off.

**3S-9:** When something threatens Viralys from outside, she stops performing and moves with a speed that makes the airhead act retroactively obvious. As soon as the threat is handled she is back on her rock, complaining about the sun angle. She eats the people she guards; she guards the village she treats as a resource. No one has named this to her face.

**3S-10:** In conversation she laughs easily, touches people with the tip of her tail, remembers jokes. Her body language is calculated: she slumps, drapes, gestures loosely, taking up space that routes humans closer to her face. She has never knocked over a market stall, never crushed a cart, never displaced a rope ladder by accident — a perfect safety record, except for the people who disappear.

#### 5-Variant Synthesis (from A + B + C + D + E)

**5S-1 (Performance):**
She knows they think she is stupid — she decided the performance was worth more than the acknowledgment and never reconsidered. To anyone watching, she sunbathes through council meetings and forgets names she has been told twice. She has not misread a room in years. The act drops entirely around Vesper, who saw through it before the first year was out.

**5S-2 (Framework):**
She tracks the town the way a farmer tracks a herd — birth rates, sickness, who arrives, who stops showing up. The framework lets her eat a man she spoke to yesterday and check on his district tomorrow. She named what other giantesses flinch from naming, and it has no entry for Vesper — the one thing she chose before the system existed.

**5S-3 (Predation):**
She does not chase or grab. She suggests, invites, asks for help near her face. A human who has watched her bumble through three conversations will not think twice about climbing onto her palm. She does not think of it as hunting — she thinks of it as hospitality that goes one way. She has never used the method on a child, and would not recognize this as mercy if confronted.

**5S-4 (Core Want):**
She does not frame it as wanting. Every morning she does not leave is not a decision to stay — it is the absence of a reason to go. She has been absent of reasons for years. When the village discussed evacuation, she moved to block the only route large enough for her body and said she was too lazy to travel.

**5S-5 (Core Fear):**
She has rehearsed the deflection — if someone says the actual words, she will yawn, scratch her neck, ask about dinner. People who are not afraid do not plan escapes from hypothetical sentences. Vesper came close once, said something about how Kallya "chose this place." Kallya changed the subject so fast that Vesper stopped talking for the rest of the afternoon.

**5S-6 (False Belief):**
She has told herself the story so many times it has the smoothness of something true — the tree, the climate, the effort of moving. Ask her what would make her leave and the answer comes slower, full of qualifications, and ends with a subject change. When the tree was damaged in a storm, she repaired it with an urgency that surprised her — not because she needed shelter, but because it was hers, and "hers" had started meaning more than property.

**5S-7 (Value-Conflict):**
She knows the excuses are getting worse — unsporting, inconvenient, not hungry. She does not examine why because the examination would end the system, and the system is how she eats and cares in the same body without choosing. She ate a man she liked — his particular way of laughing at nothing — and ate him anyway. He resurrected. She has not spoken to him since.

**5S-8 (Grief):**
She does not call it anything. The body moves to a place it does not usually occupy, stays there, then moves back. To the village it looks like a territorial quirk. She did it once for a human she had eaten — the human resurrected, walked past her, and waved. She moved back to her usual place without acknowledging the wave.

**5S-9 (Protection):**
When something threatens Viralys, she stops performing and moves with a speed that makes the airhead act retroactively obvious. She does not think of it as protection — she thinks of it as pest control. After the last threat, a child brought her food as thanks. She ate the food, not the child, and said nothing. The child has been coming back for two months.

**5S-10 (Social Texture):**
She does not think about the positioning — it happens the way breathing happens, automatic, trained into decades of habit. She slumps, drapes, gestures loosely, routing humans closer to her face. She has never knocked over a market stall or crushed a cart. There is one human she has never positioned near her face — a woman who brings cloth samples and talks about weaving the way Kallya talks about the village. She has not decided what this exception means.
