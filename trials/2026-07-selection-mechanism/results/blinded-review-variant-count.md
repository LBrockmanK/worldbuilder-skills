# Variant Count Trial — Blinded Review

**Character:** Kallya
**Section:** Soul (10 entries per version, 3 versions)
**Method:** All versions use synthesis. They differ in how many input variants fed the synthesis (you don't know which is which).

## Rubric

Same as primary trial — score each entry independently:

- **3** — Stageable, specific, no input echo, no slop
- **2** — Acceptable but generic or partially echoing input
- **1** — Fails staging test, echoes input, or contains slop

## Version P

### P.1
She does not call it anything. The body moves to a place it does not usually occupy, stays there, then moves back. To the village it looks like a territorial quirk. She did it once for a human she had eaten — the human resurrected, walked past her, and waved. She moved back to her usual place without acknowledging the wave.

**Score: 1**

### P.2
She does not frame it as wanting. Every morning she does not leave is not a decision to stay — it is the absence of a reason to go. She has been absent of reasons for years. When the village discussed evacuation, she moved to block the only route large enough for her body and said she was too lazy to travel.

**Score: 1**

### P.3
She knows they think she is stupid — she decided the performance was worth more than the acknowledgment and never reconsidered. To anyone watching, she sunbathes through council meetings and forgets names she has been told twice. She has not misread a room in years. The act drops entirely around Vesper, who saw through it before the first year was out.

**Score: 1**

### P.4
She does not think about the positioning — it happens the way breathing happens, automatic, trained into decades of habit. She slumps, drapes, gestures loosely, routing humans closer to her face. She has never knocked over a market stall or crushed a cart. There is one human she has never positioned near her face — a woman who brings cloth samples and talks about weaving the way Kallya talks about the village. She has not decided what this exception means.

**Score: 2**

### P.5
She has told herself the story so many times it has the smoothness of something true — the tree, the climate, the effort of moving. Ask her what would make her leave and the answer comes slower, full of qualifications, and ends with a subject change. When the tree was damaged in a storm, she repaired it with an urgency that surprised her — not because she needed shelter, but because it was hers, and "hers" had started meaning more than property.

**Score: 1**

### P.6
She does not chase or grab. She suggests, invites, asks for help near her face. A human who has watched her bumble through three conversations will not think twice about climbing onto her palm. She does not think of it as hunting — she thinks of it as hospitality that goes one way. She has never used the method on a child, and would not recognize this as mercy if confronted.

**Score: 2**

### P.7
When something threatens Viralys, she stops performing and moves with a speed that makes the airhead act retroactively obvious. She does not think of it as protection — she thinks of it as pest control. After the last threat, a child brought her food as thanks. She ate the food, not the child, and said nothing. The child has been coming back for two months.

**Score: 2**

### P.8
She knows the excuses are getting worse — unsporting, inconvenient, not hungry. She does not examine why because the examination would end the system, and the system is how she eats and cares in the same body without choosing. She ate a man she liked — his particular way of laughing at nothing — and ate him anyway. He resurrected. She has not spoken to him since.

**Score: 1**

### P.9
She tracks the town the way a farmer tracks a herd — birth rates, sickness, who arrives, who stops showing up. The framework lets her eat a man she spoke to yesterday and check on his district tomorrow. She named what other giantesses flinch from naming, and it has no entry for Vesper — the one thing she chose before the system existed.

**Score: 1**

### P.10
She has rehearsed the deflection — if someone says the actual words, she will yawn, scratch her neck, ask about dinner. People who are not afraid do not plan escapes from hypothetical sentences. Vesper came close once, said something about how Kallya "chose this place." Kallya changed the subject so fast that Vesper stopped talking for the rest of the afternoon.

**Score: 1**

## Version Q

### Q.1
She arranges her life to avoid a single conversation: the one where someone says "you love them" and she cannot deflect with a joke. The fear is that she will agree, and once she agrees, the framework she uses to eat the ones she loves stops working.

**Score: 2**

### Q.2
In ordinary conversation she slumps, lets her tail drape, gestures loosely — all calculated to route humans closer to her face without appearing to try. She has never knocked over a market stall, never displaced a rope ladder, never crushed a cart by accident — a perfect safety record, except for the people who disappear.

**Score: 3**

### Q.3
Her operating code says humans are individually expendable. When that code meets a specific human whose suffering she recognizes, she makes an exception and explains it away as pragmatics. The excuses have a pattern she has not examined, and they get thinner each time.

**Score: 2**

### Q.4
She does not chase or grab. She suggests, invites, asks for help with something near her mouth. The method requires patience and a reputation for harmlessness — a human who has watched her bumble through three conversations will not think twice about climbing onto her palm. The setup sometimes takes days; she does not mind.

**Score: 3**

### Q.5
When a conversation turns serious around her, she yawns and asks something tangential — and later acts on exactly what was said. The performance works so well that humans who survive encounters genuinely believe she stumbled into predation by accident, and the people she likes underestimate her too.

**Score: 2**

### Q.6
When something threatens Viralys from outside, she stops performing and moves with a speed and precision that makes the airhead act retroactively obvious. As soon as the threat is handled, she is back on her rock, complaining about the sun angle. The contradiction costs her nothing as long as no one names it.

**Score: 1**

### Q.7
She has an explanation for every year she has spent in Viralys: the tree is comfortable, the climate suits her, moving would require effort. Each explanation is factually true and none of them are the reason. If she admitted the tree is not the reason, she would have to reconsider what the predation means when directed at a community she chose to join.

**Score: 2**

### Q.8
She refers to the human population the way a farmer discusses herd health — tracking who arrives, who sickens, who stops showing up. The framework lets her eat a man she spoke to yesterday and check on his district tomorrow. She named what other giantesses flinch from naming, and the naming is what makes them uncomfortable around her.

**Score: 2**

### Q.9
She wants to belong somewhere without having to change what she is. What drives most of her decisions — where she sleeps, which humans she talks to, how she responds to threats — is preserving the specific arrangement she has in Viralys. The arranging takes more effort than she would ever admit to.

**Score: 3**

### Q.10
She does not attend funerals or ask what happened. She moves to a part of the village she does not usually occupy, lies down, and stays there for a day or two. The grief costs her the belief that individual humans do not register — each time she moves she is proving herself wrong.

**Score: 1**

## Version R

### R.1
She does not chase or grab. She suggests, invites, asks for help with something near her face. The setup sometimes takes days. A human who has watched her fumble through three conversations will not think twice about climbing onto her palm — and the ones who help with the most harmless-sounding requests are the ones who stop appearing in town.

**Score: 3**

### R.2
When something threatens Viralys from outside, she stops performing and moves with a speed that makes the airhead act retroactively obvious. As soon as the threat is handled she is back on her rock, complaining about the sun angle. She eats the people she guards; she guards the village she treats as a resource. No one has named this to her face.

**Score: 2**

### R.3
She has an explanation for every year she has spent in Viralys: the tree is comfortable, the climate suits her, moving would require effort. Each explanation is factually true and none of them are the reason. Ask her what would make her leave and the answer comes slower, full of qualifications, and ends with a subject change.

**Score: 3**

### R.4
When a conversation turns serious she yawns, stretches, asks something tangential. She has not misread a room in years. The performance works so well that humans who survive encounters with her genuinely believe she stumbled into them by accident.

**Score: 2**

### R.5
Her operating code says humans are individually expendable. When a specific human's suffering registers — not a category but this person, this moment — she makes an exception and explains it away: unsporting, inconvenient, not worth the effort. The excuses get thinner each time, but as long as she has one, the code holds.

**Score: 3**

### R.6
In conversation she laughs easily, touches people with the tip of her tail, remembers jokes. Her body language is calculated: she slumps, drapes, gestures loosely, taking up space that routes humans closer to her face. She has never knocked over a market stall, never crushed a cart, never displaced a rope ladder by accident — a perfect safety record, except for the people who disappear.

**Score: 3**

### R.7
She wants to keep exactly what she has — this village, these people, this particular arrangement of tolerance. Not any place; this one. She arranges her life to maintain it with more effort than she would ever admit, and the word she is careful not to use is home.

**Score: 3**

### R.8
When a human she knows dies permanently, she does not attend the funeral or ask what happened. She moves to a part of the village she does not usually occupy and lies there for a day or two. To most of the village it looks like a territorial quirk. Vesper, who has watched it happen four times, recognizes it as mourning with the name filed off.

**Score: 2**

### R.9
She arranges her life to avoid a single conversation: the one where someone says plainly that she cares about these people as people, and she cannot deflect with a joke. The fear is not the accusation. The fear is that she will agree, and once she agrees, the framework she uses to eat the ones she loves stops working.

**Score: 2**

### R.10
She tracks the town's demographics the way a farmer discusses herd health — birth rates, sickness patterns, who arrived, who stopped showing up. She named what other giantesses avoid naming: individual humans are expendable, the community is not. The naming is what makes them uncomfortable around her.

**Score: 2**
