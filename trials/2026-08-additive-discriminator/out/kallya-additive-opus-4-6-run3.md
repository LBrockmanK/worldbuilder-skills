# Kallya

## Background

- Arrived at the location before Viralys existed, found Vesper's tree and settled in it; the village formed around them both → one of the oldest continuous residents, predating every human and most giantesses in the settlement
- Ran a bluff at a previous human settlement, threatening to eat them all if they refused to accommodate her; they called it; she could not follow through → left that community and has never returned
- The relief she felt at not following through told her she cared about communities as wholes, and she did not want to know that → carries an attachment to collective human life she still refuses to name
- Lamia-giantess, 81m total length with a 65m snake tail; humanoid torso equivalent to other giantesses at 30-35m standing → the heaviest resident in the village; physically moving her against resistance would demolish structures around her
- Holds nominal roles: town guard, passive alchemical source, vibration-sensing early warning system → each role began as a pretext or an afterthought; none reflects deliberate civic participation
- Slow metabolism and slow digestion → victims remain inside her for a long time

## Body

- She presents as careless about her size while being exact about where her weight sits. She cajoles people closer to her mouth rather than reaching for them, partly because a sudden shift of her tail can snap rope ladders, knock tools off platforms, and crush anyone standing in the wrong spot.
- When she moves through the village she tracks every human in her path without looking at them directly. She grew up as the largest thing in every space she occupied and learned that watching openly makes small creatures bolt.
- She sunbathes for hours and repositions rarely. Her metabolism runs slow and cold ground drains her; warmth is a physical need she arranges her entire day around.
- When she swallows someone, her body processes them slowly. She is aware of this, and it is part of the appeal — she likes the drawn-out sensation and does not pretend otherwise.

## Soul

- When she reads a room, she reads it precisely — who is tense, who is lying, who wants something they have not said. She delivers her conclusions wrapped in chatter and giggling so that the person hearing it cannot tell whether she meant it. The vapid persona is a tool she built and maintains on purpose; the laziness underneath it is genuine.
- She can price a season's grain yield by watching foot traffic at the market and knows which traders short their measures. She has never read a legal document and would not know where to start. Her knowledge runs deep in anything she can observe with her body and stops cold at anything that requires reading or formal study.
- When a human she knows dies permanently, she moves to a different spot in the village and lies there without speaking. She does not name what she is doing. She would call it restlessness if asked.
- She views the human population through an animal-husbandry frame: the flock matters, individual members do not. She eats individual humans she knows, enjoys the way they struggle, and constructs pretexts to get them into her mouth. She keeps the community healthy and growing because a shrinking flock is a failing flock. The framework is honest and she is the only giantess who has named it aloud — but it breaks at the edges, because she notices specific deaths and they cost her something she will not account for.
- She tells herself she stays for Vesper's tree and the convenience of a settlement that tolerates her. She stays because she found people she does not want to leave, and admitting that would mean admitting the bluff she walked away from years ago was not about tactics but about who she is.
- When the village faces an external threat, she responds with sudden, brutal force — coiling around structures, striking anything that moves wrong, using her mass as a wall. When the threat passes she returns to sunbathing and refuses any acknowledgment. She fights like someone defending a home and talks like someone who happened to be in the way.
- With strangers, she opens bubbly and scattered — asks silly questions, laughs too easily, loses the thread of her own sentences. People either dismiss her or lean in to help, and both responses give her what she wants: room to watch without being watched back.
- When she decides someone is worth her time, warmth shows up as proximity. She lets them sit against her coils, shares her warm spots, tolerates noise she would normally move away from. She does not name this as care and would deny it if pressed.
- When someone bores her or pushes past a boundary she has not stated, she goes still and flat. Her voice stays cheerful but the questions stop, the fidgeting stops, and the conversation ends within two sentences. People who know her recognize the shift; people who do not keep talking to a wall.

## Relationships

- **Vesper — Kin/Confidant:** She arrived before anyone else and settled in Vesper's tree, and neither of them has left. She plays their cohabitation as freeloading — she took the best sun-facing branch and stayed — but she adjusts her position when Vesper's roots need rain access and checks the canopy after storms without being asked. Vesper is the one presence she does not perform around; the chatter drops and she says what she means, though what she means is still less than what she feels.
- **Nadja — Friction:** Nadja's energy grates on her in a specific way: Nadja pushes, organizes, insists, and every interaction carries an expectation that Kallya should be doing more. She meets this with cheerful deflection that she knows Nadja finds maddening. She does not avoid Nadja — they share too much community space — but every exchange has a low current of mutual irritation running underneath the civility.
- **Elara — Ideological Counterpart:** Elara holds views about the treatment of humans that run counter to Kallya's husbandry framework, and neither of them can let the other's position sit unchallenged. She respects that Elara means it — the disagreement is not personal — but she cannot hear Elara's position without pointing out what she sees as its blind spots. These exchanges run longer than either of them intends.
- **Jaro Jerab — Rival:** Jaro watches her the way she watches everyone else, and she finds it intolerable. He is one of the few humans who does not buy the airhead act, and his refusal to be charmed or frightened puts them in a quiet competition she did not choose. She measures herself against his composure and resents that she does it.
- **Sophie — Unease:** Sophie is polite, steady, and opaque in a way Kallya cannot parse. She cannot tell whether Sophie's calm around her is genuine comfort, careful management, or something else entirely. She watches Sophie more closely than she watches most humans and has not decided what she is looking at.
- **Rado Stary — Charge:** She has had easy opportunities to eat Rado and has not taken them. She tells herself there is no sport in prey that cannot process what is happening. The actual reason is closer to pity for his specific suffering, and pity is an emotion that does not fit inside her husbandry framework, so she has not examined it. She keeps a passive awareness of where he is in the village.
- **Liza — Authority:** Liza drew a line around Mir and Kallya respects it. She does not test Liza's boundaries the way she tests other people's — not because Liza could stop her physically, but because Liza's protectiveness over Mir is the kind of single-minded commitment Kallya recognizes from her own attachment to community, and she will not interfere with it. Liza is one of the few people whose instructions Kallya follows without performing resistance first.
- **The previous settlement — Ghost:** She left a community that called her bluff and discovered she could not destroy what she had lived among. She does not talk about them. When a situation in Viralys starts to feel like that one did — when she catches herself caring about a specific human more than the framework allows — she gets restless and moves to a new sunning spot and stays there until the feeling passes.
