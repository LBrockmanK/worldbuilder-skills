# Nadja

## Background

- Grew up in Viralys, watched over by her mother until her mother judged her old enough to be on her own → left with no bitterness about being on her own early; her mother stayed longer than some giantess mothers do
- Maja's mother vanished when Maja was young and no one ever saw her leave → Nadja started looking out for Maja, a role that outgrew the two of them
- The village's daily needs kept adding to what looking after Maja had meant → now runs the kitchen that feeds both humans and giantesses, a job she never set out to take
- Viralys runs on resurrection for most deaths, but not all → treats any accident on her watch as one that might not come back from

## Body

- She scoops a stray human up in one hand without breaking her stride, the same motion whether she's pulling them off a hot stovetop or out of a giantess's path.
- When a human strays somewhere dangerous, she doesn't reach for the nearest open hand. She closes a fist around them or drops them into her apron pocket, because a loose grip is how someone gets dropped.
- She holds a hazard up at eye level and talks them down before she puts them back on the counter, and doesn't let go until they've stopped squirming.
- Her hands stay busy at counters built for people a tenth her size. She plates food in portions she checks against the size of the person eating it, never against her own.
- She swats Maja for stepping out of line the way she'd swat a peer, hard enough to sting, because Maja can take it.
- With Liza she scales the same discipline down to a hand across the backside or a flick of a finger, the way she'd correct a child.
- She works angled forward over the counters, weight tipped toward the kitchen floor, because her attention lives at counter height, not at the height of anything taller than her.

## Soul

- When the kitchen slips out of her control, she takes the task back herself instead of handing it to someone else, because trusting someone else to be careful enough has never worked out.
- She checks the same locked cabinet three times before she leaves for the night, because the one time she stops checking is the time something gets into it.
- After an accident, she adds two more rules to the rotation instead of loosening her grip, even when the accident happened because someone was too rushed to follow the rules already in place.
- She tells the kitchen staff her way of doing things is what any responsible person would do, while she's the one who takes over every task before anyone else gets a chance to prove they can handle it.
- She calls the extra hours and the double-checking nothing worth mentioning, while she's the only person in the kitchen who hasn't taken a full day off in longer than anyone can remember.
- With someone new in her kitchen, she hands them the smallest task first and watches how they handle it before she hands over anything bigger.
- When someone she cares about is having a good night, she doesn't say so. She sends over a second helping of whatever they mentioned liking last week, unasked.
- When someone questions how she runs the kitchen, she doesn't argue the point. She keeps doing it her way and waits for them to stop asking.
- She answers questions about her own day in short declaratives and moves straight to the next task, rarely finishing a sentence about herself.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin, Confidant:** Nadja swats her for stepping out of line the way she'd swat a peer, because Maja can take it and nobody else in her life can. She tells Maja things she trails off on with everyone else, half-finished worries she doesn't hand to anyone but her.
- **Liza — Charge:** Nadja disciplines her like a child and checks on her more than the job requires, because Ataraxia isn't around to do it and someone has to.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Nadja picks up the work Ataraxia leaves behind without saying anything about it directly, but she goes short and quiet with anyone who speaks well of Ataraxia, because the absence never gets acknowledged by the person leaving it.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Nadja tracks every delivery Melinda's supply chain owes the kitchen against every delivery the kitchen owes back, and calls in the debt the moment a shipment runs late, because a kitchen that can't count on its supply lines can't feed anyone.
- **Tomas — Authority:** Nadja hands him the smallest, safest tasks first and reassigns anything he gets wrong before he gets a second try at it, because she'd rather redo the work herself than watch him get hurt learning it.
- **Ossa — Rival:** When Ossa's storehouse runs leaner than Nadja's kitchen, Nadja works longer hours to close the gap instead of asking how she manages it, because losing the comparison would mean she isn't as needed as she thinks she is.
- **Corrin — Unease:** Nadja can't tell if he's competent and undersupervised or careless and lucky so far, and she keeps finding reasons to walk past his station instead of asking him outright, because putting the question to him would mean trusting his answer.
- **Elder Wren — Ideological Counterpart:** When Wren argues the humans should run more of their own safety checks, Nadja doesn't concede the point even when she can't refute it, because letting go of the checks means finding out what happens when she isn't the one holding them.
