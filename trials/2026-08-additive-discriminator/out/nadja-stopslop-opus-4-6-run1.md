# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once Nadja reached independence → Nadja accepted this as ordinary; giantesses leave earlier than humans expect
- Maja's mother disappeared when Maja was young; Nadja took on a big-sister role for the younger giantess → the caretaking habit started here, with one specific kid who had no one
- The village kitchen needed a forewoman; Nadja's caretaking scaled from Maja outward into the role → she did not choose the job so much as accumulate it, one responsibility at a time
- Runs the kitchen feeding both the human and giantess populations of Viralys → her workload is constant, self-imposed, and larger than any one person should carry
- Ataraxia, Liza's parent, is absent from daily village life → Nadja appointed herself responsible for Liza without being asked

## Body

- She moves through the kitchen at a pace that keeps smaller bodies out of her path before they have to dodge. She learned the spacing after years of working around humans and treats it as her job, not theirs.
- When she handles humans physically, she pins them under a finger, puts them on a shelf, or tucks them into a pocket. The containment is firm and careful. She handles them the way someone handles something precious and breakable, and she does not notice the condescension in that.
- Her hands stay busy. When she talks, she is portioning, stirring, stacking. Stillness means something has gone wrong or she is about to say something she has been holding back.
- When she disciplines Maja, she hits her. Maja is a giantess and can take it. When she disciplines Liza, she uses a measured hand appropriate to a child. The calibration is automatic; she has sorted everyone into a durability category and acts accordingly.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen, she adds a rule or takes over the task herself. She does this because the last time she let someone else handle a dangerous job, the result confirmed what she already believed: no one else is careful enough. The pattern tightens every time it fires.
- She works past the point where fatigue makes her sloppy, then makes the kind of mistake she built the rules to prevent. She does not connect the overwork to the error. She connects the error to insufficient oversight and works harder.
- When someone she has been managing shuts down or pulls away, guilt hits her as a physical restlessness. She responds by cooking for them, staying late, doing more. She does not ask what went wrong. She serves her way back toward proximity.
- She sees her level of involvement as normal and thinks others should step up more. She does not see that her constant intervention is what prevents them from developing the capacity she wants them to have. She is solving a problem she maintains.
- When she catches herself enjoying the noise of people laughing over a meal, she redirects to a task. She gives lighter punishments for small infractions than she believes she does. If anyone pointed this out, she would deny it.
- When she meets someone new, she assesses what they need before she learns what they want. She asks whether they have eaten. She watches how they move around the kitchen and decides within minutes how much supervision they require.
- When she trusts someone enough to let them work unsupervised, it shows as silence. She stops checking. She does not say anything about it, and if asked, she would call it minding her own business.
- When someone questions her authority or pushes back on a rule, she repeats the instruction once and does not explain it again. If they push further, she does the task herself. She treats the challenge as proof they are not ready.
- She presents herself as practical and unmoved. She is practical. She is not unmoved. The distance between those two facts is visible to anyone watching closely: she remembers what each person likes to eat, she notices when someone is off, she adjusts portions without being asked. She calls this efficiency.
- She treats humans as people she is fond of, feeds, and protects individually. She does not treat them as equals. Humans are small and fragile and need looking after. This is so obvious to her that it operates below the level of opinion.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin, Confidant:** Maja is the person Nadja started all of this for. She still sees the kid who had no one, even though Maja grew up. When Maja's energy pulls a room sideways, Nadja watches it happen with an expression she would call disapproval and everyone else would call pride. Maja gets more of Nadja's real reactions than anyone: sharper corrections, louder arguments, and the only version of gentleness Nadja does not dress up as professional duty.
- **Liza — Charge:** Nadja watches Liza the way she would watch a pot about to boil over. Ataraxia is not around to do it, so Nadja took the job without asking or being asked. She holds Liza to standards, checks her meals, and disciplines her when she needs it. She has not examined whether Liza wants this arrangement.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Nadja does Ataraxia's job for her and resents that the job exists. She does not confront Ataraxia about the absence. She folds the resentment into extra work and lets it come out as clipped answers when Ataraxia's name comes up.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Melinda runs the supply chain Nadja depends on. Nadja respects the discipline Melinda brings to her side of the operation and holds herself to the same standard in return. When Melinda's deliveries are late or short, Nadja does not complain to Melinda's face. She adjusts, covers the gap, and keeps a quiet count.
- **Nadja's mother — Ghost:** Her mother left when Nadja was old enough. Nadja accepted this without bitterness, but her mother's standard for "old enough" became Nadja's own. She measures readiness in others by it without realizing the yardstick came from somewhere.
- **The kitchen crew (humans) — Charge:** She knows each of them by name, by what they eat, by how they move around hot surfaces. She positions herself between them and anything at giantess scale. She calls this doing her job. The crew calls it something else when she is not in earshot.
- **The village elders — Authority:** She answers to the village's organizational structure and follows its decisions about resource allocation. When those decisions conflict with what she believes the kitchen needs, she complies and then quietly adjusts her own operation to compensate. She does not argue in meetings. She argues with pots afterward.
- **Sorrel — Rival:** Sorrel is the only other giantess in the village who could run the kitchen. Nadja knows this and monitors Sorrel's suggestions with a vigilance she would call quality control. When Sorrel proposes a change to the meal rotation, Nadja finds a reason it will not work. She has not lost this position and does not intend to.
