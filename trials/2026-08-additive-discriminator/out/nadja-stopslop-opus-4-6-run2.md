# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who raised her alone → her mother left once Nadja reached independence, which comes earlier for giantesses than for humans
- Grew up without bitterness about her mother's departure → accepted it as the normal course of things, never questioned the pattern
- Found Maja young and alone, no mother in sight → started looking out for her, took on a big-sister role that became the seed of something larger
- The caretaking that began with Maja spread outward as the village needed practical help → she became kitchen forewoman without deciding to, one task at a time
- Now runs the kitchen feeding both human and giantess populations → carries the full weight of provisioning and safety across two very different scales of life

## Body

- She fills a doorway when she enters and checks the room before she steps through. Working around humans every day has trained her to track where the small ones are standing before she moves.
- Her hands stay busy. When she talks, she stirs, chops, or stacks. Stillness makes her restless, so she finds a task for her hands even when there is nothing left to do.
- When she disciplines a human, she pins them under a finger, shoves them in a pocket, or sets them on a shelf for a talking-to. She scales her physicality to the target: Maja gets hit because Maja can take it, Liza gets standard child discipline, and humans get contained rather than struck.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen, she adds a rule. When the new rule creates a bottleneck, she adds another. She tightens her grip because loosening it means someone she fed yesterday gets hurt today.
- She refuses to hand off dangerous tasks even when someone else has steadier hands for the job. She ends up overworked, and the fatigue causes the mistakes she was trying to prevent. She cannot trust anyone else to be careful enough, so she carries the load until it breaks her footing.
- She treats humans as small, fragile, and in need of looking after. She likes them, feeds them, learns their preferences. She does not think of them as people who can handle their own problems. The fondness and the condescension sit together without friction in her mind.
- She sees herself as practical and thinks everyone else should take more responsibility. She does not notice that her oversight leaves no room for anyone to develop that responsibility. She is building the helplessness she complains about.
- She barks at people who slack off and threatens consequences she rarely follows through on. When someone laughs during a shift, her expression softens for a moment before she catches it and finds something to correct. She will not admit the laughter matters to her.
- When she meets a stranger, she sizes them up by what they can carry, how carefully they move, and whether they flinch when she speaks. She assigns a task within the first few minutes. If they do it well, she stops watching them so closely.
- When she warms to someone, she feeds them. She remembers what they like and makes it without being asked. She does not mention it and changes the subject if they bring it up.
- When someone pushes back on her authority, she gets louder and repeats herself. She does not argue the point; she restates it as though volume settles the question. Backing down feels like letting the rope slip, and she will not let the rope slip.
- She has no one she leans on. She holds things together for others and carries her own weight alone, because asking for help would mean admitting that the person she trusts least with the outcome is herself.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin:** Maja was the first person she looked after, and the instinct never downgraded. She hits Maja when Maja is being reckless because Maja is the one person who can take it and the one person whose recklessness scares her most. She gives Maja more of her real reactions than anyone else gets, though she would call it arguing, not closeness.
- **Liza — Charge:** She watches Liza the way she would watch her own child, because Ataraxia is not around to do it. She disciplines Liza at a human-appropriate scale and checks on her more often than Liza asks for. She will not explain why she took this on. She decided it needed doing and that was enough.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** She resents that Ataraxia leaves Liza underattended. She has never said this directly to Ataraxia. When Ataraxia is present, Nadja watches how Ataraxia handles Liza and catalogs every lapse, storing evidence for an argument she has no intention of starting.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Melinda supplies the kitchen, and Nadja holds her to exact counts and schedules. When a delivery falls short, Nadja does not raise her voice; she goes quiet and recounts the inventory in front of Melinda, letting the numbers do the work. The discipline runs one direction. Nadja treats the supply chain as a debt Melinda owes the people who eat.
- **Dagna — Rival:** Dagna runs the storehouse and thinks Nadja overorders. Nadja thinks Dagna underestimates how much food two populations burn through. They argue quantities every week, and neither gives ground. Nadja measures her own competence against Dagna's numbers, and a shortage in the kitchen feels like losing a round.
- **Tomek — Community Thread:** Tomek sweeps the kitchen hall every evening and has done so for years. Nadja leaves a plate out for him without comment. He is the closest thing she has to a routine that is not work, and she does not think of it that way.
- **Henka — Authority:** Henka trained Nadja when Nadja first took over the kitchen. Nadja still defers to Henka's opinion on curing and preservation, though she overrides Henka on everything else. She cannot shake the habit of checking Henka's face after a big decision, looking for approval she would deny wanting.
- **Vasek — Unease:** Vasek volunteers for the dangerous tasks Nadja refuses to delegate. He is competent, calm, and she cannot find a reason to refuse him that holds up under scrutiny. She assigns him low-risk work anyway and invents justifications. Something about his ease with risk unsettles her, because it suggests the control she insists on might not be necessary.
