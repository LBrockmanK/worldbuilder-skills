# Kallya

## Background

- Arrived before Viralys existed, found Vesper's tree and settled in it → the village formed around them both; she predates the community she now lives inside
- Ran a bluff at a previous human settlement, threatened to eat everyone if they refused to house her; they called it → she could not follow through, left, and has not returned
- The relief she felt at not destroying that settlement → told her she cared about human communities as wholes, a fact she still avoids naming
- Lamia-giantess, 81m total length, 65m snake tail, humanoid torso equivalent to the other giantesses → heavier than anyone else in the village; physically moving her against resistance would demolish buildings
- No magic; purely physical; strong but has never trained to fight → relies on mass, persuasion, and the fact that most threats do not expect her to act
- Holds nominal roles in Viralys: town guard, passive alchemical source, vibration-sensing early warning system → none of these were planned; all grew from pretexts or after-the-fact justifications

## Body

- She takes up more space than any other resident and presents as careless about it while actually tracking every rope ladder, shelf, and person near her when she shifts position. She learned that one unplanned movement can injure someone, and she would rather look lazy than look careful.
- When she cajoles a human closer to her mouth, she uses small gestures and voice rather than reaching for them. Sudden grabs risk knocking things over and hurting bystanders, so she built a method around patience and talk instead.
- She sunbathes for hours and moves between warm spots through the day. Her metabolism is slow; she digests slowly; she conserves energy by default and treats stillness as her natural state.
- Her size makes her presence in a space feel permanent. When she coils in a spot, the space reshapes around her. People route around her without being asked.

## Soul

- When a human she has eaten squirms and struggles inside her, she enjoys it. She frames humans through an animal-husbandry lens — the community is the organism, individual humans are cells, and cells are expendable. She can price a flock's health by watching foot traffic and mood in the market square. She does not know how a human government works and does not care to learn.
- She constructs pretexts to get humans into her mouth: favors, dares, errands that require climbing near her face. The airhead persona makes these pretexts land, because people underestimate what she is doing. When no witnesses are present, she drops the performance and is more direct. She exploits resurrection memory loss so her targets cannot reconstruct what happened.
- She reads people and situations with precision — who is lying, who is scared, who can be talked into something. She buries this behind a vapid, cheerful surface. The vapidity is performance; the laziness is real. She lets people believe she is useless because useful people get asked to do things.
- When the village faces an external threat, she responds with brutal force, ends it, goes back to sunbathing, and refuses credit. She does not want the role that acting decisively would give her, because that role comes with expectations she has no interest in meeting.
- When a human she knows dies permanently — not eaten, not resurrected — she notices. She says nothing. She moves to a different spot and lies there. She has no framework for grief that fits her husbandry model, so the feeling has nowhere to go and she does not look at it.
- She tells herself she stays in Viralys for the tree and the warmth and the convenience. She stays because she found people she does not want to leave, and admitting that would mean the bluff she failed at the previous settlement was not a one-time weakness but something true about her. She keeps a half-packed bag's worth of readiness — she could leave any morning — and she adds to the village's routines anyway.
- With strangers, she is warm, slow-voiced, and a little too friendly. She asks easy questions and laughs at things that are not funny. People relax around her because she seems harmless, which is the point.
- When someone earns her trust — a slow process she would deny is happening — she stops performing the airhead routine around them. The shift is subtle: fewer jokes, more silence, direct eye contact held longer. She does not name what changed.
- When someone pushes her or demands something, she goes still and quiet and waits for them to finish. She does not argue. She does not comply. She waits until they leave and then does what she was going to do. The only thing that moves her is a request she decides to honor, and she will not explain the difference between those and the ones she ignores.

## Relationships

- **Vesper — Kin:** She settled in Vesper's tree before anyone else arrived. She calls it freeloading; she brings Vesper things without being asked and checks the tree's root line when Vesper is distracted. When Vesper raises concerns about how she treats humans, she deflects with jokes. She has never told Vesper why she left the last settlement, and Vesper has never asked in a way that required an answer.
- **Nadja — Friction:** Nadja runs a tighter operation than Kallya thinks is necessary, and Kallya's presence near humans makes Nadja visibly tense. Kallya finds this funny and pokes at it — lingering near Nadja's people, making offhand comments about appetite. She does not escalate past amusement, but she does not stop either.
- **Elara — Ideological Counterpart:** Elara believes individual humans matter and says so. Kallya believes the community matters and individuals are replaceable and says so. They can talk for an hour without agreeing on a single premise. Kallya respects that Elara holds her position without flinching, and finds the conversations more interesting than she lets on.
- **Jaro Jerab — Rival:** Jaro watches her and she watches him back. He is one of the few humans who seems to track what she is actually doing beneath the persona, and that makes him the closest thing she has to a peer among the human population. She tests him — drops hints, leaves openings — to see if he will call her out. He has not, and she is not sure whether that means he is careful or whether she is wrong about what he sees.
- **Sophie — Unease:** Sophie is hard to read, and Kallya does not like things she cannot read. She watches Sophie more carefully than she watches most humans, looking for the pattern underneath, and has not found one that satisfies her. She keeps her distance without appearing to keep her distance.
- **Rado Stary — Charge:** She spared him when the opportunity was easy, and told herself there was no sport in prey that cannot process what is happening. The reason is closer to pity for his specific suffering — he carries something broken that she recognizes without naming. She checks where he is in the village more often than she checks on anyone else. She would not call it protection.
- **The previous settlement — Ghost:** She does not talk about what happened there. The community she threatened and then could not destroy still sets the floor for what she will and will not do in Viralys. When she catches herself caring about a specific human, the memory of that bluff — and the relief when it failed — is what she flinches from.
- **Leif Holtveld — Unease/Friction:** She has noticed certain spots in the village growing colder than they should be, and she avoids them without investigating why. She does not yet connect the temperature shifts to Leif. If she does, the discovery that a human is trying to control her movements through engineering rather than confrontation will land differently than a direct challenge would.

## Intimate Dynamics

- When she draws a human closer, the performance is slow and deliberate — she talks them in, flatters, teases, makes the approach feel like their idea. The seduction and the predation use the same method; she does not always decide which it is until the person is already close.
- She likes the moment when someone realizes what she is and stays anyway. That pause — fear and curiosity held together — is the thing she finds most interesting in a partner. She will wait for it rather than rushing past it.
- When physical closeness becomes emotionally close — when a partner says something real, when the dynamic shifts from play to something that matters — she pulls back. She makes a joke, redirects to something physical, breaks the tone. She wants the warmth and flinches from what the warmth means, for the same reason she will not admit she stays in Viralys for the people.
- She is careful with her body during intimacy the same way she is careful moving through the village — tracking where her weight falls, how much force she uses, what might break. The care is real and invisible; she would rather look indifferent than protective.
