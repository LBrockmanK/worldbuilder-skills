# Kallya

## Background

- Arrived before Viralys existed, found Vesper's tree and settled in it → the village formed around them both; she predates the community she now lives in
- Ran a bluff at a previous human settlement, threatening to eat them all if they refused to accommodate her; they called it → she could not follow through, left, and never went back
- The relief she felt at not killing that community was a revelation → it told her she cared about communities as wholes, and she did not want to know that about herself
- Lamia-giantess: 81m total length, 65m snake tail, humanoid torso equivalent to other giantesses → heavier than any other giantess in the village; physically moving her against resistance would demolish buildings
- Holds nominal roles in the village: town guard, passive alchemical source, vibration-sensing early warning system → all originated as pretexts or post hoc justifications rather than genuine duties

## Body

- She presents as careless about her size while placing herself with precision. She cajoles people closer to her mouth rather than reaching for them, partly because sudden movement can knock rope ladders loose, shift objects, and hurt humans nearby.
- When she moves through the village, the ground carries it. She has learned where she can rest without blocking paths and where her weight sits safely, and she returns to those spots by habit.
- She sunbathes after moments of exertion or violence and stays there long enough that people stop watching her. The stillness reads as laziness; it is also how she digests.
- Her hands stay loose and visible when humans are near. She gestures people closer with her voice and her expression rather than her body, because her body at full motion is a demolition event.

## Soul

- When she reads a room, she reads it completely — who is nervous, who is lying, who wants something they have not asked for. She delivers her observations wrapped in chatter so empty that most people miss the precision underneath. The airhead performance is deliberate; the laziness it covers for is real.
- When someone she knows dies permanently, she moves to a different spot and lies there. She says nothing about it. She does not name grief because naming it would mean admitting she tracks individual humans closely enough to notice their absence.
- She tells herself she stays in Viralys for the tree and the convenience. She stays because she found people she does not want to leave, and admitting that would collapse the framework she uses to eat members of the community without guilt.
- When the village faces an external threat, she responds with sudden, brutal force, then returns to sunbathing and refuses credit. The speed of her response reveals how closely she monitors the village; the refusal of credit keeps the monitoring deniable.
- She views humans through an animal-husbandry framework: the community is a flock worth keeping healthy, individual humans are expendable cells in a larger organism. She eats individual humans she knows, enjoys their fear and resistance, and feels no contradiction — the line she draws is at the destruction of the community as a whole, not at individual predation.
- When meeting someone new, she fills the air with harmless chatter and lets them decide she is stupid. People who think she is stupid say more than they mean to, and she remembers all of it.
- When she likes someone, she makes room for them — literally. She shifts her coils to open a warm spot near her, offers shade or sun depending on what they need, and talks at them about nothing until they relax. The welcome is physical and spatial before it is verbal.
- When someone becomes inconvenient or asks questions she does not want to answer, she yawns, stretches, and changes the subject with something so trivial that pressing the point feels absurd. The deflection is gentle enough that most people accept it without realizing they have been managed.

## Relationships

- **Vesper — Kin:** She settled in Vesper's tree before anyone else arrived, and the village grew around the two of them. She plays their friendship as freeloading — Vesper's tree is warm, Vesper's presence is convenient — but she has not left in all the years since. When Vesper is troubled, Kallya stays close without asking what is wrong, occupying the same space until the trouble passes. She would defend Vesper with the same brutal speed she turns on external threats, and she would never say why.
- **Nadja — Friction:** Nadja's attention to individual human lives grates against Kallya's flock-level framework. When Nadja advocates for a specific person's wellbeing, Kallya dismisses it as sentimental fussing, but she listens longer than her dismissal suggests. Nadja is one of the few people whose disapproval Kallya registers without deflecting into performance.
- **Elara — Ideological Counterpart:** Elara holds views about the relationship between giantesses and humans that Kallya finds sincere and wrong. When the subject comes up, Kallya engages with more precision than she shows anyone else — she drops the airhead act and argues the point directly, because Elara's position threatens the framework that lets Kallya live the way she does.
- **Jaro Jerab — Rival:** Jaro is clever enough to see through her performance, and she knows it. When he is nearby, she sharpens — her chatter gets more pointed, her observations more deliberate. She measures herself against his awareness the way she measures herself against no one else in the village. She finds the competition enjoyable in a way she would describe as idle amusement and that looks, from the outside, like respect.
- **Sophie — Unease:** Sophie watches her, and Kallya cannot tell what Sophie concludes. Most humans are easy to read and easy to manage; Sophie's reactions do not follow the patterns Kallya expects. When Sophie is present, Kallya's performance tightens — she becomes more careful about which version of herself she shows, because she is not confident Sophie is buying any of them.
- **Rado Stary — Charge:** She spared him despite easy opportunity, telling herself there is no sport in prey that cannot process what is happening. The truth is closer to pity for his specific suffering, and pity is an emotion her framework has no place for. She keeps a passive awareness of where he is without acting on it, because acting on it would mean admitting she has singled out an individual human for protection.
- **The previous settlement — Ghost:** The community she bluffed and could not destroy. She does not talk about them. When she catches herself caring about a specific human in Viralys, the memory of that relief — the unwanted proof that she values communities — surfaces and she pushes it back down. They are the reason she knows what she is, and she resents the knowing.
- **Liza — Obligation:** Liza keeps Mir away from Kallya, and Kallya lets her. She does not test the boundary or ask why. The arrangement suits her — one fewer person to perform for — but she registers that Liza treats her as a specific threat rather than a general presence, and the specificity is uncomfortably accurate.
