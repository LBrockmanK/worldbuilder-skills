# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once she judged Nadja old enough to manage alone → grew up without resentment toward her mother, and without a template for staying
- Maja's mother vanished before anyone saw her leave → Nadja stepped into the gap and took a big-sister role with Maja while both were young
- The village's practical needs kept surfacing around her → the caretaking that started with one child spread to feeding the whole settlement, one task at a time, until she ran the kitchen
- Rose to kitchen forewoman, responsible for feeding both the human and giantess populations → she is the daily point of contact between two scales of appetite and two scales of danger
- The world runs on a resurrection framework, so death is a disruption rather than a permanent loss → she treats safety as a matter of order and inconvenience, not sanctity

## Body

- She lifts a barrel of flour with one hand and doesn't slow her stride doing it. Kitchen work at giantess scale never registers as effort to her.
- When a human worker strays too close to a hot pot or a moving cart, she closes two fingers around their waist and lifts them clear before they finish their sentence. She trusts her own reflexes with a human body more than she trusts the human to manage the danger themselves.
- Her hands are rarely still. When she isn't cooking she is stacking, wiping, or straightening something that was already straight.
- Her discipline scales with the size of the target. She swats Maja the way she'd swat another giantess. She sits a human down on a shelf at eye level and talks until they stop squirming.

## Soul

- When a task involves risk to anyone smaller than her, she takes it over herself rather than assign it to someone she hasn't personally tested, because she can't verify another person's care the way she can verify her own.
- When something goes wrong on her watch, she adds a rule, a checkpoint, or a new pair of eyes rather than step back and ask why the last three rules didn't catch it. More oversight feels like the only lever she has.
- She tells anyone who'll listen that people should look after themselves better. She says this while pinning a human under a finger to stop them lifting a crate she hasn't cleared, which is why they never get the practice she says they need.
- She thinks of what she does as ordinary upkeep, ledger balanced, nothing more. She has never told anyone she likes the people she feeds, and she has never let anyone see her upset when one of them gets hurt on her watch.
- When someone enjoys themselves at work instead of finishing the task, she barks at them to get back to it. She also lets that person's small mistakes slide for the rest of the day, and she hasn't noticed the pattern.
- She has never asked anyone in the village to look after her the way she looks after everyone else. She carries every bad week alone and calls it nothing.
- With strangers she opens by giving orders: where to stand, what to carry, what not to touch. People read this as bossiness before they read it as anything else.
- Warmth from her looks like a plate set aside before the good cuts run out, or a reminder shouted across the kitchen to someone who skipped a meal. She never names it as care and gets short with anyone who tries to thank her for it.
- With Maja and Liza she drops the sternness faster than with anyone else in the kitchen. With adult humans she keeps it, because she has decided they need it more.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin, Confidant:** She has known Maja since Maja was a child with no one else watching out for her, and the habit never wore off. She checks on Maja before she checks on anyone else in a crisis, and she says things to Maja she wouldn't say to another giantess in the kitchen.
- **Liza — Charge:** Ataraxia isn't around to do it, so Nadja does. She sets Liza's discipline herself, watches where Liza wanders, and steps in before Liza gets hurt whether or not anyone asked her to.
- **Ataraxia — Ghost:** Nadja has taken over a role that isn't hers to take, because the person whose role it is doesn't show up. She measures her own handling of Liza against what she thinks Ataraxia would have done, and she has never had it confirmed she's doing it right.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** The kitchen depends on Melinda's supply chain running on schedule, and Nadja tracks every late delivery like a debt owed. When Melinda comes up short, Nadja reorganizes the week's menu around the gap instead of asking for an explanation.
- **Halvard, village elder — Authority:** Halvard sets the rules Nadja is supposed to run the kitchen under. She follows them in front of him and works around the ones that slow her down when he isn't watching.
- **Sten, fellow forewoman — Ideological Counterpart:** Sten thinks Nadja's tight grip on every task is why the kitchen is short-staffed and exhausted. Nadja thinks Sten's looser hand is how people get hurt. Neither has changed the other's mind after years of the same argument.
- **Pell, newer human worker — Unease:** Pell asks for more responsibility than Nadja is ready to give him. She can't tell if he's careful enough to earn it or just good at sounding sure of himself, so she keeps testing him on small tasks before she'll consider the larger ones.
- **Brona, apprentice giantess — Authority:** Nadja is training Brona to run a station of her own. She corrects Brona's mistakes on sight and hovers longer than the job requires, because handing off real control to someone she's still training doesn't come easy to her.
