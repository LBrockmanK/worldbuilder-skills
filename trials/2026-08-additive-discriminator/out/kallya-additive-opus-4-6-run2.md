# Kallya

## Background

- Arrived before Viralys existed, found Vesper's tree and settled in it; the village formed around them both → one of the oldest continuous residents, predating every human alive there
- Ran a bluff at a previous human settlement, implying she would eat them all if they refused her; they called it; she could not follow through → left that place and has not returned
- The relief she felt at not killing that community was the revelation that she cared about communities as wholes → she did not want to know that about herself and still resists admitting it
- Lamia-giantess, 81m total length, 65m snake tail, humanoid torso equivalent to other giantesses at 30–35m standing → heavier than any other giantess in the village; physically moving her against resistance would demolish buildings
- Holds nominal roles: town guard, passive alchemical source, vibration-sensing early warning system → the guard and alchemical roles grew out of pretexts; the vibration sensing is real but she treats it as incidental
- No magic; purely physical; strong but has never trained to fight → her effectiveness in threats comes from size and weight, not skill

## Body

- She takes up more space than anyone in the village and presents as careless about it while actually being precise. She adjusts her coils before shifting, checks clearance before turning, and cajoles people closer to her mouth rather than reaching for them — because sudden movement displaces rope ladders, objects, and bystanders, and she has learned what her weight does to structures.
- When she suns herself she goes still for hours, coiled in a broad open spot, eyes half-closed. Her metabolism is slow and she runs cold; warmth is a physical need, and she arranges her day around where the sun falls.
- She moves her mouth more than her hands. When she wants a human closer she talks them in — leaning down, voice easy, offering a reason to step forward. The hands stay where they are. People who have watched her long enough notice that the stillness is part of the technique.
- When the village faces an external threat she moves fast and hits hard, using her mass to block passages and crush what needs crushing. Afterward she returns to her spot and lies back down as though nothing happened.

## Soul

- When someone asks what she is doing in Viralys, she says the tree is comfortable and the sun is good. She has a half-packed explanation for every role she fills — guard duty fell to her because she was already lying in the road, alchemical sourcing started as a way to get humans close. She keeps the reasons shallow because the real one — that she found people she does not want to leave — is something she has not said out loud and does not plan to.
- When she talks to humans she performs a loose, slightly vacant friendliness — asks easy questions, laughs at things that are only half-funny, lets silences sit until the other person fills them. The performance reads as an airhead who ended up in town and never left. Underneath it she is tracking the conversation precisely: who is nervous, who is lying, who can be guided somewhere without noticing.
- When a human she has known dies permanently — not the temporary deaths the resurrection system handles, but gone — she says nothing about it. She moves to a different spot in the village and lies there for a while. She does not name what she is doing and would deny it if asked.
- She can price a season's worth of alchemical materials and knows which traders are inflating scarcity. She can read a crowd's mood from the pitch of background conversation. She has no idea how Viralys's civil administration works and has never asked; when paperwork appears she ignores it until someone else handles it.
- She eats individual humans from the village — this is real predation, not ritual, and she enjoys the physicality of it: the resistance, the struggle, the slow digestion. She constructs pretexts to get them into her mouth willingly, using her airhead persona to make the approach feel harmless. She exploits the resurrection system's memory loss so that victims do not carry the experience forward. The community as a whole she preserves; individual members she treats as expendable, the way a farmer treats livestock. She has named this framework to herself and it is the most honest version of what several giantesses in the village practice without admitting.
- She presents as the village's most useless resident — freeloading off Vesper's tree, contributing nothing, sunbathing through crises. She spans more cross-population relationships than most residents and acts as a structural node in the community's social web. She would deny this if confronted with it, and the denial would be genuine — she does not see herself as someone who holds things together.
- When someone pushes her to take credit for something she did — a threat handled, a problem quietly solved — she deflects with a joke or a shrug and changes the subject. Accepting credit would mean accepting that she is invested, and she keeps a running story that she is here for the sun and the tree and nothing else.
- With strangers she is immediately warm in a way that feels low-stakes — easy smile, idle questions, no apparent agenda. The warmth is real and the agenda is hidden. She decides within the first exchange whether this person is useful, interesting, or neither, and the friendliness continues unchanged regardless — the only thing that changes is what she does with the information.
- When someone she has decided to care about is in trouble, she acts before being asked and without announcing it. She puts herself between the threat and the person, or removes the threat, and then returns to whatever she was doing. If thanked, she acts confused about what they are referring to.
- When she dislikes someone she does not confront them or avoid them. She stays close, stays friendly, and waits. The patience is not strategic; she genuinely does not feel urgency about interpersonal conflict. Weeks later the other person finds their situation has shifted in ways that are hard to trace back to her.

## Relationships

- **Vesper — Kin:** She settled in Vesper's tree before the village existed and has been there since. She calls it freeloading; she brings kills to the tree's root system and adjusts her coils around structural weak points she has memorized. When Vesper's decisions about the village come up, Kallya offers opinions disguised as idle observations — "that road is getting muddy" means something specific — and does not push when Vesper ignores them. She has never told Vesper she stays for her. She keeps a half-packed bag under her bed and adds to Vesper's tree-care routine anyway.
- **Nadja — Friction:** Nadja keeps the village running with a clerk's precision and expects cooperation from every resident. Kallya ignores administrative requests, misses meetings, and responds to direct questions with tangents. When Nadja presses harder, Kallya smiles wider and gives less. She finds Nadja's need for order funny in a way she would not explain if asked — it reminds her of farmers she knew at her previous settlement, counting heads every morning.
- **Elara — Ideological Counterpart:** Elara believes giantesses should protect humans from harm, including from other giantesses. Kallya eats humans from the village and considers this compatible with caring about the community. When Elara raises the subject, Kallya does not argue the ethics — she asks Elara how many humans she has watched die of old age, and whether that bothered her less because she did not cause it. The question is genuine. She respects Elara's position and has no intention of changing her own.
- **Jaro Jerab — Rival:** Jaro watches the village and catalogs what he sees; Kallya watches the village and acts on what she sees. She finds him interesting because he pays attention at a level most humans do not, and irritating because his attention occasionally lands on her patterns. When he is nearby she performs the airhead act harder than usual, not because she thinks he is fooled but because maintaining it is the game. She would be disappointed if he stopped watching.
- **Sophie — Unease:** Sophie runs the village's human-side coordination and interacts with Kallya through official channels. Kallya cannot read Sophie's actual position on the giantesses clearly — Sophie is polite, competent, and gives nothing away about what she thinks of a lamia who eats townspeople. Kallya watches Sophie more carefully than she watches most humans. She has not decided whether Sophie is someone who has made peace with the arrangement or someone keeping a record.
- **Rado Stary — Charge:** Rado has a condition that makes him unable to process what is happening around him in the way other humans can. Kallya has had easy opportunities to take him and has not. She tells herself there is no sport in prey that cannot understand the game. The actual reason sits closer to pity for his specific situation — he suffers in a way she recognizes as pointless, and adding to it gains her nothing. She checks where he is when she moves through the village and adjusts her path if he is nearby.
- **Liza — Obligation:** Liza keeps Mir away from Kallya, and Kallya knows it. This is one of the few contexts where another giantess has drawn a line with her and she has respected it without testing it. She owes Liza nothing formal, but the restraint functions like a debt — Liza asked without asking, and Kallya agreed without agreeing. She is careful around Liza in a way she is not careful around the others.
- **Previous settlement — Ghost:** The community she bluffed and failed to destroy. She does not talk about it. When a situation in Viralys starts to echo that one — a confrontation where someone calls her bluff, a moment where she has to decide what she is willing to do — she goes quiet and pulls back before anyone notices the shift. The relief she felt at not following through told her something about herself she has spent years avoiding, and it still moves under the surface when the conditions rhyme.

## Intimate Dynamics

- When she is interested in someone she draws them in the same way she draws prey — slow conversation, easy warmth, reasons to come closer. The line between predatory and intimate blurs in her approach because the skills are the same: read what the other person wants, offer a version of it, let them close the distance. She knows the similarity and it does not bother her.
- When a partner shows genuine vulnerability with her — not performed, not strategic — she slows down and goes quiet in a way that is different from her usual stillness. She holds the moment carefully, almost frozen, because she does not trust herself to respond to real feeling without exposing how much she is capable of caring. The vulnerability scares her more than the intimacy does.
- She wraps her coils around a partner incrementally, adding weight and pressure in stages, checking the response at each one. The physical control matters to her — she needs to feel the other person's reactions through her body. If they tense in a way that reads as genuine distress rather than excitement, she loosens before they ask.
- When an encounter becomes emotionally real — when the other person says something that lands, when the closeness stops being a game — she pulls back to banter or physicality. She can stay in the intimate space longer each time, but she retreats from the emotional one on reflex. The pattern is the same one she runs everywhere: care expressed through action, denied in words.
