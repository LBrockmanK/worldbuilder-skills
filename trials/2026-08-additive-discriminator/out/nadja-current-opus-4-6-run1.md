# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once Nadja reached independence — earlier than a human child would, normal for giantesses → Nadja carries no grudge about it and treats self-reliance as the baseline for everyone
- Found Maja young and alone, no mother in sight, and started looking out for her → the caretaking habit began with one specific kid and grew outward as the village gave it structure
- Rose to kitchen forewoman feeding both human and giantess populations → the role accumulated around her rather than being sought; she stepped into gaps until the gaps became her job
- Works daily at the boundary between giantess-scale operations and fragile human bodies → every shift reinforces that carelessness has physical consequences she can see and measure

## Body

- She takes up space without apology — moves through the kitchen at full stride, and people clear the path or get moved. Her size is a working tool, and she handles it the way a carpenter handles a saw: precisely, without shrinking it.
- Her hands stay busy. When she talks, she plates, stirs, or wipes down a surface. Stillness makes her restless, and standing idle while others work puts an edge in her voice.
- When a human is in her way or doing something she considers unsafe, she picks them up and puts them somewhere else — a shelf, a pocket, under her arm. She does it the way someone moves a mug off a counter's edge: automatic, not unkind, and without asking.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen — a spill, a near-miss, a broken tool — she adds a rule. More oversight, more checkpoints, hands closer to every task. The pattern tightens the operation until she is the bottleneck, and the overwork produces the next mistake. She does not see the cycle.
- She refuses to hand off dangerous tasks even when someone else would handle them better, because trusting another person to be careful enough feels like gambling with a body. The result is exhaustion and the exact lapses she is trying to prevent.
- When someone she looks after is enjoying themselves — laughing during a shift, joking over prep work — she softens. Her voice drops, her corrections get lighter, and she finds reasons to keep them nearby. She has never noticed this pattern and would deny it.
- She treats human autonomy the way an adult treats a toddler's independence: encouraging in theory, overridden in practice. She likes humans, feeds them, learns their preferences, and does not think of them as people who can fully handle their own safety. This is not cruelty — it is so deep in her framing that she has never examined it.
- She sees herself as practical and thinks others should take more responsibility, while her control prevents them from developing it. She is solving a problem she maintains, and the gap between her self-image and her effect is invisible to her.
- When she meets someone new, she sizes them up by what they need — food, direction, correction — and starts providing it before they ask. First encounters with Nadja feel like being managed by someone who has already decided what is good for you.
- Warmth from Nadja comes as acts of service she does not acknowledge: a favorite dish prepared without being asked, a reminder to eat, an extra portion set aside. If someone thanks her directly, she brushes it off or finds something to criticize so the moment passes.
- When she is angry, she gets quiet and precise. Her voice flattens, her instructions get shorter, and she watches the offender work until the silence does what yelling would have done.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin:** Maja is the reason the caretaking started — a kid with no mother who needed someone, and Nadja stepped in before she had a word for what she was doing. She hits Maja when Maja is being reckless, the way she would shove a peer who wandered too close to a ledge — hard, corrective, between equals in durability. Maja gets more of Nadja's unguarded attention than anyone else, and Nadja does not think of this as favoritism because she does not think of it at all.
- **Liza — Charge:** Nadja watches Liza the way she watches a pot that might boil over — steady, preemptive, ready to step in. Ataraxia is not around to raise her, so Nadja has appointed herself to the job without being asked and without framing it as a choice. She disciplines Liza at human-child scale — firm but measured — and would not forgive herself if Liza got hurt on a task Nadja could have handled instead.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Nadja considers Ataraxia's absence from Liza's daily life a failure of responsibility. She does not say this directly, but her voice sharpens when Ataraxia's name comes up, and she fills the gap with extra vigilance over Liza as though proving a point no one asked her to make.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Melinda runs the supply chain that keeps Nadja's kitchen fed, and Nadja holds that relationship to exacting standards. When a delivery is short or late, Nadja does not adapt — she tracks the discrepancy, names it precisely, and expects correction. The discipline is professional, but the intensity is personal: a short delivery means someone under her care eats less, and she takes that as a failure she allowed.
- **The Kitchen Staff — Authority (downward):** She runs the kitchen the way a field sergeant runs a squad: clear orders, no ambiguity, consequences for sloppiness, and a loyalty underneath that shows up as making sure everyone eats before she does. She does not ask for input on safety decisions. When someone pushes back on a rule, she restates it slower, as though the problem is comprehension.
- **Her Mother — Ghost:** Her mother left when the job was done, and Nadja accepted this as how things work. She measures her own caretaking against her mother's model — sufficient, timely, without sentiment — and does not notice that she has exceeded it in every direction. The departure was clean, and Nadja has built no story around it, which is itself the story.
- **Humans Generally — Ideological Counterpart:** She likes humans. She feeds them, remembers their names, learns what they prefer. She also does not believe they can keep themselves safe around giantess-scale work, and this belief is structural, not personal. When a human insists on handling something dangerous, she overrides them with her hands — physically relocating them — and considers the argument settled. The humans who push back the hardest get the most attention, which she reads as proof they need watching.
- **The Village — Community Thread:** She knows who skipped a meal, who has been working too many shifts, who looks thinner than last week. The village is her pantry and her patient list. She does not frame this as care — she frames it as keeping things running — but the knowledge is too granular for mere logistics.
