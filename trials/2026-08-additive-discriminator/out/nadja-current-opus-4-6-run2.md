# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once Nadja reached independence, earlier than a human child would -> Nadja accepted the departure without bitterness; it was normal for her kind
- Found Maja young and alone, no mother in sight, and started looking out for her -> the caretaking habit began with one specific child and spread outward as village needs gave it structure
- Rose to kitchen forewoman feeding both human and giantess populations -> the role accumulated around her rather than being sought

## Body

- She takes up space without apology and moves through the kitchen at full stride, expecting others to clear the path. Smaller workers learned early that she does not slow down.
- When a human worker makes a mistake near something dangerous, she reaches in and moves them bodily — pinned under a finger, lifted onto a shelf, shoved into a pocket — before addressing the problem. The correction comes after the containment.
- Her hands stay busy even in conversation: wiping a surface, adjusting a pot, checking a temperature. Stillness looks wrong on her.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen, she adds rules. More oversight, more hands-on checks, more tasks she handles personally. The pattern tightens the operation around her and leaves less room for anyone else to learn, which produces the next mistake she will respond to with more rules.
- She treats humans as fragile by default. She likes them, feeds them, learns their names and preferences — and does not think of them as people who can handle serious responsibility. She adjusts portions, lowers shelves, and removes sharp tools from reach without being asked, the way someone childproofs a room.
- She refuses to hand off dangerous work even when someone else has better technique, because she cannot believe anyone else will be careful enough. The result is she works longer hours than anyone on her staff and occasionally makes errors from fatigue — the exact failures she reorganized to prevent.
- When she catches someone enjoying themselves at work — laughing over a shared task, humming while they chop — she barks at them to focus. Her punishments for the resulting small infractions land lighter than she intends. She does not notice the pattern.
- She presents herself as purely practical: she feeds people because the work needs doing. The warmth underneath — preparing a worker's favorite dish without being asked, making sure someone who skipped a meal gets a plate — comes out as acts of service she never draws attention to. If someone pointed it out, she would dismiss it.
- With strangers, she sizes them up fast: how much trouble will this person cause, and can they follow instructions. She assigns tasks within minutes of meeting someone, treating the interaction as an intake interview whether or not that is what it is.
- When someone earns her trust through steady, competent work, she starts feeding them better. The portions get larger, the dishes get more considered. She says nothing about the change and would deny it if asked.
- When pushed back on, she gets louder and more specific — listing every time the other approach failed, every incident that justifies her way. She does not raise her voice to intimidate; she raises it because being questioned on safety feels like being asked to accept a future accident.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin:** Maja is the person Nadja started caring for before she cared for anyone else, and she still treats Maja differently than the rest of the kitchen. Maja is durable — a giantess peer — so Nadja's physical discipline lands directly: a smack, a shove, contact she would never use on a human. She tells Maja more than she tells anyone else, not through deliberate confession but through proximity and habit. Maja has been around long enough to hear the unguarded version.
- **Liza — Charge:** Nadja watches Liza the way she watches a pot that might boil over. Ataraxia is not around to raise the girl, so Nadja stepped into the gap without being asked and without framing it as a choice. She disciplines Liza at a human-child scale — firm but measured — and keeps track of where Liza is and what she is doing with an attention that goes well past professional interest.
- **Ataraxia — Ghost:** Ataraxia's absence is the reason Nadja watches Liza. She does not talk about Ataraxia often, but her standard for how Liza should be raised comes from somewhere, and she measures herself against it. When Liza does something reckless, Nadja's frustration carries a layer meant for someone who is not in the room.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Melinda handles the supply chain that feeds Nadja's kitchen. Nadja holds Melinda to exact standards on delivery timing and quality, and the discipline runs one direction — Nadja tracks shortfalls, names them, and follows up. She does not extend Melinda the slack she gives her own kitchen staff, because a supply failure cascades into the kind of disruption Nadja cannot fix by working harder herself.
- **The kitchen staff — Authority (downward):** She runs the kitchen the way a sergeant runs a platoon: clear orders, no ambiguity, physical intervention when someone is about to get hurt. She knows every worker's name, dietary needs, and weak points. The protection is real and so is the control — she has not considered that they might be the same problem.
- **Her mother — Ghost:** Her mother left at the right time by giantess standards, and Nadja accepted it. But the model her mother set — watch until they can manage, then go — is the one Nadja cannot apply to anyone else. She stayed, and staying became the kitchen, and the kitchen became everything.
- **Human workers (as a class) — Charge:** She feeds them, contains them when they are in danger, and arranges the workspace to minimize what they can break or what can break them. She cares about individual humans specifically and genuinely — and still does not think of them as adults in the way she thinks of another giantess as an adult.
- **Senior kitchen staff — Friction:** The workers who have been around longest push back on her newer rules and tighter oversight. She hears the pushback, overrides it, and then checks their work more closely for the next few days. She is aware this makes them resentful and considers the resentment a fair trade for fewer accidents.

## Intimate Dynamics

- When someone gets close physically, she stays task-oriented — adjusts their collar, brushes flour off their shoulder, checks whether they have eaten. Touch comes through caretaking because that is the only channel she has left open.
- When someone tries to take care of her, she deflects with work: there is always a next task, a delivery to check, a station to clean. She does not refuse care outright — she redirects until the moment passes.
- If someone persists past the deflection and sits with her anyway, she gets quiet. She does not send them away. She does not know what to do with attention that asks nothing of her, and the stillness makes her restless in a way she cannot fix by doing something useful.
- She is drawn to competence. Someone who handles a dangerous task well — steady hands, no wasted motion, no need for her to intervene — holds her attention longer than she would admit. The attraction is tangled with relief: here is someone she does not have to protect.
