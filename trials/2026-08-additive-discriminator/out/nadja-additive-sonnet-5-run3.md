# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once she judged Nadja old enough to manage alone, sooner than a human mother would leave a child but later than some giantess mothers wait → the parting carried no bitterness on either side
- Maja's mother disappeared before Nadja or anyone else in the village saw her go → Nadja started looking out for Maja as a girl who needed someone, with no plan beyond that one child
- The village kept handing her more people and more tasks once she'd proven she'd handle them → the looking-after spread past Maja to the whole village's daily needs without her setting out to make that her job
- Now the kitchen forewoman, cooking and rationing for the village's human and giantess populations under one roof → answers for every plate that leaves it
- Liza's guardian, Ataraxia, is often away with no one else assigned to Liza's day-to-day care → Nadja took the job on herself without being asked
- Current situation: has not taken a full day off from the kitchen in longer than she can name → she no longer registers the missing rest as something worth mentioning

## Body

- She works a kitchen built to giantess scale and crosses it without checking her own footing, because she has worked this room since before she finished growing into it.
- When a human strays too close to a hot pan or an open ledge, she closes two fingers around them before they finish the thought, sets them down somewhere safe, and goes back to stirring without missing a beat.
- She carries a human in one palm the way someone else carries a bowl, barely adjusting her grip, never looking down to check she's got them right.
- Her hands stay busy through nearly every conversation — chopping, wiping, restacking — and standing still with empty hands makes her restless, so she avoids doing it.
- Her handwriting doesn't fit paper cut to human scale, so she calls the storeroom counts out loud for someone else to write down rather than write them herself.
- When a giantess her own size crowds a doorway she's working in, she holds her ground and makes them go around her. When a human does the same thing, she steps aside without a word.

## Soul

- When a task carries real risk — a blade, hot oil, a ledge — she takes it on herself rather than hand it to someone just as capable, because the one time it goes wrong on someone else's watch is a time she can't take back.
- She refuses to hand off the most dangerous kitchen jobs even to cooks who'd do them better, then works those shifts on top of her own. The tiredness from carrying both makes her careless in exactly the ways she's trying to prevent, and she takes on the next dangerous job anyway.
- She tells people they should take more responsibility for themselves, then steps in before they get the chance to prove they can. She doesn't connect the two — to her, stepping in is just what the moment needed.
- She insists the kitchen would fall apart without her checking every plate before it leaves, and hands off nothing that could prove her wrong. The one thing that shakes this, briefly, is watching someone she's protecting stop trying because she never let them.
- With humans, she checks whether they've eaten, pulls them off anything giantess-scale, and answers questions put to them before they get the chance to answer for themselves. A person small enough to break reads to her as a person who needs someone bigger managing the room.
- When something goes wrong on her watch, she adds a rule, takes back a task she'd handed off, or starts checking a station herself, rather than asking what she could stop doing.
- She can stretch a short grain delivery to feed both populations without anyone going hungry, and knows which giantess spices will poison a human portion. She has never learned to keep the ledger in a hand small enough for human paper, and hands that job to someone else without embarrassment.
- Grain and firewood run on separate schedules for the two populations she feeds, and the count never balances for long — short on grain one week, short on firewood for the small ovens the next — and neither problem waits for anyone to raise it before she's already working it in her head.
- She holds the kitchen together through every argument, bad night, and near-miss without asking anyone to carry it with her, and rarely admits something's wrong until it's already fixed.
- She has not decided whether to train anyone to run a shift without her. Some weeks she starts showing someone the ledger. Most weeks she finds a reason it isn't the week.
- When someone new starts in her kitchen, she runs them through the safety rules before she asks their name — she'll get to know them once she knows they won't get hurt.
- She shows she likes someone by remembering what they favor eating and setting it in front of them without saying why. Asked outright whether she likes someone, she shrugs the question off.
- When a giantess her own size questions how she runs the kitchen, she plants her feet and repeats herself louder instead of reconsidering. When a human pushes back the same way, she goes quiet and does it her way regardless.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin, Confidant:** Maja's mother disappeared before anyone in the village saw her go, and Nadja started looking out for her because no one else stepped in first. She still corrects Maja hands-on — a shove, an open-hand smack — the way she'd correct anyone durable enough to take it, where a human gets held instead of hit. Maja is the one person she'll admit a bad day to, and even then only once, only if Maja asks twice.
- **Liza — Charge:** Ataraxia isn't around to see to Liza day to day, so Nadja took the job on without anyone assigning it to her. She disciplines Liza the way she'd discipline any child — sent to a corner, told to sit, nothing scaled up for size — and decides her meals and bedtime without waiting to be asked.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Nadja covers whatever Ataraxia doesn't show up to cover and doesn't raise it once Ataraxia's back. She goes short and quiet whenever Liza's name comes up between them, then changes the subject to the kitchen before it turns into an argument.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Nadja checks every delivery against the order twice before she'll sign for it, because a short shipment means someone eats less that week. When a shipment comes up short, she doesn't take it up with Melinda directly — she cuts giantess portions first and says nothing until the next order.
- **Her mother — Ghost:** When Nadja decides someone is old enough to be left to manage alone, she measures the decision against how long her own mother waited, and pulls back if it comes sooner than that.
- **Yselde — Rival:** Yselde runs the hall kitchen on the other side of the village, and Nadja keeps a running count of whose stores hold out longer each season without meaning to have started counting. When Yselde changes how a shared delivery gets split, Nadja redoes the split her own way before the next meal goes out.
- **Corin — Authority:** Corin is new to the line, and Nadja decides what he's allowed near before he's touched a knife. She pins him under a finger or sets him on a shelf until he's heard a correction, then puts him back to work like nothing happened, going lighter on him than her own rules call for without noticing she does it.
- **Vashti — Unease:** Vashti oversees the grain stores and never gives Nadja a straight answer about what's coming in next season. Nadja can't tell if the vagueness is caution or something being kept from her, and orders extra against both possibilities without saying why.
