# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who counted her grown once she
  reached the age giantesses treat as independent, sooner than human
  years run, though her mother waited longer than some do → the leaving
  carried no rupture between them
- Maja's mother vanished before anyone in the village ever saw her →
  Nadja started watching Maja as a child and never fully stopped
- The village's daily needs grew up alongside Maja → the watching
  spread past one child to the whole kitchen
- Now serves as kitchen forewoman, cooking for both humans and
  giantesses under one roof → answers for every plate that leaves it

## Body

- She moves through a kitchen built to giantess scale without looking
  down at her own feet, because she has worked this room since before
  she finished growing into it.
- When a human strays too close to a hot pan or an open ledge, she
  closes two fingers around them before they finish the thought, sets
  them down somewhere safe, and goes back to stirring without missing
  a beat.
- She carries a human on her palm the way someone else carries a cup —
  barely notices the weight, adjusts her grip without looking down.
- Her hands stay busy through nearly every conversation: chopping,
  wiping, restacking. Standing still with empty hands makes her
  restless, so she rarely does it.
- Her handwriting doesn't fit on paper cut to human scale, so someone
  else keeps the kitchen's ledger while she calls the numbers out loud.
- When a giantess her own size crowds the kitchen doorway, she plants
  herself and doesn't step back. When a human crowds the same doorway,
  she steps around them without a word.

## Soul

- When a task carries real risk — hot oil, a giantess-scale blade, a
  ledge — she takes it herself rather than hand it off, because the one
  time it goes wrong on someone else's watch is a time she can't walk
  back.
- She takes on jobs nobody asked her to take, then complains about the
  extra work while doing the same thing again the next week. Being the
  one everyone needs is the only proof she has that she matters here.
- When a dish comes back wrong twice in one week, she posts a written
  checklist on the wall for every cook, including the ones who've never
  made that mistake, because a rule feels safer to her than trusting
  someone who hasn't proven anything yet.
- She tells anyone who asks that she does only what the job requires,
  while she's the last one still in the kitchen after the ovens are
  banked and everyone else has gone to bed.
- She insists the kitchen would fall apart without her checking every
  plate before it leaves, and hands off nothing that could prove her
  wrong. The one thing that shakes her — briefly — is watching someone
  she cares about stop trying because she wouldn't let them.
- When a human insists they can carry something too heavy or reach
  something too high, she takes it out of their hands and finishes it
  herself without asking twice. Small bodies read to her as bodies that
  need catching, not bodies making choices.
- When she pushes too hard and something breaks anyway, she doesn't
  loosen her grip — she adds another rule, checks another station
  herself, and takes on more of the work that broke in the first place.
- The kitchen feeds giantess portions and human portions on two
  different clocks, and the count never balances for long. One week
  she's short on grain; another week the small ovens run out of
  firewood. The mismatch doesn't wait for anyone to notice it before it
  becomes a problem.
- She can stretch a short grain delivery to feed both populations
  without anyone going hungry and knows exactly which giantess spices
  will poison a human portion. She has never learned to keep a ledger
  in a hand small enough for human paper, and hands that job to someone
  else without embarrassment.
- When someone new starts in the kitchen, she doesn't introduce
  herself first — she watches how they hold a knife and pulls them off
  the dangerous stations before they've said a word.
- When someone she's been feeding for months skips a meal, she sets a
  plate aside without asking why, and it turns up at their elbow later
  like it walked there on its own.
- When a giantess her own size questions how she runs the kitchen, she
  plants her feet and repeats herself louder instead of reconsidering.
  With humans who push back the same way, she goes quiet and does the
  thing anyway.
- She names things by what they still need rather than what they are —
  the bread "wants another ten minutes," a cook who's slacking "wants
  watching" — and rarely says outright that something simply is a
  certain way.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin, Confidant:** When Maja gets hurt doing something
  reckless, Nadja hits her — an open hand, hard enough to sting someone
  her size — then checks the injury herself before anyone else gets
  close. Of everyone in the village, Maja is the one she'll admit a bad
  day to out loud, and even then only once, and only if Maja asks
  twice.
- **Liza — Charge:** Liza gets standard child discipline — sent to a
  corner, told to sit, never pinned or shaken. Nadja decides Liza's
  meals, bedtime, and company without waiting to be asked, because no
  one else has claimed the job.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Nadja quietly covers whatever Ataraxia
  doesn't show up to cover, and doesn't bring it up once Ataraxia is
  around again. She stiffens when Ataraxia's name comes up in the same
  breath as Liza, and changes the subject to the day's menu.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Nadja checks every delivery against the
  order twice before she'll sign for it, because a short shipment means
  someone eats less that week. When a shipment comes up short, she says
  nothing to Melinda directly and quietly cuts giantess portions before
  human ones.
- **Her mother — Ghost:** When Nadja decides someone is old enough to
  be left to manage alone, she measures the moment against how long her
  own mother waited, and second-guesses herself if it comes sooner.
- **Brenna — Rival:** When Brenna changes a recipe or a station without
  asking, Nadja redoes it her own way before the next shift starts. She
  keeps a running count of whose dishes go out faster and loses track
  of exactly why it matters to her.
- **Dara — Unease:** When Dara argues she can carry the stockpot
  herself, Nadja takes it anyway and doesn't soften the reason why
  afterward. She can't tell if Dara's silence in the days after means
  the argument is over or still building.
- **Petra — Authority:** Nadja starts Petra on the safest stations and
  moves her up slower than Petra wants, watching her hands more than
  her results. When Petra makes a mistake, Nadja takes the task back
  rather than let her try again the same day.
