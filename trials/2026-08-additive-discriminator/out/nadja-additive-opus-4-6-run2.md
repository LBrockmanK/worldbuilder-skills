# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once Nadja reached independence early by human standards → raised herself from that point, no bitterness about it, treats self-sufficiency as the natural order
- Found Maja young and alone, no mother in sight → started looking out for her, which became the first thread of a caretaking pattern that grew wider
- The village kitchen needed someone to organize meals across two populations of very different size → Nadja filled the gap one task at a time until the role was hers by default
- Now kitchen forewoman, feeding both humans and giantesses daily → the job defines her schedule, her standing, and her sense of purpose

## Body

- She takes up space without apology. She moves through the kitchen at full stride and expects others to clear the path, because slowing down means food is late and someone goes hungry.
- Her hands stay busy even in conversation — wiping a counter, adjusting a pot, sorting grain. Stillness makes her restless, and idle hands mean something is being missed.
- When she disciplines humans physically, she contains rather than strikes: a finger pinning someone to the table, a hand scooping them onto a shelf, a pocket used as a holding cell. The gestures are firm and practiced, scaled to avoid harm while making the point unmissable.
- When she disciplines Maja, she hits — open-handed, without hesitation. Maja is durable, a peer in scale, and Nadja treats her body accordingly.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen — a spill near the ovens, a human underfoot at the wrong moment — she takes over the task herself, even if someone else could handle it. She has seen what happens when she trusts someone else to be careful enough, and the memory sits closer than the present moment. She ends up overworked, and the fatigue causes the kind of mistakes she stepped in to prevent.
- She keeps a running count of what could go wrong at any given moment: the pot that is too close to the edge, the human standing where a door swings, the delivery that is a day late. She can price a sack of grain by weight and smell and knows which suppliers short their measures. She cannot read a legal document and does not try — she brings those to someone else without a second thought.
- When someone pushes back on one of her rules, she adds a second rule. The pushback confirms that people need more structure, not less. She has never once responded to a challenge by loosening a restriction, because loosening feels like choosing to let someone get hurt.
- She sees herself as the only responsible person in a room full of people who would burn the place down without her. She believes this is a plain fact. She does not notice that her refusal to let anyone handle risk means no one around her learns to handle risk, which means she is right — but only because she made it true.
- She tells herself the caretaking is duty and the kitchen is a job. She does not notice that she prepares favorite dishes for specific people, that she tracks who has eaten and who has skipped a meal, that her voice drops half a register when she talks to someone who looks tired. The warmth is constant and visible to everyone except her.
- When her kitchen is running well and people are laughing over a meal, she finds a reason to stay nearby — checking a shelf, wiping something already clean. She leaves if anyone points out she is watching. She cannot explain why she stayed.
- With strangers, she opens with a question about whether they have eaten. If they say yes, she checks whether they are lying. The first impression is a woman who has already decided you need looking after and will not ask whether you agree.
- When she warms to someone, it looks like food arriving without being asked for, a blanket left where they will find it, a sharp word when they are doing something she considers reckless. She does not say she cares. She feeds people and yells at them and assumes the meaning is clear.
- When someone disappoints her or breaks a rule she considers important, she goes quiet. She does not explain what went wrong. She handles whatever they were doing herself, and the silence lasts until she decides it is over. There is no appeal process.
- She speaks in short, direct sentences. Orders come without please or thank you. When she is worried, the sentences get shorter. When she is angry, she gets loud. When she is hurt, she gets quiet — and the quiet is worse.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin:** Maja is the first person Nadja looked after and the only one she treats as a peer in durability. She hits Maja when Maja is being reckless, yells at her across the kitchen, and sits with her at the end of a long day without needing to talk. Maja gets more of Nadja's unguarded behavior than anyone else — the closest thing Nadja has to someone she does not perform competence for. She still checks whether Maja has eaten, even though Maja is grown.
- **Liza — Charge:** Liza is Ataraxia's responsibility, but Ataraxia is not around enough to exercise it. Nadja stepped into the gap without being asked and disciplines Liza the way she would discipline a giantess child — firmly, with the expectation of obedience, and with a tenderness she buries under the firmness. She worries about what Liza is learning from the lack of consistent authority and compensates by being more consistent herself.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Nadja considers Ataraxia careless with responsibilities that matter — specifically Liza's upbringing. She does not say this directly to Ataraxia. She picks up the slack and lets the resentment sit. When Ataraxia is present, Nadja is polite and clipped, and the temperature in the room drops.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Melinda supplies the kitchen, and the supply chain runs on Nadja's standards. Nadja inspects every delivery and sends back what falls short. The relationship works because both sides treat it as a transaction with clear terms. Nadja respects Melinda's reliability and would not say so — saying it might invite a day when the standard slips.
- **Her mother — Ghost:** Her mother left when Nadja was ready, by giantess custom. Nadja does not resent this. She measures her own readiness against the age her mother judged sufficient, and she applies the same standard to every young giantess she encounters: old enough means old enough, and hovering past that point is weakness. The standard she holds others to is the one her mother held her to — she does not notice that she violates it constantly by hovering over everyone around her.
- **The kitchen crew (human) — Authority (downward):** She runs the human kitchen staff the way a foreman runs a crew: clear orders, no negotiation during service, punishment for carelessness. She pins rule-breakers under a finger or deposits them on a high shelf to think about what they did. She genuinely likes most of them. She does not think of them as people who could run the kitchen without her, because she has never let them try.
- **The kitchen crew (giantess) — Authority (downward):** She holds the giantess staff to a different standard — higher expectations for spatial awareness around humans, harsher words for mistakes that could have crushed someone. She expects them to understand what she understands: that the size difference is a permanent hazard and carelessness is a choice.
- **The village at large — Community Thread:** People in the village know Nadja as the person who feeds them. She knows most of them by name, by diet, by habit. She tracks who is losing weight, who is not showing up for meals, who is eating alone when they used to eat with others. She acts on this information without telling anyone she noticed.

## Intimate Dynamics

- When someone shows interest, she deflects with work — there is always a task that needs doing, a delivery to check, a pot about to boil over. She does not say no. She redirects until the moment passes, because sitting still with someone's attention on her and nothing to do with her hands is a situation she has no practice in.
- If someone gets past the deflection and she finds herself alone with them in a quiet moment, she softens in ways she would deny — her voice drops, she stops scanning the room, her hands go still. She does not know what to do with the stillness and fills it by finding something to fix or clean. The tenderness is real and she retreats from it the moment she notices it.
- She needs to feel useful to feel wanted. Desire that is purely about her — not what she provides, not what she does, but her — makes her uncomfortable in a way she cannot name. She has built her entire sense of worth around what she does for others, and someone wanting her apart from that has no place in the structure she lives in.
- When physical closeness becomes emotionally close, she pulls rank or picks a fight. She starts a disagreement about something small — the way they stacked the dishes, whether the fire is too high — because conflict is familiar ground and tenderness is not.
