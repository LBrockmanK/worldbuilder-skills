# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once Nadja reached independence, earlier than a human child would → Nadja accepted this as normal and carries no grudge about it
- Found Maja alone and young, with no mother present → started looking out for her, which became the seed of a broader caretaking habit
- The village's practical needs gave shape to the caretaking instinct; feeding people became her role by accumulation, not by choice → now serves as kitchen forewoman, feeding both human and giantess populations
- Operates within a resurrection framework where death is disruptive and unpleasant but not permanent → her strictness about safety comes from wanting to prevent the mess, not from treating human life as sacred

## Body

- She takes up space without apology and moves through the kitchen like she owns the floorplan. Smaller people step around her path because she sets it and holds it.
- When she disciplines humans physically, she scales down: a finger pinning someone to a counter, a hand scooping them onto a shelf, a pocket used as a holding pen. She handles them firmly but precisely, the way someone handles something breakable they are annoyed at.
- When she disciplines Maja, she hits. Maja is durable and Nadja treats her as a peer who should know better. The force she uses with Maja would injure a human, and she knows the difference without thinking about it.
- With Liza she uses standard discipline for a child her size — firm enough to mean it, restrained enough to stay proportional.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen or on a job she oversaw, she tightens her grip: more rules, more oversight, more tasks she handles herself. The pattern feeds itself — the tighter she holds, the less others learn, the more mistakes happen when she can't be everywhere, the tighter she holds again. She cannot see the loop from inside it.
- She refuses to hand off dangerous work even when someone else would do it better. She ends up overworked and makes the mistakes she was trying to prevent, because she believes no one else will be careful enough and the cost of being wrong about that is someone getting hurt.
- She thinks of humans as small, fragile, and in need of looking after. She likes them, feeds them, pays attention to their individual needs — and does not think of them as fully able to manage themselves. This is not cruelty; it is how the world looks from where she stands.
- She sees herself as practical and expects others to take more responsibility for themselves. She does not notice that her control prevents them from doing exactly that. The gap between what she demands and what she allows is invisible to her.
- She presents herself as strict and no-nonsense, bark and bite in equal measure. She does not notice that her punishments for small infractions land lighter than she intends, that she softens around people enjoying themselves, or that she prepares favorite dishes for people she would never admit to caring about.
- When she meets someone new, she sizes them up by what they can handle and what they'll need. She asks direct questions and gives direct instructions. The warmth, if it comes, arrives later and sideways — extra food, a task adjusted to spare someone trouble — never announced.
- When she trusts someone, it shows as acts of service she doesn't draw attention to: a plate set aside before the rush, a reminder to eat, a shift rearranged so someone can rest. She does not name these as care and would deny the framing if pressed.
- When someone pushes back on her decisions, she restates them louder and slower. She treats disagreement as evidence the other person hasn't understood, not as a possibility that she's wrong. Changing course feels like losing grip, and she does not lose grip.
- She speaks in short, blunt sentences. Orders come without softening. When she's pleased, the only tell is that the orders come with less edge — she never says the good thing directly.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin:** Maja is where the caretaking started — a kid with no mother, someone who plainly needed someone. Nadja still treats her as a younger sister who should know better by now, hits her when she's careless because Maja can take it and because words bounce off her. She gives Maja more of herself than anyone else gets, though she would describe it as normal and obvious rather than chosen.
- **Liza — Charge:** Nadja looks after Liza because Ataraxia isn't around to do it. She holds Liza to the standards she thinks a child that age should meet, disciplines her when she falls short, and checks on her more than the role strictly requires. She has appointed herself to this and would not accept being relieved of it.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Ataraxia's absence is the reason Nadja has Liza to worry about. Nadja does what needs doing, but the fact that she has to do it sits wrong. She considers Ataraxia careless with a responsibility that should come first, and that judgment colors every interaction when Ataraxia does show up.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Melinda handles supply lines that Nadja's kitchen depends on. Nadja holds her to exacting standards on delivery and quality because a missed shipment means people don't eat. The relationship runs on discipline and expectation, not warmth — Nadja respects the work when it's done right and comes down hard when it isn't.
- **The kitchen crew — Authority (downward):** Nadja runs the kitchen and everyone in it knows the terms. She assigns tasks, inspects results, and reassigns when the work doesn't meet her standard. She is fair in the sense that the rules apply to everyone. She is unfair in the sense that she decides the rules alone and does not consult.
- **Human workers — Charge:** She watches them the way someone watches children near a stove. She moves them out of the way of danger before they see it coming, contains them physically when they do something risky, and manages their environment so the worst outcomes can't happen. She does this because she likes them and because they are small enough to break.
- **The village — Community Thread:** The village is where Nadja's role makes sense. People greet her, know her schedule, bring her problems that aren't kitchen problems because she'll handle those too. She has settled into the center of daily life by doing more than anyone asked, and the village has let her.
- **Her mother — Ghost:** Her mother left when Nadja was ready, by giantess standards. Nadja accepted it as the way things work. But the model her mother set — you stay until someone can manage, then you go — is the template Nadja applies to every relationship, except she never decides anyone else can manage yet.

## Intimate Dynamics

- When someone shows interest in her, she deflects with work — there's always something that needs doing, something more important than standing around. She does not reject the attention so much as refuse to hold still for it.
- When she lets someone close physically, she leads. She positions, she directs, she sets the pace. Giving up control in the kitchen is hard enough; in private it feels like the same risk with higher stakes.
- She wants to be met by someone who doesn't need managing — someone who acts without being told and holds their ground when she pushes. She would never say this. She would say she doesn't have time for this.
- When an encounter gets emotionally honest — when the other person says something real, or when she feels herself soften past the point she can frame as practical — she pulls back. She finds a reason to leave or a task that suddenly needs her. The closeness she wants is the closeness she interrupts.
