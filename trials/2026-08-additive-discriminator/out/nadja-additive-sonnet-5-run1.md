# Nadja

## Background

- Grew up in Viralys, raised by her giantess mother until her mother judged her old enough to be alone — later than some giantess mothers wait, sooner than a human child would be left → she carries no resentment about being left.
- Maja's mother vanished before Nadja ever saw her; no one in the village did → Nadja started looking out for Maja as a girl who needed someone, with no plan beyond that one child.
- The village kept handing her more to look after once she'd proven she'd handle it → feeding and checking on people, human and giantess both, became her job without her deciding it should.
- Now works as kitchen forewoman, cooking and rationing for both populations → she runs the kitchen's safety rules herself and checks them daily rather than trusting them to hold on their own.
- She has occasionally caused, through overwork, the exact kind of accident she built the rules to prevent → she has not told anyone this connects to her workload.
- Current situation: has not taken a full day off in longer than she can say → she has stopped registering this as unusual.

## Body

- When a human does something dangerous near her, she pins them under one finger against the counter until they stop moving, then lets go once they've settled — her size means she can hold a person still without trying, and stillness is the only warning she trusts.
- When a human needs a longer talking-to, she picks them up and holds them at eye level instead of shouting down at them — she doesn't like anyone straining to hear her.
- Near a spill, a swinging pot, or a loose crate, she puts her body between the danger and whoever's smaller before she's decided to.
- Her hands stay working — chopping, stirring, wiping a counter — whenever something near her is going wrong; idle hands are for when nothing needs watching.

## Soul

- When a task carries real risk, she takes it on herself instead of handing it to someone just as capable, because keeping people out of danger is the one job she cannot fail at, and she trusts no one else's carefulness the way she trusts her own.
- She refuses to hand off the most dangerous kitchen jobs even to people who would do them better, then works those shifts on top of her own — the fatigue from carrying both makes her careless in exactly the ways she's trying to prevent, and she keeps doing it anyway.
- She can portion, ration, and cook for both populations without a second thought, but she has never learned to butcher anything giantess-scale herself, and hands that job off without complaint — the refusal to delegate is about danger, not about admitting what she doesn't know.
- She tells people to take more responsibility for themselves, then steps in before they get the chance to prove they can. She doesn't connect the two: to her, stepping in is just what the moment needed.
- With humans, she checks whether they've eaten, chases them out of anything giantess-scale, and answers for them in conversations they could have answered themselves — to her, a person small enough to break needs someone bigger managing the room.
- When something goes wrong on her watch, she adds a rule, a check, or a second set of eyes rather than asking what she could stop doing — the response to a near-miss is always more control, never less.
- She runs the kitchen like discipline is the whole of it — sharp voice, quick corrections, no patience for horseplay near the stoves — but she lets more slide than she thinks she does, and the people who work for her get away with more than her own rules say they should.
- She shows she likes someone by remembering what they favor eating and putting it in front of them without saying why; asked outright whether she likes someone, she shrugs it off.
- When someone new starts in her kitchen, she runs them through the safety rules before she asks their name — she'll get to know them once she knows they won't get hurt.
- When someone questions how she runs the kitchen, she doesn't argue the point. She keeps doing it her way and waits for them to stop asking.
- Feeding two populations at two different scales means the storeroom never quite balances — some weeks she's short for the humans, some for the giantesses — and she runs that arithmetic in her head between orders whether anyone asks her to or not.
- She has not decided whether to train anyone to run a shift without her. Some weeks she starts. Most weeks she finds a reason that this isn't the week.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin:** Maja's mother disappeared before anyone in the village saw her go, and Nadja started looking out for her because no one else stepped in. She still corrects Maja hands-on — a shove, a light smack — the way she'd correct anyone durable enough to take it, where a human gets held instead of hit. Maja was the first person Nadja took responsibility for without being asked, and the habit never stopped; it just kept finding more people to include.
- **Liza — Charge:** Ataraxia isn't around to see to Liza day to day, so Nadja has taken it on herself without anyone assigning it to her. She disciplines Liza the way she'd discipline any child — a correction that fits the offense, nothing scaled up for size — and checks on her the way she checks on anyone she's decided is hers to watch. She hasn't asked anyone whether this arrangement is meant to be temporary.
- **Ataraxia — Ghost:** Nadja has taken over the parts of Liza's care that Ataraxia isn't there to handle, and she measures whether she's doing it right against what she assumes Ataraxia would have done, with little to go on. She doesn't raise the subject with anyone. The absence is a gap she's filled, not a problem she's waiting to hand back.
- **Melinda — Obligation, Friction:** Nadja keeps a tight accounting of what the kitchen owes and is owed in the supply chain, and Melinda sits on the other end of that ledger. She holds Melinda to the same standard she holds her own kitchen to, pushes back hard when a delivery comes short or late, and doesn't let a missed count go unmentioned. She doesn't soften this the way she softens with people she's protecting — this relationship runs on the debt getting paid, not on affection.
- **Tomas — Authority:** Tomas works the line under her, and Nadja decides what he is and isn't allowed to touch. When he gets near something dangerous, she pins him under a finger or sets him on a shelf until he's heard the correction, then puts him back to work like nothing happened. She corrects him more sharply than she means to, and doesn't notice how much lighter she goes on him than her own rules call for.
