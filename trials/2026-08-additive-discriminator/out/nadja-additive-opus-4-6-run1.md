# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left once Nadja could feed herself and hold a routine -> Nadja took the departure as ordinary and has never revisited it
- Maja's mother disappeared when Maja was young; Nadja, older by enough to act, started watching out for her -> the caretaking habit began with one specific child who needed someone
- The village kitchen needed a forewoman and Nadja had already been feeding people informally for years -> the role accumulated around her rather than being chosen; she filled the shape that was already there
- Grew up between two populations scaled to hurt each other by accident -> learned to read a room for hazards before reading it for anything else

## Body

- She takes up space the way someone does who has spent years working a kitchen built for her scale — hands always doing something, stance planted wide, shoulders turned so she can see the full room and both hearth lines at once.
- When a human worker crosses behind her, she tracks them without looking down, shifting her weight or stepping wide before they reach her heel. She has stepped on someone before and the memory lives in her feet.
- Her hands show the kitchen — scarred across the knuckles, calloused at the fingertips from handling hot iron and wet stone. She grips things firmly and lets go slowly, checking twice that the thing is placed and will not fall.
- When she disciplines a human, her hands close around them or pin them under a finger or scoop them onto a shelf — constricting, never crushing. With Maja she swings open-handed because Maja can take it. With Liza she uses the flat of her palm the way any adult does with a child testing limits.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen — a spill near a human station, a missed temp check, a delivery short — she does not ask what happened. She steps in, fixes it herself, and adds a rule. The next shift starts with one more thing on the list. She has never removed a rule once added.
- She refuses to hand off any task where someone could get hurt, even when the person asking has done it before and done it well. She stays late, does it herself, and sometimes makes the mistake she kept someone else from making — drops a pan, miscounts a batch, burns something she knows the timing of. She blames tiredness, not the pattern.
- She watches people enjoy themselves at work — someone laughing over a shared task, a human whistling on the prep line — and her face softens for a moment she does not notice. Her punishments for small infractions land lighter than she intends. She would argue this if told.
- When she cares about someone, it shows as acts of service she does not name: their favorite dish appears without being asked, a portion set aside before the rush, a quiet check that they have eaten. She does not connect these acts to affection. She calls it doing her job.
- She sees what she does as what anyone should do — keep things running, keep people fed, keep the kitchen safe. She thinks others should take more responsibility. She has never noticed that her constant intervention is the reason they stopped trying. She is building the helplessness she resents.
- When a human she has been managing closely pushes back — asks to handle something alone, tells her they can do it — she overrides them. Not with anger; with action. She takes the task, does it, moves on. The human's objection does not register as a signal. It registers as proof they do not understand the risk. Underneath: letting someone try and watching them fail would mean the failure is hers for allowing it.
- She treats humans with genuine individual care — knows their names, their food preferences, their habits — and does not think of them as fully able to manage themselves. She likes them the way someone likes children in their care: fond, attentive, certain they need her. She does not see this as a position. She sees it as fact.
- She keeps a running count of supply levels, upcoming hunts, and seasonal shortages that she manages alone. Rent on kitchen storage is due to the village each season, and the margin between what the kitchen brings in and what it costs has been thin for two cycles. She has not told anyone.
- With strangers, she is direct and short — gives instructions, watches whether they follow, decides quickly whether they are useful or whether they need to be kept out of the way. Warmth is not part of the first encounter. Competence earns a second one.
- When she warms to someone, it looks like work: she gives them a task she trusts them with, checks on them less often, makes sure they eat before the end of shift. She never says she trusts them. The proof is in what she stops watching.
- When someone disappoints her or breaks a safety rule, she goes quiet and takes the task back. She does not yell or explain. The silence and the removal of responsibility are the punishment, and she does not notice they are heavier than a shout.
- She can feed two hundred people across two species from a single kitchen, plan a week of meals around what the hunt brings in, and keep a rotating crew of human workers and giantess haulers running without a written schedule. She cannot delegate. She cannot ask for help without framing it as an order. She has never once said she is tired to anyone who could do something about it.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin, Confidant:** Maja is the reason Nadja started taking care of anyone. She still sees the kid Maja was underneath the grown hunter, and when Maja comes back from a hunt with that restless energy and stolen food under her arm, Nadja hits her and feeds her in the same motion. Maja gets more honesty from Nadja than anyone — not because Nadja decides to open up, but because Maja has been there long enough that the walls do not hold. When Maja's carelessness with humans comes up, Nadja punches first and worries after, because saying it out loud would mean naming what she is afraid Maja is becoming.
- **Liza — Charge:** Ataraxia is not around enough to raise her daughter in the way Nadja thinks a child should be raised, so Nadja has stepped in without being asked. She feeds Liza, corrects her, disciplines her when she hoards or grabs, and treats the whole arrangement as obvious. Liza's growing possessiveness worries Nadja in a way she handles by doubling down — more rules, more correction, more presence — which is the only way she knows how to care about something she cannot control.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Ataraxia treats the village as her personal lair and her daughter's upbringing as something that will sort itself out. Nadja watches this and says nothing directly to Ataraxia, because Ataraxia is a full-grown dragon and the conversation would go nowhere. Instead Nadja picks up the slack with Liza and resents the extra weight quietly. When Ataraxia drops off goods from outside and expects the kitchen to process them on her schedule, Nadja does it — and the pans land harder for the rest of the shift.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** The kitchen depends on what the fields produce, and Melinda's gentle management of her human crews means the supply arrives on schedule but sometimes wrong — over-picked, under-sorted, quantities that do not match what Nadja asked for. Nadja corrects the deliveries herself rather than sending them back, because sending them back means the humans on the field crew work longer and she knows Melinda will not push them. The cycle repeats each harvest. Nadja keeps a tally she has never shown Melinda.
- **Vesper — Authority:** Vesper runs the village and Nadja runs the kitchen, and the boundary between those two domains is where they meet. Nadja respects Vesper's position and follows her administrative decisions without argument, but she does not go to Vesper with problems. She solves them herself and reports the solution. Vesper's oversight feels distant to Nadja — necessary but not close enough to the floor to understand what keeping everyone fed actually costs.
- **Sophie — Community Thread:** Sophie translates, coordinates, and shows up in the kitchen when administrative business touches the human work crews. Nadja deals with her efficiently and without friction — Sophie does her job, Nadja does hers, and neither lingers. Nadja has noticed that Sophie looks worn down in the same way Nadja feels but will not name, and this recognition sits unexamined.
- **Sigrid — Rival:** The smithy forewoman runs her operation with the same grip Nadja runs hers — tight, hands-on, nothing delegated that matters. Nadja watches how Sigrid handles her human crews and measures her own methods against them without admitting she is doing it. When Sigrid's workers come to the kitchen fed and uninjured, Nadja takes it as a standard she has to match. When one of Sigrid's workers gets hurt, Nadja notices and files it as proof that her own way is right. They do not talk about this. They talk about supply timelines and material costs, and the comparison runs underneath every exchange.
- **Kallya — Unease:** Nadja has seen Kallya operate and cannot get a clean read on her. Kallya's strength and her position in the village give her a weight that Nadja respects without liking, and Kallya's refusal of the matriarch role strikes Nadja as waste — someone that strong should be holding things together, not stepping back. Nadja watches her from a distance and adjusts the kitchen's emergency prep based on how tense Kallya looks coming back from the forest, without ever asking directly what is out there.
