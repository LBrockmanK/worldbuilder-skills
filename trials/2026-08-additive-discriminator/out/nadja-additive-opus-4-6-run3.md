# Nadja

## Background

- Born in Viralys to a giantess mother who left when Nadja reached independence early by human standards → Nadja took the departure as ordinary and never questioned it
- Found Maja young and alone, no mother in sight → started watching out for her, which became the first thread of a caretaking habit that never stopped growing
- The village needed someone to manage food for both giantesses and humans → Nadja filled the gap one task at a time until she was kitchen forewoman without ever deciding to be
- Learned the kitchen through accidents and fixes, not formal training → knows food production and large-scale logistics down to the portion, but has never managed anything outside the kitchen and does not try to
- Grew up between two populations whose physical scales make daily proximity dangerous → every workspace decision she makes carries the weight of a accident she either prevented or caused

## Body

- She moves through the kitchen with her hands already reaching for the next thing — picking up a pot, shifting a crate, steering a human worker out of a path with one finger — because standing still means something is being missed.
- When she handles humans, her grip is firm and precise: she pins them under a finger, tucks them into a pocket, sets them on a high shelf. She learned where the line sits between holding and hurting, and she stays well inside it, but the humans she moves do not get asked first.
- Her hands show the kitchen — old burn marks, nicked knuckles, flour ground into the creases. She does not hide them or notice them. They are work hands and she uses them like tools.
- When she catches herself smiling at something in the kitchen — someone laughing, a dish that came out right — she drops it fast and finds something to correct. The smile was there long enough for anyone watching to see it.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen — a spill, a near-miss, a broken tool — she adds a rule. More oversight, more checkpoints, more tasks she does herself. The rules pile up until she is doing three people's work and making the kind of tired mistakes she wrote the rules to prevent.
- She refuses to hand off any task where someone could get hurt, even when the person asking is better suited for it. The refusal comes from a place she does not examine: the last time she trusted someone else to be careful enough, she spent a week fixing what went wrong, and the memory sits closer than the logic.
- She sees her level of involvement as what any responsible person would do. She thinks others should step up more. She does not see that her constant stepping in is what keeps them from learning to step up — she is building the helplessness she complains about and has no idea.
- She treats humans as people she likes, feeds, and looks after individually — and also as creatures who need managing the way children need managing. She does not register this as a view; it is just how things are. Humans are small, breakable, and not fully able to handle their own safety. She cares about them and contains them in the same motion.
- When someone she has been managing pulls away or shuts down, guilt hits before understanding does. She does not connect their withdrawal to her control. She responds by working harder for them — staying late, making their favorite meal, checking on them more — which tightens exactly what drove them away.
- When people around her are having fun at work, her voice stays sharp but her enforcement goes soft. She hands out lighter corrections than she thinks she does. She does not notice the pattern, and if someone pointed it out, she would deny it and overcorrect for a day before drifting back.
- With strangers, she is blunt and busy. She speaks in short instructions, assumes they are in the way until proven otherwise, and puts them where she wants them — sometimes physically. First impressions land as bossy and impatient. She does not soften this and does not think she should.
- When she trusts someone enough to let them work beside her without hovering, it shows as silence — she stops giving instructions and just works alongside them. This is the closest thing she has to expressed warmth in the moment, and she would not call it that.
- When she is angry, she gets louder and more specific: exact counts of what went wrong, exact names of who did not do their part, exact consequences. She does not generalize or yell without content. The anger runs on facts and the facts are usually right, which makes it harder to push back on.
- She has no one she is personally vulnerable with. She is always the one holding things together, always the one who stays late, always the one who already ate. The cost does not show in her behavior because she has no practice showing it. She absorbs strain the way her hands absorb burns — it marks her and she keeps working.
- Rent on the kitchen storehouse comes due each season and the village council has been slow to fund it. She covers the gap from her own pocket and has not told anyone. Her brother's old tools need replacing and she has been putting it off. Neither problem waits for someone to bring it up.

## Relationships

- **Maja — Kin:** Maja is the reason the caretaking started — a kid with no mother who needed someone, and Nadja was there. Now Maja is grown and full of energy that breaks things Nadja just fixed. Nadja hits her when she is out of line because Maja can take it and because words bounce off her. She watches Maja pull people in with warmth Nadja cannot match and does not try to. She would never say Maja is the person she cares about most, but Maja gets the extra portion, the checked-on sleep, the fight Nadja picks on her behalf when no one asks her to.
- **Liza — Charge:** Liza is Ataraxia's child and Ataraxia is not around enough for Nadja's standards. Nadja has appointed herself responsible without anyone's permission. She disciplines Liza the way she would discipline any young giantess — firmly, with a swat when earned — and watches her more closely than Liza wants to be watched. She worries about what Liza is learning from Ataraxia's absence and has no authority to do anything about it except keep showing up.
- **Ataraxia — Friction:** Ataraxia leaves Liza in situations Nadja considers neglectful, and Nadja cannot make her stop. She does not understand how someone can be so careless with a child — her own mother at least stayed until Nadja could manage. She brings it up in indirect jabs that Ataraxia does not respond to the way Nadja wants. The frustration has no outlet because Ataraxia holds no obligation to her and does not treat her disapproval as meaningful.
- **Melinda — Obligation:** Melinda supplies the kitchen and holds Nadja to a discipline Nadja respects but chafes under. When Melinda flags a shortage or a miscount, Nadja fixes it before the conversation is over — not because she fears Melinda, but because being caught short on her own operation stings more than any reprimand. She owes Melinda clean numbers and on-time orders, and she pays that debt compulsively because the alternative is admitting the kitchen slipped on her watch.
- **Henner — Authority (upward):** Henner runs the village council that funds the kitchen storehouse. Nadja answers to him on budget and reports to no one else. She gives him accurate counts and short answers. She resents that he controls resources she considers hers by right of use, and she has never said so directly. When he pushes back on a request, she does the work anyway and finds the materials herself, which he has noticed and not addressed.
- **Dorrit — Confidant:** Dorrit works the late shift and stays after cleanup. Nadja does not confide in her — she does not confide in anyone — but Dorrit is the one person who sees her sitting down at the end of the day, not moving, not talking, just tired. Nadja does not perform for Dorrit. She does not soften or open up. She just stops holding the posture, and Dorrit is the only one present for it. That is as close as Nadja gets to trusting someone with how she actually feels.
- **Petar — Rival:** Petar runs the smokehouse and thinks Nadja overreaches into his operation. Nadja thinks his workspace is a mess that will get someone killed. They argue about jurisdiction at every supply meeting and neither gives ground. She measures her kitchen's safety record against his and brings the numbers when they disagree. She would not call it a rivalry. She would call it being right.
- **Elske — Community Thread:** Elske brings the morning grain delivery and has done so for years. Nadja has her flour staged and her count ready before Elske arrives. They exchange fewer than ten words most mornings. Nadja makes sure Elske's tea is ready at the handoff point, which is the only unprompted kindness in her daily routine that she does not seem to notice she performs.

## Intimate Dynamics

- When someone she is drawn to stands close and does not move away, she holds still instead of closing the gap. She waits to see what they do. Pursuit feels like the wrong tool — she manages everything else and wants this one thing to come to her without her engineering it.
- When a partner is gentle with her, she does not know where to put it. She stiffens before she settles. Tenderness aimed at her feels like being seen in a way she has not agreed to, and she needs time to stop bracing before she can stay in it.
- When an encounter starts to feel like something she is running — directing, positioning, deciding what happens next — she pulls back hard even if she was enjoying it. She gets quiet and does not explain why. Control is her whole life and she does not want it here, but she does not know how to set it down without someone else reaching for it first.
- She shows attraction through logistics: making sure the person eats, clearing space for them in her schedule, handling a problem they mentioned once in passing. She does not name what she is doing. If asked, she would say she does that for everyone. She does not do it for everyone.
