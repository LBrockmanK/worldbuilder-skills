# Nadja

## Design Notes

### Session Notes

- Born in Viralys; mother left once she considered Nadja old enough — not very old by human standards, but giantesses reach independence faster; her mother kept watch longer than some; no trauma or bitterness about it
- [[Maja]]'s mother abandoned her young (or at least that's presumed — no one ever saw her); Nadja started looking out for Maja and took a big-sister role, which developed into caretaking more broadly
- The caretaking instinct started with Maja specifically — a kid who needed someone — and grew outward as the village's practical needs gave structure; she didn't set out to become the person who feeds everyone; it accumulated
- Now serves as kitchen forewoman, feeding both human and giantess populations
- Core fear: if she stops controlling the outcome, someone gets hurt; not just professional diligence but personal — she's between fragile humans and giantess-scale hazards every day; the warmth she won't acknowledge is what makes the stakes feel so high
- Self-image gap: sees what she does as normal, thinks others should be more responsible — while her overbearing nature prevents them from developing that responsibility; creating the problem she's solving
- Unconsciously infantilizes humans; not malicious — just how things are to her; humans are small, fragile, need looking after; she likes them, feeds them, cares individually — but doesn't think of them as fully autonomous
- When things go wrong, doubles down: more rules, more oversight, more hands-on control; human autonomy shrinks further
- Refuses to delegate dangerous tasks even when someone else would do them better; result is she's overworked and occasionally makes the exact mistakes she's trying to prevent; root is the control pattern — can't trust anyone else to be careful enough
- Won't admit she enjoys people having fun at work; bark worse than bite; doesn't notice she softens around people enjoying themselves or that her punishments for minor infractions are lighter than she thinks
- Self-reliant; no one she's personally vulnerable with; always the one holding it together
- Warmth is acts of service she doesn't draw attention to: preparing someone's favorite dish, making sure they remember to eat
- Physical discipline scales with target: [[Maja]] gets hit (durable peer); [[strelitzia-liza|Liza]] gets standard child discipline; humans get contained — pinned under a finger, shoved in a pocket, put on a shelf, held up for a talking-down; constricting but not harmful
- Protectiveness operates within resurrection framework: strict about safety because accidents are disruptive and unpleasant, not because she treats human life as sacred

### Builder Context

- Narrative function: the giantess whose caretaking has become control; demonstrates how genuine kindness becomes suffocating when the caretaker cannot let go; the self-reinforcing cycle (accident → tighter grip → overwork → more accidents) is the engine
- The [[Maja]] dynamic is reciprocal across both notes: Maja's Confidant entry describes getting more of Nadja than anyone; Nadja's Kin entry describes the origin of the caretaking instinct
- [[strelitzia-liza|Liza]]'s note labels Nadja as Authority; from Nadja's side the label is Charge — she has self-appointed the responsibility because [[Ataraxia]] isn't around to do it
- [[Melinda]]'s note does not currently have a Nadja relationship entry; Nadja's Obligation entry describes the supply-chain discipline dynamic, which may warrant a reciprocal entry from Melinda's side

## Background

- Born in Viralys; mother left once she was old enough → giantesses reach independence faster than humans; her mother kept watch longer than some; no bitterness about it
- Maja's mother abandoned her young, or at least that is what everyone presumes → Nadja started looking out for Maja and took a big-sister role
- The caretaking that started with Maja grew outward as the village's practical needs gave it structure → she did not set out to become the person who feeds everyone; it accumulated
- Now kitchen forewoman → feeds both human and giantess populations from a multi-hearth facility that runs every day

## Body

- When Maja breaks something, Nadja hits her. Maja is durable and a peer, and the hit is how they have always handled correction. When Liza acts out, Nadja grabs her by the scruff. When a human causes trouble, Nadja pins them under a finger, shoves them in a pocket, puts them on a shelf, or holds them up at eye level for a talking-down. The contact is constricting, not harmful. She scales force to what the body in front of her can take without thinking about it.
- She moves through the kitchen without pausing, reaching over and around people and obstacles as though they are part of the layout. Her hands are always doing something: stirring, checking temperature, adjusting a pot, moving a human who has drifted too close to a hazard.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen or someone gets hurt, she adds rules, oversight, and direct intervention. Human autonomy shrinks each time. She cannot leave the outcome to someone else. The root is fear: the last time she was not watching, someone got burned, and the time before that, someone fell. That the burns heal and the falls end in resurrection does not soften the reaction. The disruption, the lost hours, and the mess are reason enough.
- She sees her level of control as what any responsible person would do. She thinks others should step up more, and she says so often. Her management is the reason they have not had to. She never gives anyone the room to fail and learn from it. She is creating the dependency she complains about and has not connected the two.
- She threatens more than she follows through. She does not notice that she eases up when people around her are enjoying themselves, or that her punishments for small infractions are lighter than she thinks they are. She will not admit she likes people having fun at work. She would argue the point.
- She treats humans the way a careful person treats something precious and breakable. She likes them. She feeds them. She knows their names and what they eat. When a human says they can handle something, she checks anyway. When a human does handle it, she credits her own preparation rather than their capability. Nothing in her daily experience pushes back on this.
- She refuses to hand off dangerous tasks even when someone else would do them better, because she cannot trust anyone else to be careful enough. She is overworked and makes the exact mistakes she is trying to prevent: a pot left too long, a shelf misjudged, a human underfoot at the wrong moment.
- When she meets someone new, she is direct and already sorting: who needs feeding, who is in the way, who is about to do something dangerous. She asks questions to find out what someone can handle, not to make conversation.
- Warmth is acts of service she does not draw attention to. She prepares someone's favorite dish without being asked. She makes sure they eat. If someone notices, she changes the subject.
- She is always the one holding it together. When someone asks how she is, she answers with what needs doing next. She does not have anyone she goes to when the work is too much, and she would not describe it as too much.
- The kitchen feeds the entire village, every day, at two scales. She has built the operation around herself. She counts portions, tracks stores, plans meals weeks ahead. She can run a kitchen feeding two populations and keep it going through any crisis. She cannot teach someone else to do it, because teaching requires letting them make mistakes, and she will not watch someone make a mistake she could prevent. Some weeks she is behind on sleep and ahead on the meal plan. Some weeks it is the reverse. The schedule owns her and she would not hand it to anyone else.

## Relationships

- **Maja -- Kin, Confidant:** She found Maja young and alone, and started looking after her before she had a word for what she was doing. Maja is the one person she hits when correction is needed, because the familiarity is that old and Maja can take it. Maja gets more of Nadja than anyone: more honesty, more temper, more of the worry she carries about the kitchen and the people in it. Nadja calls this family, not openness.
- **Strelitzia (Liza) -- Charge:** She has taken on Liza's care because Ataraxia is not around enough to do it. She feeds her, disciplines her, checks on her. None of this was asked for. She treats Liza the way she treats every young creature in her reach: with structure, standards, and the certainty that without supervision, something will break.
- **Melinda -- Obligation:** Melinda supplies the kitchen, and Nadja holds her to the standard the kitchen requires. When deliveries are late or quality drops, Nadja does not soften the response. The relationship runs on the debt of consistent supply, and Nadja enforces it without sentiment and without examining whether the standard she sets is one anyone other than herself could meet.
- **Vesper -- Authority:** Vesper runs the village. Nadja defers on administrative matters and does not challenge the structure. Inside the kitchen, she runs everything herself and does not ask permission. She respects Vesper's position the way she respects any system that works: she follows it where it applies and does not think about it where it does not.
- **Ataraxia -- Friction:** Ataraxia is Liza's mother and is not raising her. Nadja has filled the gap without being asked and resents that the gap exists. She does not confront Ataraxia about it. Ataraxia is a dragon and does as she pleases. Nadja channels the frustration into the work of parenting Liza, which feeds the control pattern she already runs on.
- **Sophie Liska -- Unease:** Sophie is a human who runs half the village administration, speaks both languages, and operates with authority Nadja does not extend to other humans. Sophie does not fit the framework. Nadja cannot treat her as fragile or dependent, and the discomfort shows: she is more formal with Sophie than with other humans, less physical, careful in a way she is not elsewhere. She has not decided what Sophie is to her, and the indecision makes her brusque.
- **Kallya -- Rival:** Kallya runs the smithy the way Nadja runs the kitchen: tight schedule, high standards, no tolerance for preventable mistakes. When the kitchen needs something from the forge and the forge is behind, Nadja pushes and Kallya does not move. They argue over shared resources, over priorities, over whose operation comes first this week. Nadja sees Kallya's refusal to bend because it looks like her own.
- **Sigrid -- Ideological Counterpart:** Sigrid is the strongest giantess in the village and refuses the matriarch title. Nadja respects what Sigrid does in the field and cannot understand the refusal. Someone who sees what needs doing and does not take formal charge of it is leaving the structure for others to build. That Sigrid's refusal has reasons does not make it less frustrating to someone who ends up building it.
