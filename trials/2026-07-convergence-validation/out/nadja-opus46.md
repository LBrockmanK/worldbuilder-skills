# Nadja

## Design Notes

### Session Notes

- Born in Viralys; mother left once she considered Nadja old enough — not very old by human standards, but giantesses reach independence faster; her mother kept watch longer than some; no trauma or bitterness about it
- [[Maja]]'s mother abandoned her young (or at least that's presumed — no one ever saw her); Nadja started looking out for Maja and took a big-sister role, which developed into caretaking more broadly
- The caretaking instinct started with Maja specifically — a kid who needed someone — and grew outward as the village's practical needs gave structure; she didn't set out to become the person who feeds everyone; it accumulated
- Now serves as kitchen forewoman, feeding both human and giantess populations
- Core fear: if she stops controlling the outcome, someone gets hurt; not just professional diligence but personal — she's between fragile humans and giantess-scale hazards every day; the warmth she won't acknowledge is what makes the stakes feel so high
- Self-image gap: sees what she does as normal, thinks others should be more responsible — while her overbearing nature prevents them from developing that responsibility; creating the problem she's solving
- Unconsciously infantilizes humans; not malicious — just how things are to her; humans are small, fragile, need looking after; she likes them, feeds them, cares individually — but doesn't think of them as fully autonomous
- When things go wrong, doubles down: more rules, more oversight, more hands-on control; human autonomy shrinks further
- Refuses to delegate dangerous tasks even when someone else would do them better; result is she's overworked and occasionally makes the exact mistakes she's trying to prevent; root is the control pattern — can't trust anyone else to be careful enough
- Won't admit she enjoys people having fun at work; bark worse than bite; doesn't notice she softens around people enjoying themselves or that her punishments for minor infractions are lighter than she thinks
- Self-reliant; no one she's personally vulnerable with; always the one holding it together
- Warmth is acts of service she doesn't draw attention to: preparing someone's favorite dish, making sure they remember to eat
- Physical discipline scales with target: [[Maja]] gets hit (durable peer); [[strelitzia-liza|Liza]] gets standard child discipline; humans get contained — pinned under a finger, shoved in a pocket, put on a shelf, held up for a talking-down; constricting but not harmful
- Protectiveness operates within resurrection framework: strict about safety because accidents are disruptive and unpleasant, not because she treats human life as sacred

### Builder Context

- Narrative function: the giantess whose caretaking has become control; demonstrates how genuine kindness becomes suffocating when the caretaker cannot let go; the self-reinforcing cycle (accident → tighter grip → overwork → more accidents) is the engine
- The [[Maja]] dynamic is reciprocal across both notes: Maja's Confidant entry describes getting more of Nadja than anyone; Nadja's Kin entry describes the origin of the caretaking instinct
- [[strelitzia-liza|Liza]]'s note labels Nadja as Authority; from Nadja's side the label is Charge — she has self-appointed the responsibility because [[Ataraxia]] isn't around to do it
- [[Melinda]]'s note does not currently have a Nadja relationship entry; Nadja's Obligation entry describes the supply-chain discipline dynamic, which may warrant a reciprocal entry from Melinda's side

## Background

- Born in Viralys; mother left once she judged Nadja old enough → Nadja raised herself from that point; giantesses reach independence early, and her mother stayed longer than some
- Found [[Maja]] young, apparently motherless, with no one looking out for her → took the big-sister role, the first act of the caretaking that became her work
- The village's practical needs gave the caretaking shape → the kitchen forewoman role built up around her; she did not set out for it and has not stepped back from it
- Now feeds both human and giantess populations from the kitchen → the village's food production runs through her, and she has not handed any of it off

## Body

- When [[Maja]] steps out of line, Nadja hits her. Maja is a peer and can take it. When [[strelitzia-liza|Liza]] misbehaves, Nadja disciplines her as any child: firm, quick, no apology. When a human steps out of line, Nadja pins them under a finger, shoves them in a pocket, puts them on a shelf, or holds them up at eye level for a scolding. The hold is tight but does no harm. The force scales to the target. The intent does not.
- She is always moving in the kitchen. Multiple hearths, two populations, timing that does not wait. She works through the space without stopping, and anyone in it either keeps up or gets moved aside.
- Her hands show the work. She handles cookware at giantess scale, manages fire across hearths, and portions food down to human servings with a steadiness that comes from years of doing it. She does not think about the size difference while she works. Everyone around her does.

## Soul

- When something goes wrong in the kitchen or someone gets hurt, she adds rules. More checking, more direct handling, less room for anyone else to act. The tighter she grips, the less capable everyone around her becomes, and the more she needs to grip. She fears that if she stops controlling the outcome, someone gets hurt, because she stands between fragile humans and giantess-scale hazards every day and the warmth she will not name is what makes it feel personal.
- She believes her level of involvement is normal and thinks others should step up. Her constant stepping in is what stops them. She is solving a problem she is making, and she would be the last person in the village to believe it.
- She refuses to hand off dangerous tasks even when someone else would do them better. She stays later, takes on more, and sometimes makes the exact mistakes she is trying to prevent. She cannot trust anyone else to be careful enough, because trusting someone means accepting that the outcome leaves her hands.
- She treats humans as small, fragile, and in need of looking after. She likes them. She feeds them, knows what they like, cares about them one by one. She assumes they need managing. When things go wrong, she tightens: watches more closely, steps in more often, leaves less room for humans to act on their own. The pattern is how the world looks from her height and her history.
- Her strictness about human safety is practical. A death means lost time: the resurrection runs its course, the work stops, someone has to cover. She treats human death the way she treats a cracked hearthstone. It is a problem to prevent, not a loss to mourn.
- She will not admit she likes people having fun in her kitchen. She softens when people around her are enjoying themselves, and her punishments for small offenses are lighter than she thinks they are. What she presents and what is true are not the same, and she is the only one who does not see it.
- With someone new in her space, she sizes them up by what they need and how much trouble they will be. She starts managing before she has their name. She offers food before she offers anything else.
- When she cares about someone, it shows in what she makes. She remembers who likes what, adjusts without being asked, and makes sure the people she watches eat when they forget to. She calls this her job. Everyone else calls it something different.
- When someone pushes back on how she runs things, she does not argue. She does the task herself, does it faster, and does not bring it up again. The quiet is how she holds the line.
- She is overworked all the time. The kitchen runs on her and she will not share it. She counts supplies twice, checks temperatures three times, and stays up after the evening meal to set up for the next. Some weeks she sleeps in the kitchen. She keeps a list of tasks she could hand off. The list is longer each season. She has handed off none of them.
- She knows food, supply timing, and what it takes to feed two populations from the same hearths. She knows which suppliers cut corners and which cuts of meat go furthest. She does not follow village politics, does not track who owes what beyond her kitchen, and hands those questions off without a second thought.
- She gives orders, not requests. Short sentences, plain words, no softening. When she is angry the volume goes up but the words stay the same. She sounds harsher than she acts, and the people who have worked under her long enough watch what she does, not what she says.

## Relationships

- **[[Maja]] — Kin:** Maja was young and alone when Nadja found her, and the big-sister role Nadja took then is the root of everything she does now. They run Hunt and Hearth together: Maja hunts, Nadja cooks, they argue about everything in between, and she has never worked as well with anyone else. When Maja crosses a line, Nadja hits her, because Maja can take it. Nadja gives Maja more of her temper, her time, and her cooking than anyone else in the village.
- **[[strelitzia-liza|Liza]] — Charge:** Nadja took on Liza because [[Ataraxia]] is not around enough to do it. No one asked her to. She disciplines Liza the same way each time: firm, clear, without apology. She tracks what Liza eats, where Liza goes, and whether Liza is picking up habits worth correcting.
- **[[Melinda]] — Obligation:** Nadja depends on Melinda's supply line and holds it to her own standard. When a delivery is late or short, Nadja counts what is missing, names the cost, and expects it fixed before the next one. She sets the terms of what the kitchen needs and how it arrives. She treats the arrangement as settled and the debt as always slightly owed.
- **[[Vesper]] — Authority:** Vesper runs the village. Nadja runs the kitchen. The line between those two sits where Nadja drew it, and Vesper has not moved it. When Vesper needs something from the kitchen, Nadja provides it. When Vesper questions how the kitchen works, Nadja listens, nods, and changes nothing.
- **[[sophie-liska|Sophie]] — Friction:** Sophie runs the human side of the village well enough that Nadja's instinct to step in has nowhere to go. Nadja watches Sophie handle work that would overwhelm most humans and still catches herself checking whether Sophie has eaten, whether she is sleeping enough, whether someone should step in. She steps in anyway, in small ways she does not notice.
- **[[Ataraxia]] — Unease:** Ataraxia is Liza's mother and does not attend to Liza in ways Nadja considers enough. Nadja fills what is missing and says nothing to Ataraxia about it. She cannot tell whether Ataraxia has handed Liza off on purpose or whether Ataraxia does not think about it at all. Either answer would change what Nadja does next, and she has not asked.
- **[[Kallya]] — Rival:** Kallya handles her work in a way Nadja watches more closely than she would admit. When Kallya makes a call Nadja would have made differently, Nadja runs the other way in her head for days. She does not think of it as competition. She thinks of it as holding a standard.
- **[[Sigrid]] — Ideological Counterpart:** Sigrid's way with the people under her is looser than Nadja can stand. Where Nadja tightens, Sigrid gives room, and the results come close enough that Nadja cannot dismiss them. She does not argue the point with Sigrid. She works harder to prove her own way right by outcome.
