# Viralys — Seed Document

> Foundational reference for all worldbuilding phases. All Wide-phase work branches from this document. Do not modify without running worldbuilder-world-planning.

---

## Foundational Questions

**Setting wound:**
Decades ago, a war between rogue giants and Viralys ended in catastrophic magical discharge. Concentrated mana saturated the surrounding forests, forming corrupted hearts — pulsing cores that spread mutagenic corruption outward through the root systems of the trees. The cursed forest is still spreading. The giantess most responsible for the original discharge now lives in Viralys as its strongest protector, hiding what she did and what she is.

**Community character:**
A soft oligarchy of absolute physical disparity. The giantesses do not need the humans — they could survive without them. The humans cannot survive here without the giantesses. This imbalance is total and everyone knows it. And yet the village mostly functions, because most giantesses find humans useful, interesting, or worth protecting in their own way. The relationship is less a contract than an ecosystem: bees and beekeepers, where the keeper's care is genuine and the power differential is absolute. Outside of work, the two populations keep a quiet distance from each other that neither has fully acknowledged.

**Player connection:**
A human newcomer to Viralys — origin unclear, arrival circumstances likely involving a giantess intervention they don't fully remember. They have an unusually strong soul: they remember deaths that others forget, and they carry a scent that wildlife and giantesses find inexplicably appetizing. This makes them both more resilient and more reliably endangered than the average villager.

**Hidden layer:**
The souls of all humans in Viralys are bound to the great tree at the heart of Vesper's sanctum. This is framed as a resurrection system — when a human dies in the village or surrounding forest, their soul returns to the tree and is restored. What is not discussed: when a human finally dies of old age and cannot be resurrected, their soul is absorbed by the tree, and by extension, by Vesper. The village is slowly, passively empowering her. This is not why the system exists, and Vesper may not have intended it — but it is what the system does. The humans tied to the tree cannot leave Viralys permanently; their soul anchor keeps them here.

**Era:**
Timeless fantasy — no connection to any historical period. Pre-industrial. Magic is real and structural, not exceptional. Giants are a known and ancient species. The village exists as a self-contained settlement, effectively isolated from wider civilization by the surrounding forests.

**Household clusters:**
Six giantess-anchored clusters and two human population clusters. See Household Clusters section below.

## Setting Summary

Viralys is a walled village deep in an ancient forest, home to roughly a hundred humans and fourteen giantesses. The giantesses — all female, all partly some non-human species — stand between thirty and thirty-five meters tall and serve as the village's hunters, forewomen, and protectors. The humans work the fields, staff the workshops, and live in the narrow quarters too small for any giantess to enter. Three rings of forest surround the village: the Dark Forest, where the hunters work; the Forbidden Forest, patrolled by a lone warden; and the Cursed Forest, where corruption spreads from ancient magical wounds and nothing goes willingly.

The village has no matriarch. The strongest giantess refuses the role. The oldest one runs everything in her absence.

## Genre & Tone

**Primary genre:** Dark fantasy / survival comedy

**Tonal range:** The world is genuinely dangerous — the forest kills, death is frequent, and the power differential between humans and giantesses is not played for false comfort. Within that danger, there is warmth, humor, and found-family texture. The comedy is dark: it lives in the gap between how enormous and careless the giantesses are and how small and fragile the humans are. Digestion and predation are real consequences, treated with dark humor rather than horror.

**Content notes:** Predation content (vore, size-differential danger, swallowing, digestion) is a primary mechanic and is handled in detail throughout. Intimate dynamics: none. Death is frequent but impermanent for most characters due to the soul-tree resurrection system — for the player character, it is permanent in memory if not in body.

## Inspirations

**Setting inspirations:**
- *Dungeon Meshi / Delicious in Dungeon* — the mundane texture of surviving in a world with lethal things in it; creature ecology given real thought
- *Made in Abyss* — beauty and horror coexisting in a place that is genuinely trying to kill you; the cost of going deeper
- *Mushishi* — quiet, ancient, and indifferent natural forces; things that are not evil but will consume you anyway

**Tonal inspirations:**
- Dark comedy tradition of protagonists who are cosmically unlucky but resilient
- Soft pred/prey genre conventions — danger that is intimate rather than malicious
- Isekai settlement fantasy — the outsider integrating into a community with its own established logic

## Key Tropes & Themes

1. **Size as power made literal** — the hierarchy isn't metaphorical; it's a physical fact that cannot be argued with
2. **The gilded cage** — safety purchased with freedom; the soul-tree makes leaving impossible and most humans don't fully grasp this
3. **Predation as danger and dark comedy** — being eaten is a genuine risk, handled with more dark humor than horror
4. **Redemption through service** — the strongest giantess has done terrible things; she serves now because she believes she owes it
5. **Care that doesn't understand its own power** — giantesses who genuinely like humans who still crush, forget, or consume them by accident
6. **The natural order as philosophy** — the belief that the strong consuming the weak is not cruelty but harmony; embodied in the elf warden and partially in the outer patrol giantess
7. **Secrets between siblings** — the two most powerful giantesses are related; no one in the village knows
8. **Institutional rot beneath genuine care** — Vesper is the most human-protective giantess in the village and the one whose existence most threatens them
9. **Death as inconvenience, memory as the real cost** — most humans lose their deaths; the player keeps theirs
10. **Old wounds in the forest** — the corruption is not random; it is the physical residue of a war that one person in the village caused
11. **The gap between how power sees itself and how powerlessness experiences it** — each giantess believes herself reasonable; the humans live with the variance
12. **Belonging without consent** — you didn't choose this village, you can't leave, and you might come to love it anyway

## Character Note Guidance

**Relationship distribution:** Giantess and human characters should have primarily within-group relationships. Cross-group relationships (a giantess with a human as a named relationship, or vice versa) are meaningful exceptions — each one requires a specific reason it crosses the default social boundary. The cast's cross-group relationships (Sophie/Vesper, Jaro/Elara, Mir/Liza, etc.) are all notable precisely because within-group is the baseline.

## Community

Viralys is a place that works because everyone has decided it should. The giantesses largely believe they are doing right by the humans — protecting them, feeding them, keeping the forest back. The humans largely accept the arrangement, because the alternative is dying in the forest. Neither group has a word for what they are to each other, and neither has looked too closely at the question.

Outside of work hours the two populations occupy different spaces without formal segregation — the giantesses simply can't fit in the human quarter, and most humans don't approach the giant-scale buildings unless they have to. Social friction runs low because interaction stays functional. When it doesn't — when a giantess is careless or a human tests a boundary — the result is immediate and physically unambiguous.

The humans have opinions about their giantesses: strong ones, quietly held. Some are genuinely fond. Some are frightened and hide it well. A few are angry in ways they have no safe outlet for.

## World Introduction

*(Pre-game player-facing text)*

Viralys is not what you expected.

You knew about the giantesses before you arrived — everyone has heard something. What you didn't know is how normal it would feel, walking through a village where the buildings on the main street are four stories of smooth stone fitted for people thirty meters tall, where the ground shakes softly every few minutes, where a hand the size of a cart wheel might descend from above to move you out of the way without a word.

You have a bunk in the human quarter. You have been assigned to a work crew. Someone tall — very tall, compared to you, though not by giantess standards — has explained the rules, the shifts, the forest boundary you are not supposed to cross alone. The trees begin fifty meters past the gate. At night you can hear things moving in them.

The giantesses are not unkind. Most of them. You are safe here, in the way that something small and edible can be safe in a place full of things that could eat it but have mostly decided not to.

That distinction will matter more than you expect.

## Opening Situation

The village is between hunts — the last Dreadwolf incursion was put down two days ago, and the Dark Forest has been quieter since. There is work to be done in the fields and the smithy. The kitchen smells of something enormous being cooked. The administrator's sanctum is as busy as it always is. Somewhere in the Forbidden Forest, something is sitting alone by a lake and not talking to anyone.

The player has recently arrived. Their first death has already happened. They don't remember it clearly — a blur of teeth, darkness, a tree's roots, and then morning, and their bunk, and the smell of damp soil fading. The day begins normally.

## Story Direction (stub)

*To be expanded after cast is established.*

The core tension is structural: the village is stable, which means the thing corroding it — the spreading cursed forest, the unresolved matriarch question, the soul-tree's slow accumulation of power — does so slowly enough that no one is forced to act. The player's strong soul and persistent memory put them in a position no one else occupies: they accumulate understanding that the village has spent generations quietly forgetting.

The corruption in the forest has a source. It always did. Someone in this village is carrying that.

## Locations

1. **The Village Square** — the open-air crossroads of Viralys, scaled for giantesses but crossed by both populations; where announcements are made and first impressions form
2. **Human Quarter** — the narrow residential streets and low buildings too small for any giantess to enter; the only space in Viralys that belongs entirely to humans
3. **City Hall / Vesper's Sanctum** — an enormous overgrown building housing village administration, a herb garden, and the great tree that holds every human soul; Vesper rarely leaves it
4. **The Kitchen** — Nadja's domain; a cavernous multi-hearth production facility feeding both populations; perpetually in motion
5. **The Fields** — the agricultural land beyond the human quarter, managed by the farm forewoman with enthusiastic but dangerous help; where human laborers spend most of their working hours
6. **The Smithy** — the forge district at the village's eastern edge; heat, noise, the ring of enormous hammers; where steel and colossite are produced and where the right materials can become something extraordinary
7. **The Gate** — the formal boundary where the village ends and the Dark Forest begins; guarded loosely, crossed more than it should be
8. **The Dark Forest** — the nearest ring of forest, managed by the hunters; dangerous but known, with regular patrol routes and established kill zones
9. **The Forbidden Forest** — the middle ring, patrolled by a lone warden; further from the village, quieter, and hiding a lake where something very powerful goes to be alone
10. **The Lake in the Forbidden Forest** — a still body of water deep enough in the outer patrol zone that few humans have ever seen it; not on any map given to newcomers
11. **The Cursed Forest** — the outer ring; corruption thickens as you go deeper; the trees here remember what was done to them
12. **The Corrupted Hearts** — the pulsing cores of the corruption, deep in the cursed forest; glowing blue, screaming at the approach of anything with a large magic well; crushing one sends the forest into shock
13. **The Mines** — source of colossite ore; rock elementals animate here; humans are sent when the elementals are quiet and the reward in materials is worth the risk
14. **Vesper's Herb Garden** — the walled garden behind city hall; plants grow at impossible speed here; the one place Vesper retreats when the administrative weight gets too heavy

## Art Style

Detailed anime illustration — clean linework, expressive character design, strong demi-human features (animal ears, horns, tails rendered with care). Character scale contrast is a visual priority: humans and giantesses in the same frame, the size differential clear and consistent. Color palette shifts by zone: warm amber and green for the village interior, cool blues and deep greens for the forests, corrupted zones rendered in sickly blue-green. ## Musical Theme

Lush orchestral fantasy with a folk undercurrent — strings and woodwinds for the village's everyday texture, percussion and low brass when the forest comes close. Quiet moments favor acoustic instruments and ambient sound (wind, water, the distant creak of enormous things moving). Corruption zones: dissonant, slow, wrong. Reference tones: *Made in Abyss OST* (Koushirou Ono), *Mushishi* (Toshio Masuda).

## Household Clusters

### 1. Guardian's Household
**Members:** Two giantesses — the village's strongest warrior (reluctant, guilt-driven, refuses the matriarch title) and her younger sibling (the outer patrol warden, volatile, philosophically opposed to her sister's values). The sibling relationship is unknown to every other character in the village.
**Function:** De facto military authority. The elder is the undisputed strongest and the natural matriarch by every tradition — her refusal leaves the village without formal leadership. The younger patrols the Forbidden Forest, officially keeping humans out of danger, unofficially keeping herself away from the village.
**Internal tension:** The elder has rebuilt herself around service and penance; the younger believes humans are beneath genuine consideration. They are the only giantess pair in the village who fundamentally disagree about what humans are. The secret of their relationship is a pressure valve that has never been released.
**Inter-household connections:** The elder defers to the steward on administrative matters while holding absolute authority on combat and threat response. The younger's patrol creates a functional dependency — no one else covers the Forbidden Forest perimeter — that gives her more leverage than she acknowledges.
**Trajectory:** The matriarch question will force resolution eventually. When it does, the sibling secret becomes relevant in ways that will reframe the village's entire power structure.
**Narrative hook:** The elder caused the cursed forest. No one in the village knows.

---

### 2. The Steward's House
**Members:** The village's oldest giantess (dryad, plant-aligned, the de facto administrator), her long-term human protégé (the only human fluent in the giantess language, functions as human-side co-administrator), and an 80-meter lamia who was already in the tree when they started filing paperwork.
**Function:** Village management — human workforce assignment, district oversight, medicine production, translation, institutional memory. Everything administrative routes through here. The lamia contributes nothing to administration but is formally assigned here because she lives in the tree and the village needed to put her somewhere.
**Internal tension:** The administrator is genuinely overwhelmed and perpetually behind. Her protégé runs herself ragged compensating. Neither has told the other how close to the edge they are. Beneath the warmth of their relationship is a structural fact neither has examined: the administrator's tree holds the souls of every human in the village, and when those humans finally die, something of them becomes part of her. The lamia's presence adds a different pressure: her animal-husbandry view of humans — tending, naming, consuming — is an uncomfortable mirror for the administrator's own relationship to the humans bound to her tree. Confronting the lamia about it would require examining herself.
**Inter-household connections:** Every other cluster depends on this one. It is the connective tissue of the village — the intake point for new arrivals, the source of medicine, the only location where both languages are spoken fluently. The lamia's nominal town guard role creates a loose functional connection to the Guardian's Household, though she answers to no one.
**Trajectory:** If the soul-tree system is ever surfaced and understood, this cluster is at the center of the fallout. The administrator's genuine care for humans does not resolve the parasitism — it complicates it. The lamia's presence means the uncomfortable parallel is already sitting in the tree, waiting to be articulated.
**Narrative hook:** The tree can perceive through its root network. The administrator can, if she chooses, watch anywhere the roots reach. The lamia is napping in the branches and may or may not know this.

---

### 3. Hunt and Hearth
**Members:** The village's most capable huntress (cat-girl, careless, notorious for "losing" humans on hunts) and the kitchen forewoman (fox-girl, workaholic, strict, genuinely kind beneath it).
**Function:** The hunt-to-kitchen pipeline — the huntress brings meat, the cook turns it into food for both populations. They argue constantly and work seamlessly.
**Internal tension:** The huntress's relationship with humans is escalating in ways she doesn't fully understand — what started as carelessness and genuine accidents has become a habit, and the habit is masking something lonelier. The cook is the only one who sees it clearly enough to punch her about it, which is also her preferred way of expressing affection.
**Inter-household connections:** The kitchen feeds the whole village; every cluster depends on the cook. The huntress operates most deeply in the Dark Forest, in functional proximity to the outer patrol. She also regularly steals food from the kitchen, which the cook equally regularly punishes.
**Trajectory:** The huntress has never eaten a human intentionally. The village's dark comedy lives partly in the question of when that changes and what it means when it does.
**Narrative hook:** Every human "lost" on a hunt technically survived — she swallowed them to save them from something worse. She hasn't mentioned this.

---

### 4. The Sisters' Trade
**Members:** Two cow-girl giantess sisters — the older (forewoman of the smithy: rough, protective, never shows weakness) and the younger (forewoman of the fields: gentle, open, dangerously naive about human limitations).
**Function:** Material production — the forge produces steel and colossite; the fields supply crops. Both roles require consistent human labor, making these the giantesses with the most daily contact with human workers.
**Internal tension:** The older sister's protectiveness is built on fear she won't admit — she watched their parents die and has decided the world is too rough for someone that open. She is wrong about what her sister needs. The younger's relentless warmth and accidental over-taxing of human workers creates friction she never notices and her older sister is always quietly cleaning up.
**Inter-household connections:** The smithy and fields supply the kitchen and the whole village. Both sisters are deeply embedded in daily human life, giving them visibility into the human population that other giantesses lack.
**Trajectory:** The older sister's protectiveness will eventually cost something — either her relationship with her sister or her relationship with the humans she doesn't fully trust.
**Narrative hook:** The older sister will forge something extraordinary for anyone who brings her the right materials. She has never said what "extraordinary" means.

---

### 5. The Wild Warden
**Members:** One giantess — Elara, elf-type, animal-aligned, forest-edge presence. No permanent structure in the village interior.
**Function:** Animal stewardship and philosophical counterpoint to the village's protective ethos. Does not believe humans who get eaten deserve rescue. Views the resurrection system as interference with natural law.
**Internal tension:** She is inside the village's structure and disagrees with its premise. Her tension is with the Steward most directly — opposite alignments, opposite philosophies about what humans are owed — and with the Commander's Household over the value of protection as a principle.
**Inter-household connections:** Functionally adjacent to Hunt and Hearth via shared forest knowledge. Ideological friction with the Steward's House. Working proximity to the Commander's Household's outer patrol in the Forbidden Forest.
**Trajectory:** Her abstract principles will be tested by specific humans who complicate them.
**Narrative hook:** The forest's creatures trust her. Some of them are the things that eat humans.

---

### 6. The Roost
**Members:** Two dragon-giantesses — Ataraxia (Raxa), the mother, a full-grown dragon who treats Viralys as her personal lair; and Strelitzia (Liza), her daughter, still immature and developing.
**Function:** External link. Ataraxia is the only giantess in the village who can come and go freely — flight clears the corrupted forest entirely. She serves as the village's sole conduit for outside information and goods, controlling that channel entirely and on her own terms. When in the village she lazes, tends to Liza, and expects the space to be hers.
**Internal tension:** Ataraxia is a questionable model for her daughter in specific ways she does not examine — her attitude toward humans as furniture, her possession of Liza's father's soul, her comfort with absolute ownership of things she values. Liza is developing possessive hoarding instincts as she matures; the other giantesses compensate for both of them. Neither Ataraxia nor Liza registers this as a problem.
**Inter-household connections:** Ataraxia's external access creates a structural dependency the rest of the village has quietly built around. She controls what outside information reaches Viralys and what doesn't. Liza's friendship with the human teenager Mir Vlcek is her only real connection to the human population — informal, unsupervised, and increasingly shaped by instincts neither of them fully understands yet.
**Trajectory:** Liza's hoarding behavior will become a practical problem as she grows. Ataraxia's grip on external information is a leverage point no one in the village has moved to address, because doing so would require confronting what she is and what she holds.
**Narrative hook:** Ataraxia carries Liza's father's soul inside her — preserved intact, considered hers. Liza does not know.

---

### 7. The Viralys-Born
**Members:** Humans who grew up in the village. Count TBD — expansion phase.
**Function:** Institutional human memory. They know the giantesses by name, know the unwritten rules, know which behaviors invite danger and which are actually safe. Most deeply embedded in village life.
**Internal tension:** Belonging without ever choosing to. Some are at peace with it. Some have spent their lives not looking at the soul-tree question directly. A few carry resentments with no outlet.
**Inter-household connections:** The humans the giantesses know best and somewhat trust. The Steward's House depends on them for functional village operation. Sophie emerged from this cluster.
**Trajectory:** They will inherit whatever Viralys becomes.
**Narrative hook:** They know quiet, specific things about the giantesses that no outsider ever learns.

---

### 8. The Brought-In
**Members:** Humans rescued and brought to Viralys from outside. Most don't fully know how they arrived — waking up in a bunk with the smell of something organic fading is a common experience. Count TBD — expansion phase.
**Function:** New blood, outside perspective, the most visible ongoing adjustment drama in the human population.
**Internal tension:** Adjustment runs from gratitude to suppressed rage. The ones who discover they can't leave are the hardest cases. The ones who find out how they arrived and still can't leave are harder still.
**Inter-household connections:** Intake through the Steward's House. Primary protection backstop through the Commander's Household. Friction with the Viralys-Born, who regard newcomers with varying degrees of patience.
**Trajectory:** Every brought-in human eventually becomes something — Viralys-Born in waiting, a persistent problem, or a person who found something here they didn't expect.
**Narrative hook:** One of them remembers something about their arrival that they shouldn't.

## Vocabulary

**Seed phase** — Clarification-heavy opening phase. Produces `seed.md`. _Avoid: Foundation phase, setup phase._

**Wide phase** — Expansive generative phase. Produces all character, location, faction, concept, and story notes in `notes/`. _Avoid: Development phase._

**Export phase** — Conversion phase. Packages Wide-phase notes into the target system format. The only phase that writes ainime field names. _Avoid: Deliverables phase._

**seed.md** — World foundation document. Plain prose with natural section headers. Not written in export format.

**Character note** — Comprehensive single source of truth for one character. Richer than any export format.

**Concept note** — A discrete piece of world knowledge in `notes/`. Layer-tagged: surface (freely shareable), mid (contextual), deep (revealed late).

**Story note** — Narrative direction document in `notes/`. Scope hierarchy: direction → arc → intention. Introduction notes have `scope: "[[introduction]]"`.

**Cast planning** — Wide-phase process of planning the full cast before writing individual notes. Produces the cast plan in `worldbuilding-plan.md`.

**Coverage check** — Pre-completion review: main/side ratio, household balance, archetype slot coverage, negative-track characters present.
