# Blinded Review — Writing Style Trial

## Design Notes (reference)

### Session Notes

- Born in Viralys; mother left once she considered Nadja old enough — not very old by human standards, but giantesses reach independence faster; her mother kept watch longer than some; no trauma or bitterness about it
- [[Maja]]'s mother abandoned her young (or at least that's presumed — no one ever saw her); Nadja started looking out for Maja and took a big-sister role, which developed into caretaking more broadly
- The caretaking instinct started with Maja specifically — a kid who needed someone — and grew outward as the village's practical needs gave structure; she didn't set out to become the person who feeds everyone; it accumulated
- Now [FLAGGED: copula_avoidance] kitchen forewoman, feeding both human and giantess populations
- Core fear: if she stops controlling the outcome, someone gets hurt; not just professional diligence but personal — she's between fragile humans and giantess-scale hazards every day; the warmth she won't acknowledge is what makes the stakes feel so high
- Self-image [FLAGGED: ai_vocabulary]: sees what she does as normal, thinks others should be more responsible — while her overbearing nature prevents them from developing that responsibility; creating the problem she's solving
- Unconsciously infantilizes humans; not malicious — just how things are to her; humans are small, fragile, need looking after; she likes them, feeds them, cares individually — but doesn't think of them as fully autonomous
- When things go wrong, doubles down: more rules, more oversight, more hands-on control; human autonomy shrinks further
- Refuses to delegate dangerous tasks even when someone else would do them better; result is she's overworked and occasionally makes the exact mistakes she's trying to prevent; root is the control pattern — can't trust anyone else to be careful enough
- Won't admit she enjoys people having fun at work; bark worse than bite; doesn't notice she softens around people enjoying themselves or that her punishments for minor infractions are lighter than she thinks
- Self-reliant; no one she's personally vulnerable with; always the one holding it together
- Warmth is acts of service she doesn't draw attention to: preparing someone's favorite dish, making sure they remember to eat
- Physical discipline scales with target: [[Maja]] gets hit (durable peer); [[strelitzia-liza|Liza]] gets standard child discipline; humans get contained — pinned under a finger, shoved in a pocket, put on a shelf, held up for a talking-down; constricting but not harmful
- Protectiveness operates within resurrection framework: strict about safety because accidents are disruptive and unpleasant, not because she treats human life as sacred

### Builder Context

[DEFRAME: meta_framing] - Narrative function: the giantess whose caretaking has become control; demonstrates how genuine kindness becomes suffocating when the caretaker cannot let go; the self-reinforcing cycle (accident → tighter grip → overwork → more accidents) is the engine
- The [[Maja]] dynamic is reciprocal across both notes: Maja's Confidant entry describes getting more of Nadja than anyone; Nadja's Kin entry describes the origin of the caretaking instinct
- [[strelitzia-liza|Liza]]'s note labels Nadja as Authority; from Nadja's side the label is Charge — she has self-appointed the responsibility because [[Ataraxia]] isn't around to do it
- [[Melinda]]'s note does not currently have a Nadja relationship entry; Nadja's Obligation entry describes the supply-chain discipline dynamic, which may warrant a reciprocal entry from Melinda's side

## Group W

W-1. She writes a new rule after every kitchen accident and enforces it personally. The humans under her have stopped suggesting alternatives because every suggestion became a procedure they now follow without input. She built this system one incident at a time and sees it as evidence that she is the only one who takes safety seriously.

W-2. She inspects every station before and during service. A cook was burned the one time she skipped a check. She has not finished her own meal during a shift since. The inspections cost her every break and the last hour of every shift, and she has never weighed that cost against the benefit.

W-3. She does everything herself, then tells Maja the humans should take more responsibility. She does not see that she has removed every chance for them to do so. She believes the problem is their lack of effort. The evidence she points to is a situation she created.

W-4. She picks humans up and moves them away from hazards without asking, including hazards they have already noticed. She redirects a reaching hand with the same attentiveness she uses to plate that person's preferred meal later. She does not separate feeding someone from deciding what they can touch.

W-5. She pins a careless human under one finger and corrects them at eye level. She cuffs Maja for the same mistake. She gives Liza a correction scaled for a child. Each response is calibrated to what the body can take. She assigned herself this system and has never questioned her authority to run it.

W-6. She makes specific dishes for specific people without being asked and puts food where lunch-skippers will find it. If someone thanks her, she turns to a task or says she was making it already. She remembers preferences she has never been told directly and denies remembering them when asked.

W-7. She hauls the boiling stockpot herself rather than assign it. She will not let another person handle a load she considers dangerous. She ends every shift fatigued and has caught herself making the same errors she would bench someone else for. She has not traced the fatigue back to the refusal because that trace leads to a premise she will not examine.

W-8. She goes quieter and lets small mistakes pass when the kitchen staff are laughing over their work. She does not notice this change. After the shift she lists what went wrong and calls the day "acceptable." The staff know a good shift by how she sounds, not by what she says.

W-9. She carries every shift without requesting help and does not discuss anything personal outside the kitchen. She holds the operation together alone. No one holds anything for her. She made this arrangement herself and would refuse any attempt to change it.

W-10. She enforces strict safety standards because accidents are disruptive and painful, not because death is permanent. Resurrection exists. A burn or a broken bone is still a failure on her floor. Her standard is operational: nothing goes wrong while she is in charge, and she is always in charge.

## Group Z

Z-1. When something goes wrong in the kitchen, she adds a rule. When the rule does not prevent the next accident, she adds 2 more. The humans who work under her have stopped suggesting fixes because every suggestion gets answered with a tighter procedure she enforces herself.

Z-2. She checks every station, every blade placement, every pot handle angle. She does this because the one time she left a station unchecked, a human cook burned his arm. She cannot name the last time she finished her own meal before the shift ended.

Z-3. She sees herself as the only person doing what needs doing. She tells Maja that the humans would be more careful if they took responsibility. She does not notice that she has removed every opportunity for them to take it.

Z-4. She picks humans up mid-sentence and moves them away from hazards they have already seen. She adjusts a hot pan out of someone's reach while they are reaching for it. She does this with the same attentiveness she uses to plate their favorite dish. The care and the overreach come from the same impulse, and she has never separated them.

Z-5. When a human worker drops something fragile, she pins them under one finger and lectures them at eye level. When Maja breaks something, Maja gets cuffed. When Liza misbehaves, Liza gets a smack appropriate for a child. The correction scales to what each body can take. She has worked this out precisely and does not question why she made it her system to work out.

Z-6. She prepares specific dishes for specific people without being asked. She remembers who skips lunch and puts a plate where they will find it. If someone thanks her directly, she changes the subject or finds a task that needs doing right now.

Z-7. She will not hand off a dangerous task. She hauls the boiling stockpot herself rather than let someone else risk a spill. She ends shifts exhausted and has caught herself making the kind of slip she would bench someone else for. She does not connect these facts.

Z-8. When the kitchen runs smoothly and people are laughing over their work, her voice drops and her corrections come slower. She does not notice this. She would describe the same shift as "acceptable" and point out 3 things that still needed fixing.

Z-9. She carries every shift alone. No one in the kitchen has seen her ask for help. No one outside the kitchen has seen her sit down with someone and talk about anything other than work. She is the person who holds things together, and the cost is that no one holds anything for her.

Z-10. She keeps the kitchen safe because accidents are disruptive and painful, not because she believes human lives hang in the balance. Resurrection exists. She still will not tolerate a burn, a fall, or a broken bone on her floor. The standard is not life or death. The standard is that nothing goes wrong while she is in charge.
