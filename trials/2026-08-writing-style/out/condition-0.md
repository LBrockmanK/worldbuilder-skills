# Writing Style Trial — Condition 0

## PART 1: Synthesized Soul Entries

1. When something goes wrong in the kitchen, she adds a rule. When the rule does not prevent the next accident, she adds 2 more. The humans who work under her have stopped suggesting fixes because every suggestion gets answered with a tighter procedure she enforces herself.

2. She checks every station, every blade placement, every pot handle angle. She does this because the one time she left a station unchecked, a human cook burned his arm. She cannot name the last time she finished her own meal before the shift ended.

3. She sees herself as the only person doing what needs doing. She tells Maja that the humans would be more careful if they took responsibility. She does not notice that she has removed every opportunity for them to take it.

4. She picks humans up mid-sentence and moves them away from hazards they have already seen. She adjusts a hot pan out of someone's reach while they are reaching for it. She does this with the same attentiveness she uses to plate their favorite dish. The care and the overreach come from the same impulse, and she has never separated them.

5. When a human worker drops something fragile, she pins them under one finger and lectures them at eye level. When Maja breaks something, Maja gets cuffed. When Liza misbehaves, Liza gets a smack appropriate for a child. The correction scales to what each body can take. She has worked this out precisely and does not question why she made it her system to work out.

6. She prepares specific dishes for specific people without being asked. She remembers who skips lunch and puts a plate where they will find it. If someone thanks her directly, she changes the subject or finds a task that needs doing right now.

7. She will not hand off a dangerous task. She hauls the boiling stockpot herself rather than let someone else risk a spill. She ends shifts exhausted and has caught herself making the kind of slip she would bench someone else for. She does not connect these facts.

8. When the kitchen runs smoothly and people are laughing over their work, her voice drops and her corrections come slower. She does not notice this. She would describe the same shift as "acceptable" and point out 3 things that still needed fixing.

9. She carries every shift alone. No one in the kitchen has seen her ask for help. No one outside the kitchen has seen her sit down with someone and talk about anything other than work. She is the person who holds things together, and the cost is that no one holds anything for her.

10. She keeps the kitchen safe because accidents are disruptive and painful, not because she believes human lives hang in the balance. Resurrection exists. She still will not tolerate a burn, a fall, or a broken bone on her floor. The standard is not life or death. The standard is that nothing goes wrong while she is in charge.

## PART 2: Variant Spreads

### Entry 1
**Variant A:** When something goes wrong, she adds a rule. When the rule fails, she adds more. The humans under her have stopped suggesting alternatives because each one gets answered with a procedure she wrote herself.
**Variant B:** Every accident produces a new rule. Every new rule removes a decision someone else used to make. The kitchen runs on her procedures now, and the humans in it have less autonomy than they did a year ago.
**Variant C:** To a visitor, the kitchen looks well-run and strictly managed. To the humans who work there, it looks like a place where they follow instructions and wait to be told what to do next. She built it that way one incident at a time.

### Entry 2
**Variant A:** She checks every station before service and again during. She does this because the one time she skipped a check, a cook got hurt. She has not finished her own meal during a shift in months.
**Variant B:** Her station checks cost her meals, breaks, and the last hour of every shift. She cannot stop doing them because the one time she skipped, someone got burned. The cost of vigilance is hers and she pays it daily.
**Variant C:** A new worker sees someone thorough. A returning worker sees someone who will not eat until she has verified every surface, every handle, every blade. The gap between diligence and compulsion closed a long time ago and she did not notice.

### Entry 3
**Variant A:** She tells others they should take more responsibility. She has taken every responsibility away from them. She does not see the connection.
**Variant B:** She complains that no one steps up. She has made stepping up impossible by handling everything herself. The complaint and the cause are the same behavior.
**Variant C:** She sees herself as the only reliable person in the room. She reinforces this by doing everything herself and then pointing out that no one else did it. She believes the evidence.

### Entry 4
**Variant A:** She picks humans up and moves them out of danger without asking. She redirects a reaching hand the same way she plates a favorite dish. The care and the control are the same gesture.
**Variant B:** A human reaching for a hot handle gets their hand redirected before they touch it. The same hand will plate their favorite meal later. She does not distinguish between feeding someone and deciding what they can touch.
**Variant C:** She moves humans away from hazards they have already noticed. She adjusts tools out of reach while someone is using them. She does this gently, carefully, and without asking whether it is necessary. To her, the question does not exist.

### Entry 5
**Variant A:** She pins a careless human under one finger for a lecture. She cuffs Maja for the same mistake. She smacks Liza like a parent would. Each correction fits what the body can handle. She calibrated this system and never questioned why it was hers to calibrate.
**Variant B:** Her discipline scales precisely with the target's durability. Humans get contained, not struck. Maja, a peer, gets hit. Liza, a child, gets child-level correction. The precision shows how much thought she has put into something she never had authority to formalize.
**Variant C:** When a human makes a mistake, they find themselves pinned under a finger or placed on a shelf for a talking-to. When Maja makes a mistake, she gets cuffed. The difference is physical, not emotional. She cares the same amount about both. She has just calculated what each body can take.

### Entry 6
**Variant A:** She makes someone's favorite dish without being asked. She puts a plate where a lunch-skipper will find it. If they thank her, she is already doing something else.
**Variant B:** Her warmth is a plate left where you will find it. A dish prepared the way you like it without having mentioned it. She will not stay for the thank-you and will deny remembering the preference.
**Variant C:** The person who skips lunch finds food at their station. The person who mentioned a craving last week finds it on the menu. If either one thanks her, she says she was making it anyway and turns to scrub a pot that is already clean.

### Entry 7
**Variant A:** She carries the boiling stockpot rather than let someone else risk a spill. She ends every shift exhausted and has caught herself making the same mistakes she benches others for. She does not connect the two.
**Variant B:** She will not delegate a dangerous task. The result is fatigue, and the fatigue produces exactly the accidents she is trying to prevent. She has never traced the cycle back to her own refusal.
**Variant C:** An observer would see a forewoman doing too much. She sees a forewoman doing what is necessary because no one else will be careful enough. The exhaustion and the resulting mistakes are invisible to her because examining them would mean examining the premise.

### Entry 8
**Variant A:** When people laugh over their work, her voice drops and she lets small mistakes slide. She would describe the same shift as merely acceptable and list what still went wrong.
**Variant B:** She does not know she goes easier on people who are enjoying themselves. Her self-report after a good shift focuses on problems. Her actual behavior during it is the softest version of herself anyone sees.
**Variant C:** The kitchen staff know a good shift by how she sounds, not what she says. Her voice gets quieter, her corrections come slower, and minor infractions go unremarked. She would deny every part of this if told.

### Entry 9
**Variant A:** No one has seen her ask for help. No one has seen her talk about anything personal. She holds things together for everyone and no one holds anything for her.
**Variant B:** She is the person everyone leans on and no one checks in on. She has built this arrangement herself and would reject any attempt to change it.
**Variant C:** She carries every shift alone. Outside the kitchen, she does not sit with anyone to talk about things that are not work. The strength is real. The isolation is the price, and she does not acknowledge it as a price.

### Entry 10
**Variant A:** She keeps the kitchen safe because accidents are disruptive, not because she thinks death is at stake. Resurrection exists. Her standard is that nothing goes wrong on her floor, and that standard does not require mortality to justify it.
**Variant B:** Burns, falls, and broken bones are unacceptable to her. Not because they are fatal. Because they are preventable disruptions that happened while she was in charge. The standard is operational, not moral.
**Variant C:** She treats a broken bone and a ruined dish with the same urgency. Both are failures on her watch. Resurrection means death is not the fear. Disorder is the fear. She runs a kitchen where nothing is allowed to go wrong, and the reason is that she is the one running it.
