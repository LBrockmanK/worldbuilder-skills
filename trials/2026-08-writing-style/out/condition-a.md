# Writing Style Trial — Condition A

## PART 1: Synthesized Soul Entries

1. She adds a new rule after each accident. She writes the rule herself. She enforces the rule herself. The humans in the kitchen stop making suggestions. Each suggestion gets a new procedure.

2. She checks every station before service starts. She checks every station again during service. She does this because a cook got burned when she missed a check. She has not eaten during a shift in months.

3. She tells others to take more responsibility. She has removed every chance for them to do so. She does not see this pattern. She believes the problem is their lack of effort.

4. She picks humans up and moves them away from hazards. She does not ask first. She moves a reaching hand away from a hot pan. She plates that person's favorite meal later with the same care. She does not separate the 2 actions.

5. She pins a careless human under one finger. She lectures them at eye level. She cuffs Maja for the same mistake. She gives Liza a light smack. Each correction fits the body it targets. She built this system. She does not question her authority to build it.

6. She makes specific dishes for specific people. She does not wait for a request. She puts a plate at the station of a person who skips lunch. If that person thanks her, she turns away. She finds a task to do.

7. She carries the boiling stockpot herself. She does not let others touch it. She ends every shift tired. She has made the same mistakes she corrects in others. She does not connect her fatigue to her mistakes.

8. Her voice drops when people laugh at work. Her corrections come slower. Small mistakes go unremarked. She does not notice this change. She reports the same shift as "acceptable." She lists 3 things that still need fixing.

9. No one has seen her ask for help. No one has heard her talk about something personal. She holds the kitchen together. No one holds anything for her. She built this arrangement. She would reject a change to it.

10. She keeps the kitchen safe because accidents cause disruption. Resurrection exists. Death is not the risk. Her standard is that nothing goes wrong on her floor. She enforces this standard because she is the one in charge.

## PART 2: Variant Spreads

### Entry 1
**Variant A:** She adds a rule after each accident. She enforces each rule herself. The humans in the kitchen have stopped making suggestions. Each suggestion produces a new procedure.
**Variant B:** Each accident produces a new rule. Each rule removes a decision from someone else. The kitchen has more procedures now than it did a year ago. The humans in it have less authority.
**Variant C:** A visitor sees a well-run kitchen. The humans who work there follow instructions. They wait to be told what to do. She built this system one accident at a time.

### Entry 2
**Variant A:** She checks every station before and during service. She does this because a cook got burned once. She has not eaten her own meal during a shift in months.
**Variant B:** Her station checks take all her breaks and the last hour of each shift. She cannot stop. A cook got burned when she missed a check once. She pays this cost every day.
**Variant C:** A new worker sees a thorough forewoman. A returning worker sees someone who will not eat until she has checked every surface. The gap between diligence and compulsion closed. She did not notice.

### Entry 3
**Variant A:** She says others should take more responsibility. She does everything herself. She does not see the connection between these 2 facts.
**Variant B:** She does everything herself. She then says no one else steps up. The complaint and the cause are the same action.
**Variant C:** She sees herself as the only reliable person in the room. She does all the work. She then notes that no one else did it. She believes what she sees.

### Entry 4
**Variant A:** She picks humans up and moves them from hazards. She does not ask. She moves a reaching hand. She plates a favorite meal with the same care. She does not separate feeding from deciding.
**Variant B:** A human reaches for a hot handle. She moves their hand away. The same hand will serve their favorite meal. She does not tell the difference between care and control.
**Variant C:** She moves humans away from hazards they already see. She takes tools out of reach while someone uses them. She does this gently. She does not ask if it is needed. The question does not occur to her.

### Entry 5
**Variant A:** She pins a careless human under one finger and talks to them at eye level. She cuffs Maja for the same mistake. She gives Liza a child-level smack. Each correction fits what the body can take.
**Variant B:** Humans get contained. Maja gets struck. Liza gets a light correction. The response matches the body. She put thought into this system. She never had the authority to make it.
**Variant C:** A human who makes a mistake gets pinned under a finger or put on a shelf. Maja gets cuffed. The difference is physical. The concern is the same. She has calculated what each body can take.

### Entry 6
**Variant A:** She makes a person's favorite dish. She does not announce this. She puts food where a lunch-skipper will find it. She leaves before they say thank you.
**Variant B:** Her warmth is a plate left at your station. A dish made the way you prefer. She does not stay for thanks. She says she made it already.
**Variant C:** The lunch-skipper finds food at their station. The person who mentioned a craving finds it on the menu. She says she was making it already. She turns to clean a pot that is clean.

### Entry 7
**Variant A:** She carries the boiling pot herself. She will not let others do it. She ends every shift tired. She has caught herself making the mistakes she corrects in others. She does not connect the 2 facts.
**Variant B:** She will not give a dangerous task to others. She gets tired. The tiredness causes the accidents she tries to prevent. She has not traced this cycle to her own refusal.
**Variant C:** An observer sees a forewoman who does too much. She sees a forewoman who does what others will not do safely. She does not see the exhaustion. She does not see the resulting mistakes.

### Entry 8
**Variant A:** When the staff laugh, her voice gets quiet. She lets small mistakes go. She would say the shift was only acceptable. She would list what went wrong.
**Variant B:** She does not know she is easier on people who enjoy their work. After a good shift she lists problems. During the shift she is the softest version of herself.
**Variant C:** The staff measure a good shift by how she sounds. Her voice gets quieter. Her corrections slow down. Small errors go unremarked. She would deny all of this.

### Entry 9
**Variant A:** No one has seen her ask for help. No one has heard her talk about personal matters. She holds things together for everyone. No one does the same for her.
**Variant B:** Everyone depends on her. No one checks on her. She made this arrangement herself. She would refuse any change.
**Variant C:** She carries every shift alone. She does not sit with anyone to talk about things outside work. The strength is real. The isolation is the cost. She does not call it a cost.

### Entry 10
**Variant A:** She keeps the kitchen safe because accidents disrupt operations. Death is not at stake. Resurrection exists. Her standard is that nothing goes wrong while she is in charge.
**Variant B:** Burns and broken bones are not acceptable. They are not fatal. They are preventable failures on her floor. The standard is operational.
**Variant C:** She treats a broken bone and a ruined dish with equal urgency. Both are failures on her watch. Death is not the fear. Disorder is the fear. She is the one in charge, so nothing goes wrong.
