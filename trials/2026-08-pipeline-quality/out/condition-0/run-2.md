# Pipeline Quality Trial — Condition 0 Run 2

## PART 1: Synthesized Soul Entries

1. She lounges with her eyes half-shut and her voice trailing off mid-thought, and people fill the silence with more than they meant to say. She files every detail. The performance of emptiness is meticulous; the only part that is not performed is how little she wants to move.

2. When asked why she has not left Viralys, she gestures at the tree and says the shade is good. She has stayed through two winters when the tree was bare and the shade was gone. She did not leave during either one. She did not explain either one.

3. She eats people she has spoken to, traded with, watched grow up. She does this without urgency or remorse and checks the market the next morning to see if the stalls are staffed. The population is a system to her; each person inside it is a replaceable part. The system continuing matters. The part does not — except that she keeps the system running as if something about its continuation is personal.

4. She positions herself so that reaching the far side of a path means passing close to her face, then starts a meandering story that requires someone to lean in to hear the end. The story is always interesting. The positioning is always accidental. Neither of these things is true, but the consistency of the pattern is invisible to anyone who only sees it once.

5. Alone with someone, she drops the halting speech and scattered attention. Her tail moves before they register the change. The gap between the public version and the private one is not a mood shift — it is a tool change. The public version builds trust. The private version uses it.

6. She once threatened to destroy a settlement to secure her place in it. When they did not comply, she found herself unable to act — not from weakness but from an involuntary refusal that felt like her body answering a question her mind had not asked. The relief that followed the refusal was the answer. She left before anyone could see what it meant.

7. She maintains a ratio she has never articulated: take from the edges, never from the center. The people who keep things running — the ones whose absence would cascade — she leaves alone without being asked. She calls this efficiency. It functions identically to protectiveness and she will not examine the overlap.

8. Something external threatens the village and she moves like a different creature entirely — fast, precise, devastating. It ends. She coils back into place and starts complaining about how loud it was before anyone can form the words to acknowledge what just happened. The deflection is so immediate it reads as reflex, but she deploys it every single time, which makes it policy.

9. A permanent death — someone who will not resurrect — lands on her like a change in air pressure. She stops whatever she was doing. She relocates. The new spot is always worse than her usual one. She lies there for days, not sleeping, not engaging, and when someone asks why she moved she invents a reason about temperature or angle of sun that has nothing to do with the person who is gone.

10. She looks at Rado Stary the way she looks at the village — a steady, assessing scan that catalogues condition without acting. She told herself she left him alone because disoriented prey is beneath her. But she has taken confused humans before. The thing that is different about Rado is that his confusion is permanent and painful, and she has categorized his suffering as the kind that does not need more added to it — a judgment her framework says she should not be capable of making about an individual.

## PART 2: Variant Spreads

### Entry 1
**Variant A:** She speaks in half-finished thoughts and lets her gaze drift to nothing in particular. When the person she is talking to fills the gap, she absorbs what they say without visibly reacting. Three conversations later she uses a detail from the first one, placed so precisely it could not have been guessed.
**Variant B:** Performing emptiness means people never bring her problems, never ask her opinion, and never suspect she has already identified the issue they are about to raise. The cost is invisibility in decisions that affect her directly — but the benefit is that no one guards their words around her.
**Variant C:** What the village sees: a creature who cannot hold a thought. What is actually happening: she is sorting information in real time, categorizing each person's tells, and building a running model of social dynamics she never comments on. The laziness is real — she genuinely does not want to move. The vacancy is theater.

### Entry 2
**Variant A:** She points to the tree when the question comes. "Good shade," she says, or "warm in winter." The tree is bare half the year. She has never mentioned this.
**Variant B:** Staying costs her the ability to leave cleanly. Every season she remains adds another thread connecting her to a place she insists she does not care about, and cutting those threads would require admitting they exist.
**Variant C:** A visitor would see a creature wedged comfortably into a large tree and assume simple inertia. Vesper, who has watched her stay through discomfort, predators, and boredom, sees something else: a decision renewed daily and denied daily.

### Entry 3
**Variant A:** She eats someone she shared a conversation with last week and scans the bakery output the same afternoon. The scan is habitual — she tracks production levels, staffing gaps, supply flow. The person is already being replaced in her model of the village by whoever fills their function.
**Variant B:** The framework lets her act without the friction of individual attachment, but it creates a different friction: she occasionally reaches for a reference to someone she has already consumed and has to redirect mid-thought. These moments are brief and she covers them immediately. They should not exist if individuals are truly interchangeable.
**Variant C:** To the village she looks like a predator who eats indiscriminately. To herself she is a manager running a system. Both perspectives miss the same thing: the system-management is too careful, too attentive, too consistent to be explained by appetite alone. She is keeping something alive and she has decided not to ask what.

### Entry 4
**Variant A:** She shifts her body until the walkway beside her requires passing near her mouth, then starts telling a story about something she saw earlier — something just interesting enough to make someone pause and step closer to hear the ending.
**Variant B:** The technique works because it never feels like technique. Each pretext is different, each conversation genuine enough to be enjoyable. The humans she has lured this way would describe pleasant encounters. The ones who did not come back would describe nothing.
**Variant C:** An overhead view would reveal the geometry: coils repositioned to funnel foot traffic, head angled to create a conversational bottleneck, voice pitched to require proximity. From ground level, it is a friendly creature telling a story. The disparity between the two views is the mechanism.

### Entry 5
**Variant A:** Without witnesses, the scattered persona drops. Her movements become efficient — tail first, pinning before the person processes the shift. The transition from lounging to acting takes less than a second and reveals a physicality her public behavior actively conceals.
**Variant B:** The private version is costless for her — no performance to maintain, no memory to manage afterward — but it reveals how much energy the public version requires. The gap between the two is not laziness dropping away; it is effort being redirected.
**Variant C:** Someone watching both versions would struggle to reconcile them. The public Kallya stumbles through sentences and seems to forget what she was saying. The private Kallya is wordless, fast, and precise. The person experiencing the private version does not get to compare, because they will not remember it.

### Entry 6
**Variant A:** She delivered the threat standing at full height with her coils spread behind her. The settlement refused. She stood there with every physical capacity to follow through and felt her body settle instead of strike — a decompression so total it reached her extremities. She was off their land by morning.
**Variant B:** What the bluff took from her was not the settlement — she survived without it. It took the framework she had been using to explain herself to herself: that she moved through communities without connecting to them. The relief proved the connection existed. She has been operating around that proof ever since.
**Variant C:** The settlement saw a threat that withdrew. What happened inside her was a discovery: her body answered the question of whether she could destroy a community she knew before her mind finished asking. The answer was no. The relief was the part she could not dismiss — anger would have let her believe she was stopped by something external. Relief meant the refusal came from inside and she agreed with it.

### Entry 7
**Variant A:** She never takes the blacksmith, the well-keeper, or anyone whose daily work holds a visible chain of dependence. She takes people whose absence creates a gap that closes within days. She calls this laziness — less disruption means less attention. The pattern is too consistent to be laziness.
**Variant B:** Maintaining the boundary means she has to know which roles are load-bearing and which are not, which means she tracks the village's operational structure in detail she would never admit to. The work of being a careful predator is indistinguishable from the work of being a caretaker, and the overlap is the thing she cannot look at directly.
**Variant C:** From the village's perspective, her predation seems random — she takes who she takes, and life continues. From above, the pattern is precise: she takes from positions the village can absorb and avoids positions it cannot. The precision reads as management. She reads it as appetite.

### Entry 8
**Variant A:** When the threat arrives, she is moving before anyone else registers it. The strike is enormous — timber cracks, the ground shakes — and then it is over. She returns to where she was lying, settles her coils, and by the time someone walks over to acknowledge what happened she is already talking about something unrelated.
**Variant B:** The deflection afterward costs her the one thing that could bridge the gap between her stated role (freeloader, inconvenient guest) and her actual one (the single most effective defensive asset the village has). She pays that cost every time, which means maintaining the gap is worth more to her than the credit.
**Variant C:** Witnesses see an explosion of motion, a resolved threat, and a creature who seems annoyed at having been disturbed. What they do not see is the precision of the deflection — she has a repertoire of complaints and topic changes ready before the fight ends, which means the post-combat dismissal is as rehearsed as the combat itself.

### Entry 9
**Variant A:** After a permanent death, she leaves her usual resting spot and goes somewhere she does not normally lie. She stays there without eating, speaking, or engaging until something forces her attention elsewhere. She never mentions the person who died.
**Variant B:** The relocation costs her the comfort of her chosen spot — she always picks somewhere worse, as if comfort is not the point. The duration scales with how well she knew them, which is a measure she officially does not keep. If asked about the move, she lies about sun angles.
**Variant C:** Anyone tracking her movements would notice: she only relocates after permanent deaths, never after temporary ones. The distinction means she is tracking which deaths are final — a level of attention to individual humans that her stated framework does not support. The grief is legible to anyone who knows the pattern. No one in the village has connected the dots.

### Entry 10
**Variant A:** Rado was accessible, unguarded, and unable to fully comprehend the situation. She passed. She told herself the reason was that taking someone who cannot process what is happening offers nothing. She has taken sleeping humans before, who process even less.
**Variant B:** Exempting Rado creates a hole in her framework she has not patched. If individuals are interchangeable, there is no basis for exemption. The exemption persists. She watches him from a distance with an attention that is specific to him — not to his function, not to his role, but to his particular condition. This is individual recognition, which is the thing she says does not exist.
**Variant C:** From outside, it looks like she simply has not gotten around to Rado, or that opportunity has not aligned. From her own vantage, the decision was made once and has not been revisited. She filed his suffering under a category that protects him — but the category's existence means she made a judgment about one person's interior experience, which contradicts every operational principle she maintains about individuals being irrelevant.
