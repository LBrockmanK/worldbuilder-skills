# Pipeline Quality Trial — Condition 0 Run 1

## PART 1: Synthesized Soul Entries

1. She asks idle questions in a voice that wanders, lets the answer trail off, then circles back minutes later with a follow-up that proves she caught every word. The people around her mistake inattention for absence; by the time they realize she was listening, the conversation has already given her what she needed.

2. She tells Vesper she stays because the tree is warm and the location is convenient. When someone suggests she could find warmer trees elsewhere, she changes the subject with a yawn that comes half a second too fast. She has never packed. She has never scouted another site.

3. She watches the village the way a shepherd counts a flock at dusk — scanning for gaps, not faces. A missing stall or a silent workshop draws her eye before a missing person does. She will eat someone she spoke to yesterday and check on the baker's output the same afternoon, because the baker feeds forty people and the person she ate fed themselves.

4. She shifts her coils to block a doorway, stretches until the path narrows to a gap beside her mouth, and mentions how tired she is of the cold draft from outside. Someone always offers to come closer and close the gap. She thanks them warmly. The pattern repeats because no one compares notes.

5. When no one else is around and a human stumbles into her space, the playful preamble drops. She pins them with her tail before they finish their sentence. Afterward she repositions, checks the area, and waits — the gap in the village's memory resets when they return from resurrection, and she knows exactly how long that takes.

6. At a previous settlement she told them she would destroy them all unless they made room for her. They called the bluff. She stood there with the power to do it and felt her body refuse. She left that night. The thing that stopped her was not conscience — it was the physical sensation of relief at not having to follow through, and that relief told her something about herself she has been running from since.

7. She draws a line she has never spoken aloud: she will take anyone, but she will not take everyone. The boundary is not moral — it is structural. A community can lose individuals and continue; it cannot lose its continuity and rebuild. She operates inside that margin without examining why the margin matters to her, because examining it leads back to the settlement she left.

8. When something outside the village threatens it — a creature, a hostile party, a collapsing structure — she moves with a speed and force that contradicts every hour of lounging. The threat ends. She returns to her spot, coils settling, and when someone tries to thank her she talks over them about the weather or complains that the noise woke her up.

9. When a human she has known dies and does not come back, she stops talking mid-sentence if she was speaking. She moves to a different spot in the village — not her usual one — and lies there without sleeping. She does not mention the person. If someone else mentions them, she looks away and waits for the sentence to end.

10. She had every opportunity to take Rado Stary and chose not to, telling herself the reason was that confused prey offers no satisfaction. The actual reason is visible in how she watches him: the same sideways attention she gives the village itself, the quiet inventory of someone whose specific suffering she has catalogued and decided not to add to.

## PART 2: Variant Spreads

### Entry 1
**Variant A:** She asks drifting questions with half-closed eyes and lets people talk past her silence. Minutes later she drops a detail from their answer into an unrelated remark, proving she tracked every word while appearing to track none.
**Variant B:** The cost of performing vacancy is that nobody consults her. Decisions get made without her input because the village assumes she has none, and she lets them, because the alternative — showing she understood before they finished explaining — would end the act.
**Variant C:** From outside, she looks like someone dozing through a conversation. From inside, she is sorting what she hears into categories: useful, irrelevant, dangerous. The person talking to her sees a yawning face. The person who tries to lie to her finds out later that the yawn was the most attentive part.

### Entry 2
**Variant A:** She credits the tree's warmth and the lack of better options when anyone asks why she is still here. When someone points out she has never looked for alternatives, she stretches and says she is too comfortable to bother.
**Variant B:** The fiction costs her credibility — Vesper has seen her stay through cold seasons when the tree offers no warmth at all, and neither of them addresses the gap between the stated reason and the visible fact.
**Variant C:** To an outsider she looks like a squatter who found a convenient spot and stopped moving. To Vesper, who has watched her turn down opportunities to leave, the convenience explanation wore thin years ago. Kallya keeps offering it anyway, because the alternative explanation is one she has not rehearsed.

### Entry 3
**Variant A:** She scans the village at intervals, counting activities rather than individuals — the market running, the forge lit, the well in use. A gap in the pattern matters; a gap in the headcount does not, unless the missing head ran something essential.
**Variant B:** The cost is that she genuinely does not track individuals unless they anchor something communal. She ate a tanner last season and only noticed the shortage of leather two weeks later, then spent effort finding someone to replace the function without once mentioning the person who used to fill it.
**Variant C:** She looks like she is lounging and ignoring the village. She is actually running a continuous inventory of systems — food supply, structural maintenance, population flow — and the individual humans inside those systems are interchangeable components to her. The village sees indifference; the village is being managed.

### Entry 4
**Variant A:** She rearranges her coils until the path beside her narrows to a slot near her face, then complains about a draft or mentions something interesting she saw earlier. The human steps closer to hear. The conversation is the mechanism; the position is the trap.
**Variant B:** The pretexts work because she never repeats the same one twice and never rushes. The humans who fall for it remember a friendly, scattered interaction. The ones who do not come back from resurrection remember nothing at all.
**Variant C:** An observer sees a large, sleepy creature making idle conversation. The human approaching sees someone warm and disarming. Neither of them sees the geometry — how the coils have narrowed the exit, how the head has tilted to put the mouth at exactly the right angle, how the conversation's topic has been chosen to require the human to lean in.

### Entry 5
**Variant A:** When she is alone with a human and no one is watching, the rambling speech and lazy posture vanish. She pins them with her tail before they can orient themselves, and the efficiency of it is jarring against every other interaction anyone has had with her.
**Variant B:** The privacy changes the calculus — no witnesses means no persona to maintain and no questions afterward. The human returns from resurrection with no memory of the encounter, and she has already been back in her lounging position long enough that no one notices the gap.
**Variant C:** The version of her that the village knows takes her time, makes conversation, lets things unfold. The version that emerges when no one is watching is fast, direct, and mechanical. The difference is not mood — it is audience. The performance is for the community; the efficiency is for herself.

### Entry 6
**Variant A:** She stood in front of a settlement she had threatened, holding the power to end them, and felt her muscles unlock instead of strike. She left that night without explaining. The relief that flooded through her when she realized she could not do it was the thing she has been avoiding ever since — it proved she was attached to a group of people she had been treating as leverage.
**Variant B:** What the bluff cost her was not the settlement — she lost that anyway by leaving. What it cost her was the ability to pretend she did not care about groups of people. The body's refusal and the relief that followed it were evidence she could not argue with, so she stopped arguing and started ignoring.
**Variant C:** The settlement saw a predator who backed down. She experienced something worse: a predator who discovered she had limits she had not chosen. The relief was involuntary, immediate, and legible to her own body. She has never tried that bluff again — not because it failed, but because it succeeded in showing her what she is.

### Entry 7
**Variant A:** She will take any individual without hesitation, but the idea of depleting a community to collapse triggers the same physical refusal she felt at the previous settlement. She manages her intake the way someone manages a resource — not out of compassion, but out of something she calls practicality that looks suspiciously like investment.
**Variant B:** The boundary costs her nothing in practice — she does not eat fast enough to threaten a population's continuity. But she maintains it as a principle anyway, because the principle lets her avoid asking why she cares whether a community survives after she has moved on. She has moved on before. The communities she left still exist. She checks without admitting she checks.
**Variant C:** To observers, her restraint looks like moderation or laziness. She frames it that way herself. The actual mechanism is architectural: she treats each community as a structure that can absorb individual losses but not structural ones, and she monitors load-bearing roles the way an engineer monitors supports. The monitoring is not merciful. It is the behavior of someone who needs the structure to continue for reasons she will not name.

### Entry 8
**Variant A:** When an external threat reaches the village, she uncoils and strikes with a force that cracks timber and sends debris scattering. The engagement lasts seconds. She returns to her spot, resettles, and if anyone approaches with gratitude she interrupts them with a complaint about the noise or asks whether anyone is going to clean up the mess she just made.
**Variant B:** The deflection after action costs her the recognition that might anchor her more honestly in the community. People learn not to thank her because she makes it uncomfortable, so her contribution stays unofficial — which is exactly the arrangement she needs to maintain the fiction that she is here for the tree.
**Variant C:** Bystanders see a sudden, violent intervention followed by an immediate return to apparent indolence. The speed gap is disorienting — nothing about her daily behavior predicts that kind of lethality. What they do not see is that the deflection afterward is just as deliberate as the strike: she is managing their perception of her as carefully after the fight as she was during it.

### Entry 9
**Variant A:** When a human she recognized stops appearing and she learns they are not coming back, she goes quiet wherever she is, relocates to a part of the village she does not usually occupy, and stays there without engaging anyone until something else requires her attention.
**Variant B:** The grief she will not name costs her the comfort of her routine — the spot she relocates to is never as good as her usual one, and she stays there long enough for it to be noticeable. She never explains the move. If pressed, she claims the old spot got too sunny.
**Variant C:** Anyone watching sees a large creature moving to a new resting place for no apparent reason. The timing — always following a permanent death, never following a resurrection — is a pattern only someone tracking both would notice. She is not hiding the grief. She is expressing it in a language the village has not learned to read.

### Entry 10
**Variant A:** She passed on taking Rado when the opportunity was simple and risk-free, offering herself the explanation that disoriented prey lacks appeal. She still watches him with a quality of attention she does not give to humans she considers interchangeable — a steady, cataloguing focus that tracks his condition without acting on it.
**Variant B:** The decision not to take him costs her nothing materially but creates a precedent she cannot explain within her own framework. If individuals do not matter and the community absorbs losses, there is no reason to exempt one. The exemption exists. She does not interrogate it.
**Variant C:** To anyone observing, she simply never happens to be near Rado when conditions would allow her to act. The avoidance looks like coincidence. The attention she pays him from a distance — noting when he is distressed, tracking who is near him — looks like the same general scanning she does for the village. It is not. It is specific, and the specificity is the thing her framework says should not exist.
