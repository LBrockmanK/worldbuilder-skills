# PART 1: Character Note

# Kallya

## Background

- **The settlement that stood its ground [high charge]** — Kallya once demanded lodging from a human settlement and hinted that refusal would make everyone there a meal. The residents refused anyway. She left instead of killing them, and the relief she felt taught her that she could hunt people without being willing to erase the group they belonged to.
- **Vesper's tree [mid charge]** — Before Viralys had a name, Kallya found Vesper's tree and made herself comfortable there. Other homes gathered around the tree, so she became part of the village before she ever chose to join one.
- **Work turned into titles [low charge]** — Her tail picked up approaching danger, and a trick for drawing humans near also supplied material useful to local brewers. Viralys later called her a guard and a source of alchemical goods; the Steward's House also put her name on its rolls, though she does none of its work.
- **Rado left alive [mid charge]** — Kallya once had an easy chance to eat Rado Stary and passed it up. She said a person who could not grasp the hunt offered no sport, and kept the pity that stopped her out of the explanation.
- **The missing places [high charge, suppressed]** — She has outlived humans she knew who could not return. Each lasting death has made her abandon a favored resting place for another part of the village, without telling anyone why.

## Body

- **Scale and passage.** When Kallya crosses Viralys, she feeds her 65-meter tail through one bend at a time and waits before shifting the next coil. At 81 meters from head to tail, with a torso the size of a 30–35-meter giantess, she is the village's heaviest resident; trying to drag her where she does not wish to go would tear through the homes around her.
- **Care hidden as idleness.** When people, ladders, or loose goods lie near her, she rests her hands and calls people over instead of snatching at them. She looks too lazy to reach, while the still pose keeps a quick movement from snapping ropes, throwing objects, or crushing a bystander.
- **Warmth and stillness.** When the ground is warm, she spreads her coils in the sun and can remain there for most of the day. Cold patches make her gather herself and seek another place, so temperature can steer her even when commands cannot.
- **Warning through the ground.** When heavy steps or impacts travel toward Viralys, she feels the pattern along her tail, raises her head, and points out the direction before most people hear anything. She can tell that something large is coming, but fine detail and distant intent remain beyond what the tremor tells her.
- **Force without training.** When a threat reaches the village, Kallya pins, crushes, bites, or blocks it with her mass, choosing the shortest movement that will end the danger. She has no magic and no drilled fighting style; an able fighter can draw out her reach, but cannot safely meet her weight head-on.
- **Slow digestion.** When she swallows a human, her body takes a long time to finish the meal. The victim remains trapped and aware well after the struggle ends, and Kallya lingers over their movement and fear because she enjoys both.

## Soul

- **Reading from behind a blank look — psychological pattern.** When someone approaches with a request, Kallya lets her attention wander, asks for simple points again, and gives them room to correct her. While they lower their guard, she tracks who speaks for the group, who fears her, and what each person will trade to get an answer.
- **A hunt built from small agreements — psychological pattern.** When she wants a human in her mouth, she starts with a harmless favor: inspect a tooth, collect a sample, settle a wager. Each yes makes the next step seem smaller, and she changes the story when suspicion catches up so the target cannot tell whether she is joking, confused, or ready to bite.
- **Keeping the count — psychological pattern.** When choosing prey, she watches births, returns, work crews, and recent losses before taking anyone. A healthy village earns her restraint at the level of the group, not safety for any one member; she keeps the population going and eats within what she thinks it can replace.
- **Using the blank after death — psychological pattern.** When no witness can hold her to an account, she may skip the game and take someone outright. If that person returns without the memory of the killing, she treats the missing memory as room to try again, though repeated absences and public fear can still force her to ease off.
- **Real laziness, chosen act — psychological pattern.** When nothing presses her, she truly would rather sleep, sun herself, or let someone else finish the work. The foolish questions and missed cues are added on purpose; the wish to avoid effort is the part she never has to fake.
- **Company without a claim — social behavior.** When she enjoys someone nearby, she opens a safe place among her coils, lets talk interrupt her rest, and remembers what they told her next time. She calls the arrangement convenient or says they happened to choose the same shade, keeping friendship out of the words.
- **Pressure on a whole group — social behavior.** When humans face her together, she tests them with teasing threats and watches whether they scatter, bargain, or stand firm. She may single out one person later, but she leaves the group a way to continue; a crowd that holds together brings her old bluff to an early stop.
- **Answering danger, refusing thanks — social behavior.** When an outside threat enters Viralys, her slow voice and loose posture vanish, and she uses enough force to end it quickly. Once the village is safe, she goes back to her warm spot and calls the defense a way to protect her sleep.
- **Cold ground closing in — standing pressure.** Cooler places have begun appearing where she most likes to lie, and she shifts her routines to find warmth without asking who benefits from moving her. The changes feel like bad luck to her, while Leif Holtveld's hidden devices are starting to guide where her great weight settles.
- **Making herself part of the place — core want.** When village life goes on around her, she learns its daily movement, keeps paths open, warns of danger, and makes room for familiar company. She seeks a place in the shared routine while speaking as if the tree, the heat, and easy food are all that bind her to it.
- **Hiding the proof of care — core fear.** When thanks, grief, or a pointed question might show that one of her choices came from care, she supplies a lesser reason at once: appetite, comfort, sport, or timing. If the proof will not go away, she leaves the talk or moves her body elsewhere rather than let another person name what mattered to her.
- **The convenient perch — false belief.** Kallya says she stays because Vesper's tree suits her body and Viralys makes life easy. Yet when comfort would favor leaving, she changes her habits, guards the settlement, and bears inconvenience to remain near the people, then counts each choice as upkeep on a good resting place.
- **The person who becomes too specific — value-conflict stance.** When a known human begs for mercy, Kallya normally finishes the hunt even if their fear reaches her, because sparing every person she recognizes would undo the rule that lets her feed while guarding the village. An exception appears only when she can no longer reduce that person to one replaceable life—when their own way of thinking, hurting, or trusting becomes too clear to push aside. She spares them, or feels the loss if she does not, then blames poor sport, bad hunger, or bad timing and takes her rest somewhere else.
- **Helpless pose, exact control — contradiction.** She invites others to treat her as a harmless fool, yet shapes talks by noticing the need behind every correction and choosing when to misunderstand. The pose protects her freedom because people cannot easily challenge a plan they are unsure she has.
- **Predator and keeper — contradiction.** She tends the human population, protects its future, and destroys threats that could wipe it out; she also eats known villagers for pleasure and enjoys their struggle. She does not seek to settle the clash. Her rule keeps the village alive while allowing her to keep hunting inside it.

# PART 2: Variant Spreads

## Background Variants

### The settlement that stood its ground

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When a settlement refused her lodging after she hinted she might eat everyone, Kallya waited for fear to win. The residents held their answer, and she turned away rather than carry out the threat.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Leaving cost her the shelter and broke the image of a threat that always follows through. The relief that came with the loss showed her that preserving a human group mattered more than winning the bluff.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** The settlement saw a giantess retreat after being called a liar. Kallya felt herself escape a killing she had never wanted to commit and learned that a whole community could matter to her even while its members remained prey.

### Vesper's tree

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** Before houses stood at Viralys, Kallya found Vesper's tree, settled her coils through it, and stayed while humans began building nearby.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** By remaining as the settlement grew, she let paths, work, and daily habits form around her body. Leaving later would mean giving up more than a comfortable tree.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** New residents found Kallya already lounging in the place that became their center. She says the village gathered around her by chance, though years of staying made her one of its first fixed points.

### Work turned into titles

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When her tail caught distant impacts, Kallya warned the village; when a mouth-related lure produced useful bodily matter, people collected it. Officials later named these habits guard work and alchemical supply.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** The titles give the village a way to claim her help without making her attend meetings or patrol on command. Her Steward's House post adds a name to a list and no labor to its halls.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Records show a town guard, a supplier, and a Steward's House member. In practice, Kallya lies in the sun, reports tremors when she feels them, permits collection when it suits her, and ignores the House.

### Rado left alive

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When Rado was alone and easy to take, Kallya moved him out of danger instead of putting him in her mouth. She explained that prey must understand the chase for it to count.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Sparing him denied her an effortless meal and left a clear exception inside her rule about expendable people. She paid that cost rather than add fear to suffering he could not make sense of.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Others hear her dismiss Rado as poor sport. What stops her is the particular shape of his distress, which she can recognize but will not call pity.

### The missing places

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When a human she knows dies and does not return, Kallya notices the empty route or missing voice, gathers her coils, and chooses another place to lie.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** The move gives up a warm or familiar resting place and changes her daily path. It also lets her avoid admitting that one death altered the village for her.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** A neighbor sees Kallya tire of one patch of ground and claim another. She is putting distance between herself and the place where she expects someone who is gone for good.

## Body Variants

### Scale and passage

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When Kallya must cross a built-up part of Viralys, she draws her tail through turn by turn, sets each coil, and waits for nearby structures to stop shaking.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Careful travel is slow, but a faster shift from an 81-meter body could flatten a roof or tear a wall loose. Anyone trying to force her whole weight aside would damage the village before moving her far.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** From a distance she looks like an idle body taking up roads and roots. Up close, each pause places 65 meters of tail where homes, paths, and people can survive it.

### Care hidden as idleness

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When she wants a person closer, Kallya leaves her hands where they rest and coaxes the person to approach her face.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Persuasion takes longer than grabbing, but it avoids the sweep of an arm that could snap a rope ladder, scatter a stall, or strike someone she was not hunting.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Humans see someone too lazy to reach for what she wants. Her lack of motion is a safety choice, even when the safe approach ends at her teeth.

### Warmth and stillness

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When sunlight heats the ground, Kallya uncoils across it and stays until shade or cold makes her move.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Her need for warmth narrows the places where she will rest and makes cold ground one of the few ways to dislodge her without force.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** The village sees long naps and slow shifts from one sunny patch to another. Her body is following heat closely enough that a small change in temperature can redirect its largest resident.

### Warning through the ground

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When a large impact sends a tremor under her coils, Kallya raises her head, names its direction, and warns whoever is close enough to act.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Her early notice buys the village time, though she must wait for more movement or use her eyes to learn what made the signal.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Others may notice nothing before Kallya breaks off a nap and points toward the woods. She is reading force and direction through her tail, not identifying the intruder by magic.

### Force without training

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When an attacker reaches the village, Kallya blocks its path with a coil, pins it under her weight, or closes with hands and teeth.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Her strength can end a close fight at once, but broad turns and unpracticed attacks give a trained foe chances to stay outside her best reach.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** A crushed enemy makes her look unbeatable. Her advantage comes from size and force; there is no spell, drilled form, or fine plan behind the blow.

### Slow digestion

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When she swallows someone, Kallya settles down and pays attention to the struggling long after the person has disappeared from view.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Her slow body leaves prey alive inside her for an extended time, turning one quick capture into a long death.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Outside, she appears to have finished eating and gone back to rest. Inside, the victim is still trapped, and Kallya is enjoying every sign that they know it.

## Soul Variants

### Reading from behind a blank look — psychological pattern

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When someone asks her for something, Kallya blinks, repeats an obvious question, and lets them explain more than they meant to while she notes who takes charge and who gives way.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Acting slow makes others discount her and sometimes wastes time, but it gives her their wants and fears before she has to show her own aim.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** An observer sees a woman losing track of a simple talk. Kallya is following the shifts in voice, rank, and fear with care and choosing which cue to miss aloud.

### A hunt built from small agreements — psychological pattern

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When Kallya chooses a human, she asks for one safe step toward her, then another, changing the excuse whenever the target starts to pull back.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** The long setup costs more time than a grab, but each agreement makes resistance harder and keeps nearby people unsure when the hunt began.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** The target may see a string of silly requests that got out of hand. Kallya has been setting the distance, the choices, and the moment of the bite from the first favor.

### Keeping the count — psychological pattern

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When hunger turns her attention toward the village, she checks who has returned, which homes have lost workers, and how many people the next season can replace before choosing prey.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** The count protects the settlement from being eaten down past recovery, but it makes one person's terror easy for her to treat as an allowed loss.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Villagers see that she sometimes passes up an easy meal after studying the streets. She is guarding the life of the population, not granting a right to the person she leaves alone.

### Using the blank after death — psychological pattern

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When no one else is watching, Kallya may take a human without the usual lure, then approach them again after resurrection once the killing has vanished from their memory.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** The missing memory shields her from one witness, but patterns of disappearances, friends who remember, and a drop in the village count can still expose the hunt.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** The restored person meets a lounging giantess with no memory of what she did to them. Kallya remembers the whole event and uses their blank place as another opening.

### Real laziness, chosen act — psychological pattern

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When work can wait or pass to someone else, Kallya remains in the sun and offers a vague excuse. When she needs an advantage, she adds missed points and foolish guesses to the same slow manner.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Her true dislike of effort leaves formal duties undone and makes others carry routine work. The added act also keeps them from knowing when she has understood too much.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** People see one broad kind of uselessness. Part of it is honest reluctance to move; part is a careful performance that lets her listen without being measured in return.

### Company without a claim — social behavior

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When someone she likes comes near, Kallya shifts a coil to make a sheltered place, listens to their news, and leaves the space open on later visits.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Welcoming company interrupts the rest she values, while calling it shared shade lets her keep the bond without having to answer for it.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** She says a familiar visitor is merely using an empty spot. Her remembered questions and careful space show that she expected and wanted them there.

### Pressure on a whole group — social behavior

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When humans gather against her, Kallya jokes about what she might do and watches for the first person to break ranks. If the group holds, she cuts the threat short and seeks a smaller target elsewhere.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Backing away from a united crowd costs her leverage and a possible meal, but it keeps her from destroying the lasting body she believes the people form together.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** The crowd sees a bully testing whether they scare easily. Beneath the test, their unity marks a line she learned she will not cross.

### Answering danger, refusing thanks — social behavior

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When something threatens Viralys from outside, Kallya stops drawing out her words, moves into its path, and ends the attack with as few strikes as she can.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Fighting risks her comfort and can force her to use the body she prefers to keep still. Refusing thanks afterward preserves her claim that she acted only to protect her resting place.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** During the attack, residents see quick judgment and full force. Once it is over, they see the village's guard return to sunbathing and deny that guarding anyone was the point.

### Cold ground closing in — standing pressure

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When a favored patch turns cool, Kallya gathers her coils and searches for warmer ground, changing where and when she rests.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Each shift gives up comfort and quietly places her mass where someone else wants it, while she lacks a clear act of force to resist.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Kallya sees uneven weather and bad resting spots. Leif Holtveld is testing cooling devices that use her own wish for warmth to move her without her knowledge.

### Making herself part of the place — core want

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When the village begins its daily work, Kallya follows familiar footfalls, warns of approaching trouble, and keeps room beside her for people she knows.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Taking part ties her habits to people who can leave or die and makes departure harder, even though belonging is what keeps her there.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** She looks like a resident enjoying free shade and easy meals. Her constant small adjustments place her inside the village's routine and keep that routine close to her.

### Hiding the proof of care — core fear

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When someone thanks her for saving them or asks why a death changed her habits, Kallya names a selfish reason, makes a joke, or leaves the exchange.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Protecting the lie keeps others from demanding tenderness, but it also prevents her from grieving openly or accepting the place she already holds among them.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Others hear that she acted for sleep, food, or amusement. The speed and care of the excuse show that being known as someone who cares feels more dangerous than the deed itself.

### The convenient perch — false belief

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When asked why she remains, Kallya points to the tree, the warm ground, and the short trip to food, then changes her own routine to stay when any of those comforts fail.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Calling Viralys convenient lets her avoid a promise, but she accepts cold, work, and danger that convenience alone would tell her to leave behind.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** She presents herself as a guest who never found a reason to move on. Her repeated choice of the people over an easier place shows that the guest has made a home she cannot name.

### The person who becomes too specific — value-conflict stance

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When a familiar human pleads during a hunt, Kallya follows through unless some known detail—their trust, their way of understanding, their particular hurt—makes it impossible to treat them as one more replaceable life. If she stops, she calls the prey dull; if she does not, she later rests elsewhere.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Holding to her rule preserves the life she has built as both keeper and hunter, but it can leave her carrying a loss that the village count says should mean nothing. Making an exception saves one person and exposes a crack in the rule.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** An observer hears mercy granted or withheld for reasons of sport, appetite, or timing. The true divide is whether Kallya knows the person well enough that their suffering remains singular after she tries to count it as ordinary.

### Helpless pose, exact control — contradiction

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When a talk could limit her choices, Kallya plays confused, invites correction, and uses what people reveal to guide the exchange where she wants it.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Being treated as foolish costs her open respect, but that low expectation gives her more room to act without challenge.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** Her listeners think they are keeping a drifting mind on course. She is deciding which misunderstanding will make them disclose the next useful thing.

### Predator and keeper — contradiction

- **Variant A — trigger and action:** When the village as a whole is threatened, Kallya protects it; when one human falls within the losses she thinks it can bear, she may hunt and eat that person herself.
- **Variant B — cost or consequence:** Her care keeps the population alive while her appetite makes that same care unsafe for every member. Neither side cancels the harm or the protection done by the other.
- **Variant C — seen versus happening:** From one street she looks like the shield of Viralys, and from another she is the thing people need shielding from. Kallya accepts both views as long as the village survives and she remains free to feed.
