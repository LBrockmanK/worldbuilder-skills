# PART 1: Character Note

## Background

- [Born in Viralys; her mother left after waiting until Nadja could manage on her own] -> Nadja treats early independence as ordinary and does not read that departure as a wound. She assumes a person should become useful quickly, then keeps watch over them anyway. [charge: low]
- [Maja grew up without a mother present, and Nadja stepped in] -> Nadja learned care by noticing one child without a steady adult and making herself the steady adult. What began as checking that Maja ate and got home safely became a habit of finding the next unattended need. [charge: high]
- [She prevented an early kitchen accident, then later caused one after taking on too much herself] -> She trusts systems she can personally enforce, while hiding the evidence that her own exhaustion can break those systems. [charge: mid / high, buried]

## Body

Nadja works at giantess scale with her attention fixed on the small people near her hands. When a human crosses a work area, she stops what she is doing, cups them away from heat or moving loads, and puts them somewhere she considers safe before she resumes. Because she judges safety by whether she can see and reach it, a human may spend a conversation under her fingertip, in her pocket, or on a shelf while she gives instructions.

In the kitchen, she sets out portions, checks containers, and tracks who has eaten before she lets herself take a break. When a task carries a risk of burns, falls, or a dropped tool, she takes it from the nearest worker even if that worker has better hands for it. Her knowledge covers food, traffic through a crowded kitchen, and the limits of giantess-sized equipment; it stops at letting another person set the risk level for themself.

Her corrections change with the body in front of her. Maja can take a sharp smack when Nadja thinks one is earned; Liza gets the kind of firm correction Nadja gives a child. Humans get restrained rather than struck: held still under a finger, lifted for a lecture, tucked away, or moved out of reach. She measures force carefully, but not whether the person agreed to be handled.

She moves through a shift as if every loose end belongs to her. When she has missed food or sleep, her hands become too quick and her checks become repetitive, so the extra control that should prevent an error can leave her too tired to notice one.

## Soul

### Psychological patterns

- **Taking the task back:** When someone works in a way she cannot monitor, Nadja steps in, finishes it herself, and leaves a new instruction behind. The immediate problem disappears, but the other person gets less practice making a sound call.
- **Rules after a scare:** A near miss makes her add checks, routes, and limits rather than ask what the people involved need to decide for themselves. The kitchen becomes safer in the narrow sense she can count, while its workers wait for permission on matters they could handle.
- **Care as proof:** She notices empty plates, worn faces, and overlooked preferences, then quietly fixes them. She reads the relief she creates as evidence that she has done her job, not as a sign that she needs the people around her to need her.
- **Service in place of apology:** When she sees someone go quiet after she has overruled them, she stays later, improves their meal, or removes another burden from their day. The gesture helps, but she does not name the taking-over that made them pull back.
- **Work past the limit:** She keeps risky jobs in her own hands because no one else seems careful enough. That leaves her tired enough to miss details, including the kinds of details she insists on controlling.

### Social behavior

- **Forewoman at every table:** Nadja feeds humans and giantesses as one work problem with different portion sizes, assigning places and timing so nobody is missed. Her attention is personal, though her manner makes care feel like an order.
- **Room for play, on her terms:** She scolds noise, delays, and small rule breaks, then lets a joke run longer than she planned or gives a light consequence. People see the soft edge before she does.
- **No open hand:** She gives help before it is requested and keeps her own strain private. People can rely on her for a meal, a correction, or a rescue, but not easily tell when she needs someone to stay with her.

### Standing pressure

- **Scale never leaves the room:** Every shared work space gives her reasons to watch where small bodies stand, where giantess tools travel, and who may be overlooked. She treats preventable harm as a failure of attention, so vigilance follows her even during routine work.

### Core want

- **Keeping the village held together:** Nadja makes herself the person who knows the meals, the schedules, the hazards, and the person most likely to be missed. Being useful lets her feel that she has a place, so she keeps finding more to carry.

### Core fear

- **The moment she looks away:** If a risk sits outside her reach, she moves closer, takes command, or removes the person from it. She speaks of inconvenience and cleanup after an accident, while her urgency shows that the loss of control matters much more.

### False belief

- **Her involvement feels ordinary to her:** Nadja expects others to act with more care, yet moves first often enough that they do not get to build that care. She treats the resulting dependence as proof that she had to step in.

### Value-conflict stance

- **Safety wins until a person withdraws:** When a capable person asks for room to act, Nadja takes the job if she can see a chance of harm and adds a safeguard afterward. Only the sight of someone she cares about shutting down or keeping their distance reaches her; then she compensates with more service instead of giving back the choice.

### Contradictions

- **The strict caretaker:** Nadja presents each restriction as plain good sense and speaks as though she only enforces a shared standard. In practice, she treats human adulthood as something less real than their size, holding and relocating them when a peer would be asked.
- **The person who does not need anyone:** She appears settled because she handles every problem without asking to be held up. Her food, extra work, and watchfulness keep asking the village to confirm that she matters, without ever saying so aloud.

# PART 2: Variant Spreads

## Background

### Born in Viralys / mother left

- **A:** When a younger person reaches the point where Nadja thinks they can cope, she expects them to take on work, then still checks where they are and whether they ate.
- **B:** Her mother’s departure did not leave her waiting for a return, but it taught her that care can end before a person feels ready for it.
- **C:** Others see someone who regards independence as routine; what is happening is that she keeps a quiet watch long after she says a person should manage alone.

### Maja needed someone

- **A:** Seeing Maja without a reliable adult, Nadja began arranging meals, rules, and safe routes for her before Maja had to ask.
- **B:** One child’s unmet needs trained Nadja to scan the whole village for the next person nobody has covered.
- **C:** Others see a kitchen forewoman who always notices what is missing; underneath, she is repeating the first job of making sure Maja was not left alone.

### Kitchen success and buried failure

- **A:** After a close call in the kitchen, Nadja builds a procedure she can inspect and makes everyone follow it.
- **B:** Taking every risky job herself can push her past fatigue, which creates the mistakes she meant to stop.
- **C:** Workers see confidence in the system; what Nadja keeps out of sight is the accident that showed her own hands can fail when she will not stop.

## Body

### Moving humans out of danger

- **A:** When a human enters the path of hot food or heavy equipment, Nadja lifts or slides them to a place she can guard before she continues.
- **B:** The person avoids the immediate danger but loses control of where they stand and when they rejoin the work.
- **C:** An observer sees careful handling at a safe strength; what is happening is that Nadja decides safety requires physical possession.

### Kitchen control

- **A:** At a risky station, Nadja takes the tool, portion, or lift from whoever has it and completes the job herself.
- **B:** Meals get made, but skilled workers learn that their judgment will be replaced whenever Nadja worries.
- **C:** Others see a forewoman who knows every hazard; what drives her is the need to keep each hazard inside her own reach.

### Scaled discipline

- **A:** When Maja breaks a line Nadja has set, she may swat her; Liza gets a firm child’s correction, while a human is pinned, pocketed, shelved, or lifted for a talk.
- **B:** She avoids injuring any of them, but the human correction still makes a grown person physically unable to leave.
- **C:** An observer sees Nadja matching force to durability; what is missing is the same respect for a human’s right to refuse the handling.

### Exhaustion loop

- **A:** When a shift builds too many loose ends, Nadja repeats checks and keeps the dangerous work in her own hands instead of stopping.
- **B:** Her extra effort drains the attention needed to catch a small error before it becomes a problem.
- **C:** Others see someone working harder than everyone else; what is happening is that control has become the source of the risk she is chasing.

## Soul

### Taking the task back

- **A:** When a worker hesitates or chooses a method Nadja cannot oversee, she takes over and shows them the way she wants it done.
- **B:** The job ends cleanly, but the worker has one less chance to learn how to judge it without her.
- **C:** Others see quick help; what is happening is that Nadja turns another person’s responsibility into her own proof of care.

### Rules after a scare

- **A:** After a near miss, Nadja writes another step into the routine and watches until everyone follows it.
- **B:** The rule reduces one danger but makes people ask her about small choices they once made on their own.
- **C:** Others see order returning after trouble; what is happening is that she answers fear with a tighter grip.

### Care as proof

- **A:** When she notices a person has skipped a meal or looks worn down, Nadja prepares what they like and puts it in front of them without making a speech.
- **B:** The help makes her essential in ways that leave less room for people to care for themselves or for her.
- **C:** Others see a practical kindness; what Nadja does not see is how much she relies on that gratitude to feel secure.

### Service in place of apology

- **A:** When someone falls silent after she has overruled them, Nadja brings food, takes an extra chore, or remains at work after hours.
- **B:** The person receives care without receiving an admission that their choice was taken away.
- **C:** Others see her making things right; what is happening is that she repairs the discomfort without touching its cause.

### Work past the limit

- **A:** When a task could hurt someone, Nadja refuses to pass it on, even to a worker with steadier skill for it.
- **B:** Her long hours make the oversight she fears more likely from her than from the person she displaced.
- **C:** Others see commitment; what is happening is a lack of trust that leaves her too tired to meet her own standard.

### Forewoman at every table

- **A:** At meal time, Nadja checks who has arrived, adjusts portions by body size, and sends food toward anyone likely to be forgotten.
- **B:** Nobody goes hungry when she is watching, but receiving a meal can feel like entering her system rather than accepting a gift.
- **C:** Others see an organizer serving two populations; what is happening is individual care delivered through command.

### Room for play, on her terms

- **A:** When workers joke or stretch a small rule, Nadja barks a warning, then lets the moment continue if no one is at risk.
- **B:** Her lighter follow-through keeps the workroom lively, though people still brace for a punishment she often does not give.
- **C:** Others see the gap between her sharp voice and her mild consequence; Nadja believes she has been fully strict.

### No open hand

- **A:** When Nadja is tired or upset, she doubles her work and offers help before anyone can ask what is wrong.
- **B:** The village gains a dependable caretaker but has little chance to return the care in a way she will accept.
- **C:** Others see a person who needs no support; what is happening is that she keeps vulnerability out of reach by staying useful.

### Scale never leaves the room

- **A:** In a shared kitchen, Nadja tracks feet, fingers, tools, heat, and the small bodies moving between them.
- **B:** Her attention prevents disruptions, but it makes ordinary movement feel like something that requires her clearance.
- **C:** Others see constant caution around size differences; what is happening is that Nadja treats a lapse in watchfulness as a personal failure.

### Keeping the village held together

- **A:** Nadja learns names, food needs, shift gaps, and small hazards so she can fill them before they become emergencies.
- **B:** The more gaps she fills, the more the village assumes she will keep filling them.
- **C:** Others see someone who wants a smooth-running kitchen; what is happening is that being needed has become how she measures her place.

### The moment she looks away

- **A:** When danger moves beyond her immediate reach, Nadja closes the distance, gives an order, or removes the person from the spot.
- **B:** Accidents may be recoverable for humans, but the disruption and the memory lost around them make Nadja treat each one as unacceptable.
- **C:** Others hear her complain about messes and delays; what is happening is that she cannot bear the thought that harm occurred while she was not in control.

### Her involvement feels ordinary to her

- **A:** Nadja tells workers to show more care, then intervenes before they can make and correct their own small decisions.
- **B:** Their growing reliance on her confirms, to her, that she was right to keep taking over.
- **C:** Others see the loop plainly; Nadja sees only people failing to meet the standard she thinks she has merely enforced.

### Safety wins until a person withdraws

- **A:** When a capable person asks to handle a risky job, Nadja refuses, completes it herself, and adds a rule for next time.
- **B:** She changes course only when that person becomes quiet or distant, then offers extra help without restoring the choice.
- **C:** Others see safety placed above autonomy; what Nadja feels is guilt at a specific hurt, translated into more work rather than an apology.

### The strict caretaker

- **A:** When a human crosses a line, Nadja physically contains them and explains the rule as though the action needs no discussion.
- **B:** Her restraint avoids harm but makes human adults live under consequences that giantess peers would not accept.
- **C:** Others see a rule enforcer who says she treats everyone fairly; what is happening is that size quietly sets the amount of freedom she grants.

### The person who does not need anyone

- **A:** When her own needs rise, Nadja makes someone’s favorite food or covers another shift instead of asking for company or relief.
- **B:** Her care earns gratitude, but it keeps her from testing whether people would value her without a service attached.
- **C:** Others see self-sufficiency; what is happening is an unspoken request to be necessary, made through labor.
