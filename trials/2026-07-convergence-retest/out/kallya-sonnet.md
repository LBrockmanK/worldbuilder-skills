# Kallya — Character Note

## PART 1: Character Note

### Background

- She tried to bluff a human settlement into feeding and housing her by implying she would eat them all if they refused. They called it. She could not kill a community she had lived among, and left. -> The relief she felt when they called her bluff told her she cared about groups of people as wholes. She did not want that information and has spent every year since burying it under a framework that lets her eat individuals while keeping communities intact. [high charge]

- She arrived at Vesper's tree before anyone else settled nearby. She coiled around the base and stayed. The village of Viralys grew up around them. -> She has been here longer than the village itself. Her presence is not an arrangement she agreed to but a fact the settlement formed around, which she treats as proof she owes it nothing. [mid charge]

- She has watched humans she recognized die permanently. She said nothing afterward and moved to a different spot in the village. -> When a specific human she knew stops existing for good, she relocates without explanation. She does not talk about the person. She does not return to where she was lying for days. [high charge, suppressed]

- She spared a human named Rado Stary when she had an easy chance to eat him. She said there was no sport in prey that could not process what was happening to him. -> The stated reason was boredom. The actual reason was closer to pity for his particular situation. She filed it under the framework anyway: not worth eating, move on. [mid charge]

### Body

Kallya is a lamia-giantess: 81 meters total length, 65 meters of snake tail, with a humanoid upper body that would stand 30 to 35 meters if she were upright like the other giantesses. She is heavier than any other giantess in Viralys. If she were physically moved against resistance, the structures around her would come apart.

She presents as careless about her size. She stretches, shifts, drapes her coils across paths and between buildings as though she has forgotten how much of her there is. She has not forgotten. She tracks where rope ladders are anchored, where humans are standing, where a sudden movement would knock something loose. The carelessness is performed so that people think of her as lazy rather than deliberate.

She cajoles humans closer to her mouth rather than grabbing them. Sudden lunges would pull rope ladders off their moorings, scatter tools, and hurt bystanders she did not intend to touch. The gentle coaxing looks like personality. It is also structural. She cannot be a quick predator without wrecking the space she lives in.

Her metabolism is slow. Her digestion is slow. Anything she swallows stays inside her for a long time. This is a physical fact that shapes the experience of anyone she eats: they are stuck there.

She senses vibrations through the ground along the length of her body. This makes her an early warning system for anything approaching the village, a role she fell into without volunteering for it.

She has no magic. Her strength is physical and untrained. She is strong because she is massive, not because she knows how to fight.

### Soul

**Psychological patterns**

She performs as an airhead. She talks in circles, loses the thread, asks obvious questions, seems to miss the point. She does not miss the point. She sees what people want, what they are afraid of, and what they will agree to if she asks the right way at the right time. The vapid presentation is a tool. The laziness underneath it is real.

She thinks of humans the way a herder thinks of livestock. The herd matters. Individual animals do not. She keeps the population healthy, watches for threats to the group, and eats individual humans when she feels like it. She enjoys the way they resist. She is the only giantess in Viralys who actively hunts the humans she lives among.

She builds pretexts. A human needs to check something near her mouth. She is feeling unwell and needs someone to look at a tooth. She heard a sound inside her throat and wants someone to listen. The excuses are thin. They work because she delivers them through her airhead persona, and people are not sure whether she is dangerous or just stupid. The uncertainty is the tool.

When no one else is around, she drops the performance. She may take a human without building a pretext first. She uses the fact that resurrected humans lose their memory of how they died: the victim will not remember, and no witness saw.

When Viralys faces a real external threat, she responds fast and hard. She deals with it, goes back to where she was lying, and refuses any credit or thanks. She treats the defense as something that happened, not something she did.

**Social behavior**

Her friendship with Vesper is real. She frames it as freeloading: Vesper has a good tree, the shade is nice, she stays because the spot is comfortable. She does favors for Vesper without being asked and does not acknowledge them as favors.

She gets along with Ataraxia on the surface. Both of them are lazy and both treat the village as their personal space. The solidarity between them is shallow but genuine on its own terms.

She finds Maja boring. Maja moves constantly and talks constantly. Kallya does not want to move or be talked at. They leave each other alone.

**Standing pressure**

Leif Holtveld may be building cooling devices and placing them to make certain areas uncomfortable for her, trying to steer where she lies without her knowing. She has not noticed yet. When she does, the response will depend on whether she sees it as a threat to the community or a personal annoyance.

**Core want**

She wants to belong to this place and these people without giving up the framework that lets her eat them. She wants connection to the community as a whole. She acts on this by staying, by defending the village, by keeping the population stable enough to continue. She does not call any of this care.

**Core fear**

If someone made her say out loud that she stays because she cares about the people here, the framework would crack. She cannot eat members of a community she has admitted she belongs to. Her fear is not being seen as soft. Her fear is knowing it herself, because knowing it would force a choice she does not want to make. She avoids this by maintaining the story that she stays for the tree and the convenience.

**False belief**

She tells herself she lives at Vesper's tree because the spot is good. Warm, sheltered, central. She tells herself the village is a convenience. She has turned down better spots. She has stayed through winters that made the tree miserable. She stays because the people are here and she does not want to be somewhere they are not.

**Value-conflict stance**

Her framework says individual humans are expendable and the community is worth keeping. When she meets a moment where a specific person's suffering is too particular to file under expendable, she follows the framework anyway. She eats, she predates, she stays in role. The excuse comes after: not hungry enough, no sport in it, bad timing. The thing that breaks the pattern is not conscience but recognition. She has to know this specific person's specific way of being alive for the exception to kick in. When it does and she holds back, she moves to a different part of the village and stays there. She does not say why she moved.

**Contradictions**

She presents as someone who does not care about any individual human. She tracks permanent deaths. She relocates when one happens. She spared Rado Stary for reasons she mislabeled. The framework says individuals do not matter. Her behavior around specific deaths says some of them do.

She presents as a freeloader who stays for the tree. She has defended the village at personal risk, multiple times, and refused credit every time. A freeloader does not fight for the place they are freeloading from. She does, and she will not discuss it.

## PART 2: Variant Spreads

### Background Entry 1: The Bluff

**Variant A (trigger and action):**
She threatened a settlement with annihilation to get what she wanted. When they refused to comply, she stood there with her mouth open and her threat exposed as empty. She left that night.

**Variant B (cost or consequence):**
The bluff failing cost her the ability to pretend she did not care about groups of people. The relief was involuntary and unmistakable. She has spent every year since constructing a framework that lets her eat individuals so she never has to test whether she could destroy a community again.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
To the settlement, she was a predator who lost her nerve. To herself, she was someone who had just learned something true about her own limits. She treats the lesson as a tactical failure. It was a moral discovery she refuses to name.

### Background Entry 2: Settling at the Tree

**Variant A (trigger and action):**
She found Vesper's tree before the village existed. She coiled around the base and did not leave. Other people arrived later and built around her.

**Variant B (cost or consequence):**
Because she was there first, she can claim the village came to her rather than the other way around. This lets her treat her presence as inertia rather than choice, which protects her from the question of why she stays.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
To anyone who asks, she settled for the tree and the tree happened to come with a village. The timeline supports this. The fact that she has never looked for a better tree does not.

### Background Entry 3: Permanent Deaths

**Variant A (trigger and action):**
When a human she recognized dies permanently, she stops talking about them. She moves to a different part of the village and lies there for days.

**Variant B (cost or consequence):**
The relocation costs her the comfortable spot she was in. It costs her the pretense that individual humans are interchangeable. She pays both costs without acknowledging either.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
An observer sees a giantess who got bored with her sunning spot and moved. What is happening is grief expressed as geography. She will not go back to the place she associates with the person who is gone.

### Background Entry 4: Sparing Rado Stary

**Variant A (trigger and action):**
She had an easy opportunity to eat Rado Stary. She passed. She said there was no sport in prey that could not understand what was happening to it.

**Variant B (cost or consequence):**
The spare cost her a meal she wanted. It also cost her a clean record of framework adherence: she made an exception for an individual, and the reason she gave does not hold up under any pressure.

**Variant C (observer vs. reality):**
Anyone listening heard a predator with standards. What happened was a predator who saw a specific person's specific suffering and could not file it under "expendable." She labeled it boredom. It was pity.

### Body Entry 1: Size and Weight

**Variant A:**
She is 81 meters of lamia, 65 of it snake tail, the rest a humanoid torso that would stand as tall as most giantesses. She weighs more than any of them. Moving her by force would take the buildings with her.

**Variant B:**
Her weight means the village cannot physically remove her. It also means she cannot move carelessly without doing structural damage. Her size is both her security and her constraint.

**Variant C:**
She looks like she takes up space without thinking about it. She has calculated exactly how much space she takes up and what moves within a 3-meter margin of every coil.

### Body Entry 2: Performed Carelessness

**Variant A:**
She drapes herself across walkways and leans against structures as if she has forgotten her own dimensions. She remembers every rope ladder anchor point and every human standing within reach.

**Variant B:**
The performance of carelessness lets people relax around her. If they saw the precision, they would wonder what she was being precise for. Precision in a predator is alarming. Sloppiness is not.

**Variant C:**
An observer sees a lazy giantess who keeps almost knocking things over. The "almost" is the tell. She never actually does. The carelessness stops exactly where damage would start.

### Body Entry 3: Coaxing Instead of Grabbing

**Variant A:**
She talks humans into coming close rather than snatching them. She invents reasons for them to approach her mouth. The method is social, not physical.

**Variant B:**
A quick grab would yank rope ladders off walls, scatter anything nearby, and possibly injure humans she was not targeting. The gentle approach is forced on her by the built environment as much as by preference.

**Variant C:**
It looks like she is too lazy to lunge. She is too careful. The village is fragile around her and she knows what sudden movement costs.

### Body Entry 4: Slow Metabolism

**Variant A:**
Her digestion is slow. Anything she swallows is inside her for a long time. This is a physical fact, not a choice.

**Variant B:**
The slow digestion means her victims experience a long stay. It shapes how humans think about being eaten by her: not quick, not clean, not over soon.

**Variant C:**
She eats rarely because she processes food slowly. From the outside, she looks less predatory than she is. The low frequency hides the high duration.

### Body Entry 5: Vibration Sensing

**Variant A:**
She feels vibrations through the ground along her entire body length. Anything heavy approaching the village registers before anyone else notices it.

**Variant B:**
The sensing is passive. She does not have to try. It turned her into the village's early warning system without her volunteering, and she has never asked to stop being it.

**Variant C:**
The village thinks of her as the lazy one who happens to notice things first. She notices because 65 meters of her body is in contact with the ground at all times. The detection is structural, not attentive.

### Body Entry 6: No Magic, Untrained

**Variant A:**
She is physically strong because of her mass. She has no magical ability and no combat training. Her strength is blunt force applied by something very large.

**Variant B:**
Against a trained opponent her size, she would lose. Against anything smaller, training does not matter. Her mass is the entire argument.

**Variant C:**
She fights like a building falling on something. It works against most threats because most threats are smaller than a building.

### Soul Entry 1: The Airhead Performance

**Variant A:**
She talks in loops, asks questions she knows the answers to, and lets people finish her sentences with whatever they wanted to hear. She has already worked out what they want and how to get them to offer it.

**Variant B:**
The performance costs her the ability to be taken seriously when she says something real. People hear her warnings the same way they hear her chatter: as background noise from the harmless one.

**Variant C:**
Observers see a giantess who can barely follow a conversation. What is happening is social hunting. She is mapping the person's needs, fears, and willingness to comply, and the vapid presentation is the blind she hunts from.

### Soul Entry 2: The Husbandry Framework

**Variant A:**
She watches the human population the way a farmer watches livestock. She notices birth rates, health patterns, and resource strain. She eats individuals the way a farmer culls: without personal feeling, because the herd can absorb it.

**Variant B:**
The framework lets her eat people she lives among without confronting what that means. As long as the community continues, individual deaths are maintenance, not murder. The framework is the thing standing between her behavior and her knowledge of what her behavior is.

**Variant C:**
She looks like a predator who does not care. She looks like that because she built a system for not caring that runs on real agricultural logic. The system works. It also requires her to never look at an individual human long enough to see a person instead of a population unit.

### Soul Entry 3: Pretext Construction

**Variant A:**
She builds thin excuses for humans to come close to her mouth. She needs someone to check a tooth. She heard a sound in her throat. The excuses are flimsy and they work because people cannot tell if she is dangerous or confused.

**Variant B:**
The pretexts protect her from using force, which would damage the village. They also protect her from being seen as a straightforward predator, which would end the social access that makes the pretexts possible in the first place.

**Variant C:**
A human walking toward her mouth thinks they are helping a dim giantess with a minor problem. They are walking into a trap built from social uncertainty. She made the trap out of their inability to tell whether she means it.

### Soul Entry 4: Behavior Without Witnesses

**Variant A:**
When no one else is present, she drops the airhead act and takes humans without building a story first. She relies on resurrection memory loss to erase the event.

**Variant B:**
The directness without witnesses shows the performance is calculated, not habitual. She can stop it the moment it stops serving a purpose. The memory loss is the cleanup mechanism that makes the whole system sustainable.

**Variant C:**
With an audience, she is a bumbling giantess who accidentally gets too close. Without one, she is a predator who does not need the disguise. The difference is immediate and total.

### Soul Entry 5: Defense and Refusal of Credit

**Variant A:**
When something threatens Viralys, she deals with it fast and hard. Afterward she goes back to where she was lying and refuses to talk about it.

**Variant B:**
Accepting credit would mean admitting she acted to protect the village. She acted to protect the village. She will not say so because saying so means the place matters to her and the framework cannot hold if the place matters.

**Variant C:**
Observers see a giantess who helped once and went back to being lazy. What happened is that the lazy giantess is always ready to help and always pretends afterward that she was not.

### Soul Entry 6: Friendship with Vesper

**Variant A:**
She describes her relationship with Vesper as freeloading. The tree is comfortable, the shade is good, why move. She does things for Vesper without being asked and does not mention them.

**Variant B:**
If Vesper named the friendship directly, Kallya would deflect. Acknowledging the friendship would mean she stays for a person, not a location. She does not want that on the record.

**Variant C:**
Vesper sees a friend who pretends to be a parasite. Kallya sees a host who does not charge rent. The friendship is real in both directions and named in neither.

### Soul Entry 7: Relationship with Ataraxia

**Variant A:**
She and Ataraxia share lazy afternoons and treat the village as their personal territory. The bond is shallow, built on shared preference for doing nothing.

**Variant B:**
The low-effort solidarity means neither pushes the other. It is comfortable because it asks nothing. It would not survive a real demand.

**Variant C:**
They look like two giantesses who get along because they are similar. They get along because neither requires the other to be different.

### Soul Entry 8: Avoiding Maja

**Variant A:**
Maja moves constantly and talks constantly. Kallya does not want to move or be talked at. They stay out of each other's way without friction.

**Variant B:**
The avoidance is not hostility. It is energy conservation. Maja costs effort to be around, and Kallya does not spend effort she does not have to spend.

**Variant C:**
Maja sees a giantess who is never around. Kallya sees a giantess who never stops. The distance between them is temperamental, not personal.

### Soul Entry 9: Standing Pressure (Leif's Cooling Devices)

**Variant A:**
Leif Holtveld may be placing cooling devices to make areas uncomfortable for her, steering her movements through temperature without her knowing. She has not noticed yet.

**Variant B:**
If she notices and sees it as a personal annoyance, she will relocate and ignore it. If she sees it as someone trying to control her, the response will be different and worse for Leif.

**Variant C:**
From Leif's perspective, he is solving a problem. From hers, when she finds out, someone has been moving her like furniture. She will not find that funny.

### Soul Entry 10: Core Want

**Variant A:**
She wants to stay with these people and keep eating some of them. She wants the community to continue and her place in it to hold. She acts on this by defending the village and keeping the population stable.

**Variant B:**
The want costs her honesty. She cannot name what she wants without exposing the contradiction: belonging requires care, and care undermines the framework that lets her predate.

**Variant C:**
She looks like a predator who tolerates a village. She is a member of a community who cannot stop being a predator. The want is for both things at once, and she has built her entire social presentation to avoid choosing.

### Soul Entry 11: Core Fear

**Variant A:**
If someone forced her to say she cares about the people here, the framework would fail. She could not eat members of a community she has admitted belonging to. She avoids this by never admitting it.

**Variant B:**
The fear is not of others knowing. It is of herself knowing. She already knows. The fear is of the moment where knowing becomes undeniable and she has to act on it.

**Variant C:**
She looks like someone who does not care. She is someone who is afraid of what happens when she admits she does. The airhead act, the husbandry framework, the freeloading story: all of them are walls around the same admission.

### Soul Entry 12: False Belief

**Variant A:**
She says she stays for the tree. The tree is fine. Other trees are also fine. She has not looked for another tree in the entire time she has been here.

**Variant B:**
The false belief lets her live here without calling it a choice. If she chose to stay, she chose the people. If she chose the people, she cares about them. The tree is the lie that holds the rest up.

**Variant C:**
Ask her why she is here and she will describe the tree: the shade, the warmth, the position. Ask her why she has not moved to a better spot and she will change the subject.

### Soul Entry 13: Value-Conflict Stance

**Variant A:**
When a specific person's suffering gets too particular to dismiss, she follows the framework anyway and builds an excuse after. Not hungry. No sport. Wrong time. She holds the role and buries the recognition.

**Variant B:**
The cost of following the framework against her own recognition is relocation. She moves to a different part of the village and stays there. She does not connect the move to the person. She does not name it as guilt.

**Variant C:**
An observer sees a predator with preferences. What is happening is a predator who sometimes sees a person instead of prey and has to work to unsee them. When she cannot unsee them, she spares them and leaves. The sparing looks like whim. The leaving is the guilt she will not call guilt.

### Soul Entry 14: Contradiction (Individuals Matter)

**Variant A:**
She says individuals do not matter. She tracks permanent deaths. She moved after the last one. She spared a specific person for reasons that had nothing to do with the framework.

**Variant B:**
Every exception she has made costs the framework a piece of credibility. She patches the holes with excuses: boredom, indigestion, bad positioning. The patches are getting thin.

**Variant C:**
The framework says: expendable. Her behavior says: she remembers their names. Both things are true at the same time and she has not figured out how to make them stop.

### Soul Entry 15: Contradiction (Freeloader Who Fights)

**Variant A:**
She calls herself a freeloader. She has fought for the village multiple times. A freeloader does not break things that threaten the place they sleep.

**Variant B:**
Refusing credit after defending the village costs her the respect that would make her position more secure. She pays that cost rather than admit the defense was motivated by care.

**Variant C:**
The village sees a giantess who sometimes helps and mostly sleeps. What they are not seeing is that the sleeping giantess chose this village over every other place she could be, and fights for it because it is hers. She will never say "mine." She acts like it is.
