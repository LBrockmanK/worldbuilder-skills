# Generation Packet — Nadja

Generate a complete character note for Nadja from the Design Notes below.
Follow all generation rules exactly. Output the synthesized character note
AND an appendix of variant spreads.

## World Context

Viralys: a village where giantesses (30-80m tall) and humans coexist.
Humans can resurrect after death (losing memory of what killed them);
some deaths are permanent (old age, certain conditions). Giantesses vary
in how they relate to humans.

## Preprocessed Design Notes

### Session Notes

- Maja's mother abandoned her young (or at least that is presumed — no one ever saw her); Nadja started looking out for Maja and took a big-sister role, which developed into caretaking more broadly
- The caretaking instinct started with Maja specifically — a kid who needed someone — and grew outward as the village's practical needs gave structure; she did not set out to become the person who feeds everyone; it accumulated

- Core fear: if she stops controlling the outcome, someone gets hurt; not just professional diligence but personal — she is between fragile humans and giantess-scale hazards every day; the warmth she will not acknowledge is what makes the stakes feel so high
- [FLAGGED: ai_vocabulary] Self-image gap: sees what she does as normal, thinks others should be more responsible — while her overbearing nature prevents them from developing that responsibility; creating the problem she is solving
- Unconsciously infantilizes humans; not malicious — just how things are to her; humans are small, fragile, need looking after; she likes them, feeds them, cares individually — but does not think of them as fully autonomous

- Refuses to delegate dangerous tasks even when someone else would do them better; result is she is overworked and occasionally makes the exact mistakes she is trying to prevent; root is the control pattern — cannot trust anyone else to be careful enough
- Will not admit she enjoys people having fun at work; bark worse than bite; does not notice she softens around people enjoying themselves or that her punishments for minor infractions are lighter than she thinks
- Self-reliant; no one she is personally vulnerable with; always the one holding it together
- Warmth is acts of service she does not draw attention to: preparing someone's favorite dish, making sure they remember to eat
- Physical discipline scales with target: Maja gets hit (durable peer); Liza gets standard child discipline; humans get contained — pinned under a finger, shoved in a pocket, put on a shelf, held up for a talking-down; constricting but not harmful
- Protectiveness operates within resurrection framework: strict about safety because accidents are disruptive and unpleasant, not because she treats human life as sacred

### Structured Doctrine

- **Core want:** To keep everyone safe; underneath that, to be needed — the caretaking is how she knows she matters
- **Core fear:** If she stops controlling the outcome, someone gets hurt; the warmth she will not acknowledge is what makes the stakes personal
- **False belief:** Her level of involvement is normal; others should step up more — while her constant stepping in is what stops them from developing that capacity
- **Contrast declaration:** Against Maja — both care deeply about others, but Maja is chaos and energy while Nadja is control and structure; Maja pulls people in, Nadja holds them still
- **Value-conflict stance:** When her operating code — she is responsible, no one else is careful enough — meets a situation where respecting someone's competence or autonomy is the right call, she follows the code: takes over, does it herself, adds a rule. The lever that tips her is seeing the specific cost on a specific person — not the abstract idea of autonomy but watching someone she cares about shut down or pull away because she would not let them try. Guilt shows as service: she works harder, makes their food, stays later. She does not connect the two.
- **Charge-scored memories:** Maja as a child needing someone (high); first kitchen accident she prevented — proving the system works (mid); first accident she caused by overwork (high, suppressed)

## Section Structure

**Background** — Where the character comes from; factual causal context.
Format: bullet list of [formative fact] -> [consequence] pairs with charge tags.

**Body** — Physical behavioral descriptions showing how physicality shapes behavior.
Format: prose sentences with When/Behavior/Because embedded naturally.

**Soul** — Psychological and social behavioral descriptions. Minimums:
- 3-5 psychological pattern entries
- 2-3 social behavior entries
- 1 standing pressure entry
- 1 core want entry (behavioral)
- 1 core fear entry (behavioral)
- 1 false belief entry
- 1 value-conflict stance entry
- 2 contradiction entries (presented self vs actual; unresolved tension)

## Generation Rules

**Fact-to-manifestation:** Design Notes state what is true. You write how that
truth manifests as observable behavior. Same meaning, different words,
observable action. Never reproduce input phrasing.

**Multi-option spread:** For each entry, generate 3 variant renderings:
- Variant A: trigger and what the character does
- Variant B: cost or consequence of the behavior
- Variant C: what an observer sees vs. what is actually happening
All three express the same character fact with genuine divergence.

**Selection (synthesis):** After generating 3 variants per entry, write a
combined version taking the strongest elements from all three. Do not
simply pick one — synthesize.

**Writing style:** Plain language. No literary flair, no Latinate vocabulary.
No heavy trait adjectives — replace each with the behavior that earned it.
Where an expertise is stated, also state where it stops. Details that do
not change behavior are left unwritten. No negative-led characterization.
No forward references. Starting state only.

## Output Format

Write TWO sections:

### PART 1: Character Note
The synthesized character note with sections: Background, Body, Soul.
(Omit Relationships — the retest focuses on convergence in behavioral
description, not relationship dynamics.)

### PART 2: Variant Spreads
For each entry in Part 1, list all three variants (A, B, C) that fed the
synthesis. Label each with the entry name and variant letter.
